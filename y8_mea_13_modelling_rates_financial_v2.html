1Why this matters

Real money decisions that require rate modelling every day.

Imagine two situations:

  • You save up $800 AUD and fly to Japan for school holidays. How much yen do you actually have to spend at a convenience store?
  • Your part-time job pays $22 000 per year. How much tax do you actually owe — and will you be surprised by the ATO bill?

Both questions involve rates applied to money. Neither can be answered by guessing — you need a mathematical model: a clear formula, applied step by step, then checked for reasonableness.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. You will use these terms throughout the lesson.

Exchange rate
Tap to flip
A rate that tells you how many units of one currency you receive for each unit of another.
1 AUD = 96.80 JPY — every Australian dollar buys 96.80 Japanese yen.
Tax bracket
Tap to flip
A range of income with its own tax rate. You pay that rate only on the portion of income inside that band — not on your whole income.
The $18 201–$45 000 band is taxed at 19c per dollar over $18 200.
Marginal rate
Tap to flip
The tax rate that applies to the next dollar earned — the rate of the highest bracket your income reaches.
On $50 000, the marginal rate is 32.5% (Band 3: $45 001–$120 000).
Effective rate
Tap to flip
Total tax paid ÷ total income — the average proportion of income paid as tax, always lower than the marginal rate.
Earn $58 000, pay $9 317 tax → effective rate = 16.1%.
Mathematical model
Tap to flip
A rule or formula that represents a real-world situation. Always finish by reviewing: "Does the answer make sense in context?"
Model: Foreign currency = AUD × exchange rate.

5Worked example A — Currency conversion & trip budget

Mia has $800 AUD saved. She is travelling to Japan for 5 days. Use the exchange rate table below to find how many yen she has, then check whether her budget is reasonable.

Singapore DollarSGD0.862 S$
1
Formulate — state the question and given information
Identify what is known. Write the question as a mathematical one. Choose the model.
Given: Mia has $800 AUD. From Figure 1: 1 AUD = 96.80 JPY.
Question: How many yen will Mia receive?
Model chosen: JPY received = AUD amount × exchange rate
2
Solve — substitute values into the model
Replace the variables with the numbers from the problem.
JPY = 800 × 96.80
JPY = 77 440
3
Interpret & Review — real money, real context
State the answer in context. Review whether the model is appropriate and whether the answer is reasonable.
Interpretation: Mia will receive ¥77 440.

Reasonableness check: Budget hotel in Japan ≈ ¥5 000–¥8 000/night × 5 nights = ¥25 000–¥40 000. Meals ≈ ¥600–¥1 200 each. ¥77 440 is a tight but workable 5-day budget — the answer passes the sense check.

Review of model: The model assumes the exchange rate is fixed and ignores bank or conversion fees (typically 1–2%). For an estimate the model is appropriate; for an exact figure, fees should be included.
Answer $800 AUD → ¥77 440 JPY  ·  Reasonable for a careful 5-day budget trip.

5bWorked example B — Income tax using marginal brackets

Jordan earns $58 000 per year. Calculate the income tax owed using the simplified tax table below, then find the effective tax rate and compare it to the marginal rate.

Figure 2 — Simplified Australian Income Tax Brackets illustrative brackets for teaching purposes
Taxable income bandTax on this portion
$0 – $18 200Nil
$18 201 – $45 00019c for each $1 over $18 200
$45 001 – $120 00032.5c for each $1 over $45 000
$120 001 – $180 000$29 467 + 37c for each $1 over $120 000
Over $180 000$51 667 + 45c for each $1 over $180 000
The highlighted row is Jordan's top bracket. Each band is taxed separately at its own rate, then the results are added together.
1
Formulate — split the income across the brackets
$58 000 spans three rows of the tax table. Identify the portion of income that falls in each band.
Band 1: $0 – $18 200 → portion = $18 200 → tax rate: nil
Band 2: $18 201 – $45 000 → portion = $26 800 → tax rate: 19c/$
Band 3: $45 001 – $58 000 → portion = $13 000 → tax rate: 32.5c/$
Check: 18 200 + 26 800 + 13 000 = 58 000 ✓
2
Solve — calculate tax on each band, then total
Apply each bracket's rate to only the portion of income within that band.
Band 1 tax: $0
Band 2 tax: $26 800 × 0.19 = $5 092
Band 3 tax: $13 000 × 0.325 = $4 225

Total tax = $0 + $5 092 + $4 225 = $9 317
3
Interpret & Review — effective rate vs marginal rate
State the answer in dollar terms. Compare the effective tax rate to the top marginal rate and explain why they differ.
Interpretation: Jordan owes $9 317 in tax on $58 000 earned.
Take-home pay = $58 000 − $9 317 = $48 683 per year.

Effective rate = total tax ÷ total income = $9 317 ÷ $58 000 ≈ 16.1%
Top marginal rate = 32.5% (Band 3).

Review: The effective rate (16.1%) is much lower than the marginal rate (32.5%) because only $13 000 — not all $58 000 — was taxed at 32.5%. The first $18 200 attracted no tax; the next $26 800 was taxed at only 19%. The model is appropriate; the marginal-bracket structure prevents any sudden jump in total tax at each threshold.
Answer Tax owed = $9 317  ·  Take-home = $48 683  ·  Effective rate ≈ 16.1% vs marginal 32.5%

Alex earns $72 000. A student calculated the tax as shown below. One line contains a critical error. Work through the sub-questions as a class.

Student's working — identify the fault
Line 1: Alex earns $72 000. The top bracket this falls into is $45 001–$120 000. ✓
Line 2: Tax rate in that bracket = 32.5%. ✓
Line 3 (FAULTY): Tax = $72 000 × 0.325 = $23 400   ← error here
Line 4: Take-home = $72 000 − $23 400 = $48 600.
Named misconception
Applying one bracket's rate to the entire income rather than to the marginal portion only (the income above the bracket's lower threshold). This ignores the tax-free threshold and the lower-rate bands, dramatically overstating the tax owed.

Work through these sub-questions as a class:

Correct working for reference:
Band 1 ($0–$18 200): $0 tax
Band 2 ($18 201–$45 000): $26 800 × 0.19 = $5 092
Band 3 ($45 001–$72 000): $27 000 × 0.325 = $8 775
Total tax = $0 + $5 092 + $8 775 = $13 867
Effective rate = $13 867 ÷ $72 000 ≈ 19.3%
