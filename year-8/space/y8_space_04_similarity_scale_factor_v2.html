1Warm-up — same shape, different size?

Before we talk about similar shapes, look at these two photos of the same school logo.

LOGO
6 cm × 4 cm
LOGO
12 cm × 8 cm

The bigger photo is twice as wide and twice as tall as the smaller one. The logo still looks right — it hasn't been stretched or squashed. We call shapes that have the same shape but different size similar.

If we had two photos that were exactly the same size as well as the same shape, we'd call them congruent — they'd be identical twins.

Today's two big ideas:
  • Congruent = same shape and same size.
  • Similar = same shape, but possibly different size. One is a scaled version of the other.
  • The number you multiply by to get from the small one to the big one is the scale factor. In the logos above, it's 2.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. You'll use these five words constantly today.

Congruent
Tap to flip
Two shapes are congruent if they have the same shape AND same size. Identical twins.
A square with side 5 cm and another square with side 5 cm are congruent.
Similar
Tap to flip
Two shapes are similar if they have the same shape, but one is a scaled version of the other.
A square with side 5 cm and a square with side 15 cm are similar.
Corresponding sides
Tap to flip
Sides that are in the same position on two similar shapes. They are linked as a pair.
The shortest side of a small triangle and the shortest side of its enlargement are corresponding sides.
Scale factor
Tap to flip
The number you multiply by to get from a side of the small shape to the matching side of the big one.
If 3 cm becomes 12 cm, the scale factor is 12 ÷ 3 = 4.
Enlargement
Tap to flip
A transformation that produces a similar shape using a scale factor. The new shape has the same angles as the original.
Photocopying at 200% is an enlargement with scale factor 2.

5Worked example — finding a missing side

Triangle ABC is similar to triangle DEF. Use the information below to find the length of EF.

Triangle ABC
→
Triangle DEF
1
Identify corresponding sides
AB matches with DE (both are the bottom sides). BC matches with EF (both are the vertical sides).
AB ↔ DE  and  BC ↔ EF
2
Find the scale factor
Use the pair you know: divide the new length by the original.
Scale factor = newold = DEAB = 186 = 3
3
Apply the scale factor to find the missing side
Multiply the original side (BC) by the scale factor.
EF = BC × scale factor = 8 × 3 = 24 cm
Answer EF = 24 cm
