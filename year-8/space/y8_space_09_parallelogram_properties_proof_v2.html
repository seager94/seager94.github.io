Today is different

From observing to proving.

For most of Year 8 geometry, we have measured — pulled out a ruler, compared sides, eyeballed angles. Today we stop measuring. A parallelogram has properties that we can prove are true for every parallelogram, not just the one drawn on the page. The tool we use is congruent triangles. By the end of the lesson, you'll have built the first proper proof of your school career.

1Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. These five words are the language of proof — you'll use them constantly today.

Parallelogram
Tap to flip
A quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel sides. That's the definition — everything else (equal sides, equal angles, bisecting diagonals) we have to prove.
If AB ∥ DC and BC ∥ AD, then ABCD is a parallelogram.
Congruent triangles
Tap to flip
Two triangles are congruent if all three sides and all three angles match. We use the tests SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS to prove congruence without checking everything.
Symbol: △ABC ≅ △DEF (order matters — match vertex by vertex).
Diagonal
Tap to flip
A line segment joining two non-adjacent vertices of a polygon. In proofs of parallelogram properties, drawing a diagonal is almost always the first move — it splits the shape into two triangles.
A parallelogram has two diagonals: AC and BD.
Alternate angles
Tap to flip
When a transversal crosses two parallel lines, the "Z" angles on opposite sides of the transversal are equal. This is your engine for finding equal angles inside a parallelogram.
If AB ∥ DC with transversal AC, then ∠BAC = ∠DCA.
CPCTC
Tap to flip
"Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent." Once you've proven two triangles congruent, you can claim that any matching pair of sides or angles are equal.
If △ABC ≅ △CDA, then AB = CD (matching sides), ∠B = ∠D (matching angles), and so on.

💡 Notice: a statement looks like a geometric equation (sides equal, angles equal, triangles congruent). A reason names a rule or fact (alternate angles, CPCTC, vertically opposite). Every line of a proof needs both.

4The first proof — opposite sides equal

We want to prove: if ABCD is a parallelogram, then AB = DC and BC = AD. We don't have a ruler. We have something better — congruent triangles.

Parallelogram ABCD with diagonal AC drawn. Arrows mark the parallel sides.
Convention for typing answers below: use short forms — alternate for alternate angles, common for shared side, vertical for vertically opposite, CPCTC for corresponding parts. Congruence tests: SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS.
💡 What you just did: you wrote a proof. You started from the definition of a parallelogram (parallel sides), used a congruence test (ASA) to lock the two triangles together, then used CPCTC to extract the equal sides. That is the entire engine of geometric proof in Year 8. Every parallelogram property is proved this way.

5Worked example — diagonals bisect each other

Theorem. In a parallelogram, the diagonals bisect each other. That is, in parallelogram ABCD with diagonals meeting at E: AE = EC and BE = ED.

Parallelogram ABCD with both diagonals AC and BD drawn. They cross at E.
1
Choose the two triangles to work with
We want to show that E is the midpoint of both diagonals. The two diagonals create four small triangles inside ABCD. Pick the two that "face" each other: △ABE and △CDE. If we can prove these two are congruent, then their matching sides AE/CE and BE/DE will be equal — exactly what we need.
We will prove: △ABE ≅ △CDE
and then use CPCTC to deduce AE = CE and BE = DE.
2
Find three matching parts (angle–side–angle)
We line up the three pieces we need. The side AB is sandwiched between the two angles ∠BAE and ∠ABE in △ABE; the side DC is sandwiched between ∠DCE and ∠CDE in △CDE. That's the ASA pattern.
StatementReason
∠BAE = ∠DCEAlternate angles (AB ∥ DC, transversal AC)
AB = DCOpposite sides of a parallelogram are equal (proven together in Section 4)
∠ABE = ∠CDEAlternate angles (AB ∥ DC, transversal BD)
Pattern: angle — side — angle. The side is between the two angles in each triangle. This is ASA.
3
Conclude congruence — then use CPCTC
With three matching parts in the ASA pattern, we can lock the triangles together. Once congruent, every matching pair is equal — so we extract the two pairs of sides we wanted.
StatementReason
△ABE ≅ △CDEASA (from the three lines above)
AE = CECPCTC (corresponding sides of congruent triangles)
BE = DECPCTC
∴ The diagonals bisect each other.E is the midpoint of both AC and BD. ∎
Theorem proved The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
