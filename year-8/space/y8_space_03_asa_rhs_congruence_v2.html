1Warm-up — where we are, where we're heading

A quick recap of what we already know, then a peek at today's two new tests.

We've already learnt to prove two triangles are congruent (exactly the same shape and size) using two tests:

SSS
Three pairs of sides equal. e.g. AB = DE, BC = EF, AC = DF ⟹ △ABC ≅ △DEF.
SAS
Two sides and the angle between them. e.g. AB = DE, ∠B = ∠E, BC = EF.
Today we add the last two tests of the four — ASA (two angles and the side between them) and RHS (a special test for right-angled triangles). We'll also meet one combination that looks like a test but actually fails: SSA. By the end you'll be able to name the right test for any given pair, and explain why SSA cannot prove congruence.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. These five words come up constantly today.

Congruent
Tap to flip
Two shapes are congruent when they have exactly the same shape and size — every side and every angle matches.
If △ABC ≅ △DEF, then AB = DE, BC = EF, AC = DF and the matching angles are equal.
Included side
Tap to flip
The side that sits between two named angles — it joins those two corners.
Between ∠A and ∠B, the included side is AB.
Hypotenuse
Tap to flip
In a right-angled triangle, the longest side — always opposite the right angle.
If ∠C = 90° in △ABC, the hypotenuse is AB.
ASA test
Tap to flip
Angle–Side–Angle: two pairs of angles and the side between them are equal.
∠A = ∠D, AB = DE, ∠B = ∠E ⟹ congruent.
RHS test
Tap to flip
Right angle–Hypotenuse–Side: both triangles right-angled, with equal hypotenuses and one other equal side.
∠C = ∠F = 90°, AB = DE (hypotenuses), BC = EF ⟹ congruent.

💡 The four valid tests are SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS. AAA only tells you the triangles are the same shape (similar) — they could be different sizes. SSA is the tricky one — it looks complete but isn't. We'll see why next.

4Why SSA does NOT prove congruence

Two sides and a non-included angle — meaning the angle isn't between the two given sides. Watch what happens when we try to build a triangle from that information.

Suppose we're told: AB = 15 cm, BC = 9 cm, and ∠A = 30°. Notice the angle ∠A is not between sides AB and BC — it sits at the far end. Let's try to draw the triangle.

The "swing" — the same SSA gives TWO different triangles

What's happening?

  • We fix the side AB = 15 cm and draw a ray from A at 30°.
  • The third vertex must be 9 cm from B, so we "swing" an arc of radius 9 from B.
  • That arc crosses the ray in two places — at C₁ and C₂.

Both triangles △ABC₁ and △ABC₂ have the same AB, the same ∠A, and the same BC — but they are clearly different triangles. One is acute, the other obtuse.

⚠️ SSA doesn't pin down a unique triangle — so it can't prove two triangles are congruent.
💡 Discuss with your neighbour: what if the angle had been bigger (say 80°)? Would there still be two possible triangles? (Hint: when the angle is large enough, the swing arc only crosses the ray once — but for general SSA we can't trust it.)

Given:

≅?
1
Identify what's matched
List the equal angles and the equal side. Use the colour-coding to track them.
∠A = ∠D (single arc — both 60°)
∠B = ∠E (double arc — both 50°)
AB = DE (single tick — both 8 cm)
2
Match the pattern to a test
Is the equal side between the two equal angles? Side AB joins vertex A to vertex B — yes, it sits between ∠A and ∠B. Two angles and the side between them ⟹ ASA.
Pattern: ∠  —  side  —  ∠   ⟹   ASA
3
Write the congruence statement
List the vertices in matching order: A↔D, B↔E, C↔F. State the reason (ASA) in brackets.
△ABC ≅ △DEF  (ASA)
Answer △ABC ≅ △DEF  (ASA)
