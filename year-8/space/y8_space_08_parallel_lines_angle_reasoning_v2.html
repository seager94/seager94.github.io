2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip. You need every one of these to talk and write about today's diagrams.

Parallel lines
Tap to flip
Two lines that never meet, no matter how far they're extended. Marked with matching arrows (➤) or chevrons.
Railway tracks and the lines on lined paper are parallel.
Transversal
Tap to flip
A line that cuts across two (or more) other lines. In our diagrams it's the red line.
The transversal creates eight angles — and they come in matching pairs.
Corresponding angles
Tap to flip
Same position at each intersection — the F-pattern. They are equal.
"x = 75° (corresponding angles, parallel lines)" — the wording you'll write as a reason.
Alternate angles
Tap to flip
Between the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal — the Z-pattern. They are equal.
"x = 110° (alternate angles, parallel lines)".
Co-interior angles
Tap to flip
Between the parallel lines, on the same side of the transversal — the C-pattern. They add to 180°.
"x + 130° = 180° (co-interior angles, parallel lines), so x = 50°."

5Worked example — find x with full reasoning

Two parallel lines cut by a transversal. We're told one angle is 110°. Find x.

1
Identify the relationship
Trace from the 110° angle to x — they sit on opposite sides of the transversal, between the parallel lines. That's a Z-pattern.
The 110° angle and x are alternate angles.
2
State the rule
Alternate angles on parallel lines are equal. (This is the line you'd write in a proof.)
Alternate angles on parallel lines are equal.
3
Apply the rule and write the reason
Equate and solve — then justify in one short sentence.
x = 110°   (alternate angles, parallel lines)
Answer with reason x = 110° (alternate angles, parallel lines)
