1Warm-up — making decisions in steps

Before we talk about shapes, think about how you make everyday decisions.

Suppose you're getting dressed in the morning. You probably go through a chain of small decisions:

  • IF it's raining → take an umbrella ☂️
  • IF it's cold → wear a jumper 🧥
  • ELSE → just normal clothes 👕

This kind of step-by-step decision rule is called an algorithm — a clear recipe for arriving at an answer.

Today's mission: build an algorithm that takes any pair of shapes and decides — are they CONGRUENT, SIMILAR, or NEITHER? You've met all three ideas over the past few weeks. Today we pull them together into one clear procedure you can use on any pair.

2Key vocabulary — refresh

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. These are the five words our algorithm depends on.

Congruent
Tap to flip
Same shape and same size. Like identical photocopies — every side and every angle matches.
A 3-4-5 cm triangle and another 3-4-5 cm triangle are congruent.
Similar
Tap to flip
Same shape, different size. Like a photo and its enlargement — angles match, sides are scaled.
A 3-4-5 cm triangle and a 6-8-10 cm triangle are similar.
Corresponding sides
Tap to flip
Sides in matching positions in two shapes.
The shortest side in one triangle corresponds to the shortest side in the other.
Corresponding angles
Tap to flip
Angles in matching positions in two shapes.
The largest angle in one shape corresponds to the largest angle in the other.
Scale factor
Tap to flip
The number you multiply by to go from one shape to the other. Ratio of corresponding sides.
From 3 cm to 9 cm, the scale factor is 3.

4The algorithm — three questions

Here's the procedure. Just three yes/no questions, and we'll have an answer for any pair of shapes. Study this carefully — you'll use it for the rest of the lesson.

START with two shapes
↓
Q1 Are all corresponding angles equal?
NO → NEITHER YES ↓ (continue)
↓
Q2 Are all corresponding side ratios equal?
NO → NEITHER YES ↓ (continue)
↓
Q3 Is the scale factor = 1?
YES ↓ CONGRUENT
NO ↓ SIMILAR
💡 Why this order? Q1 first because if the angles aren't equal, the shapes aren't even the same shape — no point checking sides. Q2 is the similarity test. Q3 only matters once we know they're similar — it just asks whether the "scaling" is by 1 (no change → congruent) or by something else (an actual enlargement → similar).

5Worked example — apply the algorithm

Classify this pair: △ABC with sides 6, 8, 10 cm and △DEF with sides 9, 12, 15 cm. (Both right-angled triangles.)

1
Q1 — Are corresponding angles equal?
Both are right-angled triangles. The 6-8-10 has angles 90°, 53°, 37°. The 9-12-15 has the same set of angles (any triangle with sides in the same ratio has the same angles).
△ABC angles: 90°, 53°, 37°
△DEF angles: 90°, 53°, 37° ✓
→ Q1 answer: YES. Continue to Q2.
2
Q2 — Are corresponding side ratios equal?
Match shortest-to-shortest, middle-to-middle, longest-to-longest. Take each ratio (big ÷ small) and check that all three give the same number.
9 ÷ 6 = 1.5
12 ÷ 8 = 1.5
15 ÷ 10 = 1.5
All three ratios match → Q2 answer: YES. Continue to Q3.
3
Q3 — Is the scale factor = 1?
The scale factor is 1.5, not 1. So the shapes are the same shape but a different size.
Scale factor = 1.5, which is not 1.
→ Q3 answer: NO → these shapes are SIMILAR.
Conclusion △ABC and △DEF are SIMILAR (scale factor 1.5)
