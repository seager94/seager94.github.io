1Warm-up — when are two shapes really "the same"?

Today's big question. Take a moment to think about it before we lock in a definition.

Imagine you have a photo on your phone. You print it three times:

  • 📄 Print A — exact original size
  • 📄 Print B — same original size, but you ran it through the photocopier afterwards (still the same size)
  • 📄 Print C — same photo, but enlarged to A3

Two of these prints could be stacked perfectly on top of each other so the edges line up. The third can't.

Prints A and B are congruent — same shape AND same size. Print C has the same shape but is a different size — so it is not congruent to the others.

🎯 Today's framing question
"When are two shapes really the same?"
In Year 7 you learnt about three rigid transformations: translations (slides), reflections (flips), and rotations (turns). These are today's test — if you can map one shape onto another using only those three moves, the shapes are congruent.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. You'll use these five terms constantly today.

Congruent
Tap to flip
Two shapes are congruent if they have identical shape AND identical size.
If you could pick one up and lay it perfectly on top of the other, they're congruent.
The symbol ≅
Tap to flip
We write ≅ to mean "is congruent to". It's an equals sign with a wavy line on top — the wavy line says "same shape".
△ABC ≅ △PQR means triangle ABC is congruent to triangle PQR.
Corresponding sides
Tap to flip
Sides in matching positions in two congruent shapes. They have equal length.
If △ABC ≅ △PQR, then side AB corresponds to side PQ.
Corresponding angles
Tap to flip
Angles in matching positions in two congruent shapes. They are equal in measure.
If △ABC ≅ △PQR, then ∠A = ∠P, ∠B = ∠Q, ∠C = ∠R.
Rigid transformation
Tap to flip
A move that preserves shape and size: translation, reflection, or rotation. Enlarging or shrinking are NOT rigid.
Slide, flip, or turn — all rigid. Stretch or zoom — not rigid.

5Worked example — using a congruence statement to find every missing part

This is the heart of the lesson. The five steps below are exactly what you'll do every time you see a congruence statement.

GIVEN: △ABC ≅ △PQR. On △ABC we know: AB = 5 cm, BC = 7 cm, CA = 9 cm, ∠A = 80°, ∠B = 60°.
FIND: the lengths PQ, QR, RP and the angles ∠P, ∠Q, ∠R.
△ABC
≅
△PQR
1
Read the congruence statement and write down what it tells us
The symbol ≅ means "is congruent to". Congruent shapes have ALL corresponding sides equal AND ALL corresponding angles equal.
△ABC ≅ △PQR ⟹ three pairs of equal sides and three pairs of equal angles.
2
Write the vertex correspondence — read the letters in order
The order of the letters tells us the correspondence. First letter matches first letter, second matches second, third matches third. This is the most important step of the lesson.
△ABC ≅ △PQR
A ↔ P,   B ↔ Q,   C ↔ R
3
Identify corresponding SIDES — and fill in the missing lengths
A side is just the two vertices at its endpoints. So AB corresponds to PQ (because A↔P and B↔Q). Corresponding sides are equal in length.
AB ↔ PQ  ⟹  PQ = 5 cm
BC ↔ QR  ⟹  QR = 7 cm
CA ↔ RP  ⟹  RP = 9 cm
4
Identify corresponding ANGLES — and fill in the ones we already know
Angles match using the same correspondence. ∠A ↔ ∠P, ∠B ↔ ∠Q, ∠C ↔ ∠R. We're told two of them, so we get two for free.
∠A ↔ ∠P  ⟹  ∠P = 80°
∠B ↔ ∠Q  ⟹  ∠Q = 60°
∠C ↔ ∠R  ⟹  ∠R = ∠C (we don't know ∠C yet — see step 5)
5
Find the last angle using the angle sum of a triangle
The three angles in any triangle add to 180°. We use that on △ABC to find ∠C, then ∠R = ∠C from the correspondence.
∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°  (angle sum of a triangle)
80° + 60° + ∠C = 180°
∠C = 40°  ⟹  ∠R = 40°
Answer — △PQR fully labelled PQ = 5 cm, QR = 7 cm, RP = 9 cm, ∠P = 80°, ∠Q = 60°, ∠R = 40°
💡 Three big ideas to remember: (1) the order of letters in the congruence statement gives the correspondence, (2) corresponding sides are equal, (3) corresponding angles are equal — and if one angle is missing, use the angle sum 180°.
