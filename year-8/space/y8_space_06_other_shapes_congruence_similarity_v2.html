1Warm-up — same shape, same size?

A quick recap of what we already know, then we open it up to all shapes.

We've already looked at congruence and similarity in triangles. Remember:

  • Congruent triangles are identical — same size and same shape. You can place one exactly on top of the other.
  • Similar triangles have the same shape but might be different sizes — one is an enlargement (or reduction) of the other.

Today we extend those two ideas to any shape — squares, rectangles, pentagons, hexagons, trapezia. The definitions are exactly the same; we just need to check all the corresponding sides and angles, not just three.

💡 Think real-world: two passport photos from the same printer are congruent — identical copies. A small photo print and a large poster of the same image are similar — same shape, scaled up. By the end of today you'll be able to tell whether any two shapes are congruent, similar, or neither.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. You'll use these five words over and over today.

Congruent
Tap to flip
Two shapes are congruent if they are identical — same size and same shape. Every pair of corresponding sides and angles is equal.
Two squares with side 5 cm are congruent.
Similar
Tap to flip
Two shapes are similar if they have the same shape — one is an enlargement or reduction of the other. Corresponding angles are equal; corresponding sides are in the same ratio.
A 3 × 5 rectangle and a 9 × 15 rectangle are similar.
Corresponding sides
Tap to flip
The pairs of sides that match up when one shape is placed on top of the other.
In two similar pentagons, the shortest side of one matches the shortest of the other.
Corresponding angles
Tap to flip
The pairs of angles in the matching positions. In similar (or congruent) shapes these are always equal.
In similar quadrilaterals, all four pairs of angles match.
Scale factor
Tap to flip
The number you multiply each side of the small shape by to get the large shape. Found by large ÷ small for any pair of corresponding sides.
If small side = 4 and large side = 12, scale factor = 3.

5Worked example — the 3-step method

These two rectangles are similar. Find the missing side x.

1
Identify corresponding sides
Match the longer sides together and the shorter sides together.
Longer pair: 6 cm ↔ 18 cm
Shorter pair: 4 cm ↔ x cm
2
Find the scale factor
Divide a known pair: large ÷ small.
Scale factor = 18 ÷ 6 = 3
So every side of the large shape is 3 times the corresponding small side.
3
Apply the scale factor to find x
Multiply the matching small side by 3.
x = 4 × 3 = 12 cm
Check: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 ✓ (matches the scale factor)
Answer x = 12 cm
