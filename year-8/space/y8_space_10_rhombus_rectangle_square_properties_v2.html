1From last lesson — what we already know

A rhombus, a rectangle, and a square are all special parallelograms. So whatever we proved about parallelograms last lesson, they inherit for free.

What we proved about parallelograms

  • Opposite sides are equal in length
  • Opposite angles are equal
  • Co-interior angles are supplementary (add to 180°)
  • Diagonals bisect each other

All proved using congruent triangles.

What we'll prove today

Special parallelograms have extra properties. We'll prove three of them using the same technique:

  • A rhombus has perpendicular diagonals
  • A rhombus diagonal bisects the corner angles
  • A rectangle has diagonals of equal length

A square is both — so it gets everything.

5Worked example — diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular

Theorem. In rhombus ABCD with diagonals meeting at E, AC ⊥ BD (i.e. ∠AEB = 90°).

1
Pick the two triangles to compare
The diagonals split the rhombus into four small triangles. Compare two that share the diagonal BE and meet at E.
Compare △AEB and △CEB.
We want to show they're congruent — then ∠AEB will equal ∠CEB.
2
Find three matching pairs of sides — SSS
List the parts we know are equal, with a reason for each.
AB = CB   (all sides of a rhombus are equal)
AE = CE   (diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other — last lesson)
BE = BE   (common side)
∴ △AEB ≅ △CEB   (SSS)
3
Use CPCTC and the straight angle
Once the triangles are congruent, corresponding angles must be equal. Combine that with the straight line AEC.
∠AEB = ∠CEB   (CPCTC)
∠AEB + ∠CEB = 180°   (angles on a straight line AEC)
So 2 × ∠AEB = 180°, giving ∠AEB = 90°.
∴ AC ⊥ BD. ∎
Result The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular.
