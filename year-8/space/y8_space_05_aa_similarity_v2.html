1Warm-up — when does shape stay the same?

A quick recap on what we mean by similar before we look at the shortcut for proving it.

For example — imagine two skate ramps at a skatepark. One is a small ramp built for beginners; the other is a huge ramp for the pros. They're very different in size, but the angle of the slope is identical on both — both ramps climb at exactly 30°.

Picture the triangle each ramp makes with the ground:

Different sizes — same shape.
Last lesson we said two shapes are similar when one is an enlargement (or reduction) of the other — same shape, different size — and we found the scale factor from matching sides. Today's question: how little information do we actually need to be SURE two triangles are similar? The answer is surprisingly small — and it's called the AA test.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. Five words you'll use every time you reason about similar triangles.

Similar
Tap to flip
Two shapes are similar if one is an enlargement of the other — same shape, different size.
Symbol: ~. We write △ABC ~ △DEF.
Corresponding angles
Tap to flip
Angles in matching positions in two similar shapes. They are always equal.
In △ABC ~ △DEF: ∠A & ∠D correspond, ∠B & ∠E correspond, ∠C & ∠F correspond.
Corresponding sides
Tap to flip
Sides in matching positions in two similar shapes. Their ratio is the scale factor.
The side opposite ∠A corresponds to the side opposite ∠D.
Angle sum
Tap to flip
The three angles of any triangle always add to 180°.
If two angles are 60° and 80°, the third must be 40° (since 180 − 60 − 80 = 40).
AA test
Tap to flip
If two pairs of angles are equal, the triangles are similar. (The third pair must also be equal — by angle sum.)
"AA" stands for Angle-Angle — two matching angles is all you need.

5Worked example — the 3-step method

In △ABC, ∠A = 70° and ∠B = 50°. In △PQR, ∠P = 70° and ∠Q = 50°. Are the triangles similar? If so, state the reason.

1
List the angles you're given
Write them out side-by-side so you can compare easily.
△ABC: ∠A = 70°, ∠B = 50°
△PQR: ∠P = 70°, ∠Q = 50°
2
Find two pairs of equal angles
Look across the two triangles. Match up angles that are equal.
∠A = ∠P = 70°  ✓
∠B = ∠Q = 50°  ✓
Two pairs of equal angles — that's all the AA test asks for.
3
State the conclusion with a reason
Always quote the test you used. For Year 8, write "by AA" as your reason.
∴ △ABC ~ △PQR  (AA)
Bonus — the third angles must also be equal: ∠C = ∠R = 180° − 70° − 50° = 60°.
Answer △ABC ~ △PQR  (AA)

6Together — are △XYZ and △LMN similar?

△XYZ has ∠X = 45° and ∠Y = 65°. △LMN has ∠L = 45° and ∠N = 70°. Work through the three prompts below as a class.

💡 Why this is harder than it looks: △XYZ has angles {45°, 65°, 70°} and △LMN has angles {45°, 65°, 70°} — the third angles happen to match! So even though we were given different angle pairs in each triangle, they ARE similar by AA. Always work out the third angle if you're stuck.
