1Why this matters

A real decision that's secretly today's statistics.

Every five years the Australian Government runs the Census — by law, it tries to count every single person in the country on one night. It costs hundreds of millions of dollars and takes years to process.

Yet a TV company can tell how many people watched the grand final by asking only a few thousand households — not the whole nation. And a company can guess how long its light globes last without burning out every globe it makes.

Statisticians make this choice constantly. A census collects data from every member of the population; a sample collects it from a representative subset. Today you'll learn to decide which one fits — by weighing cost, accuracy and practicality. The key idea: a well-chosen sample can be surprisingly small and still be trusted.

Census fits

Sample needed

💡 A census fits when the population is small and easy to reach, or when the law demands an exact count. A sample is needed when the population is too large, when a census is too costly, or when testing destroys the item (you can't open every parachute and still sell them).

5Worked example — the 3-step method

Decide: a parachute factory must know whether its parachutes open reliably. Should it test every parachute (a census) or a representative sample?

1
Name the population and the variable
Say exactly what group we care about and what we're measuring.
Population = every parachute the factory makes.
Variable = does it open reliably when deployed?
2
Ask: is a census practical? (cost · accuracy · practicality)
Weigh what a census would actually cost here.
Testing a parachute means deploying it — the test uses it up.
A census would test (and destroy) 100% of the stock → none left to sell.
Cost of a census here = impossibly high. This is destructive testing.
3
Decide and justify
Because a census is impossible, take a representative sample and infer about the whole batch.
Test a representative sample (e.g. a fixed fraction from each production batch). If the sample opens reliably, we infer the whole population does — accepting a small, measured risk.
Decision Sample — destructive testing makes a census impossible
