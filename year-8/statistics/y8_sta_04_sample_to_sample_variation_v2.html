1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's statistics.

Two polling companies each ask 1000 randomly chosen voters the same question on the same day. One reports 46% support; the other reports 49%.

Neither made a mistake. They surveyed different random samples, so they got slightly different results. If each had asked only 20 people, the two answers could have been wildly apart — say 35% and 60%.

That gap between random samples is sampling variation. It never fully disappears, but it shrinks as the sample gets bigger. Today we'll draw sample after sample from a fixed population of heights, watch how much the mean jumps around, and see the sample size steady it.

4Worked example — summarise a sample

One random sample of 10 heights (cm): 158, 165, 172, 149, 160, 168, 155, 163, 170, 152

1
Order the data → find median and range
Sort smallest to largest, then read off the middle and the spread.
149, 152, 155, 158, 160, 163, 165, 168, 170, 172
Median = (160 + 163) ÷ 2 = 161.5 cm
Range = 172 − 149 = 23 cm
2
Add them up → find the mean
Total the ten values, then divide by 10.
Sum = 1612
Mean = 1612 ÷ 10 = 161.2 cm
3
Compare to the population — and expect variation
This sample's mean is 161.2 cm; the population mean is 161.1 cm. Very close — but the next random sample of 10 will almost certainly give a different mean. No single sample is "the" answer.
This is just one of thousands of possible samples of 10. Draw another and the mean, median and range all shift a little. That shifting is sampling variation.
This sample Mean 161.2 · Median 161.5 · Range 23 cm
