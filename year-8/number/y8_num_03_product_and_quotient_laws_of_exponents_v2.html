1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's maths.

Computer storage is measured in powers of 2. One kilobyte is 210 bytes. One megabyte is 210 × 210 bytes.

Scientists recording climate data write measurements like 103 × 104 (thousands of thousands). Astronomers express distances as 3 × 108 metres per second for the speed of light.

Every time someone multiplies or divides two powers with the same base, they are using the laws you'll learn today — usually without even writing out the expanded form.

The question is: is there a shortcut?

Today we discover that 210 × 210 = 220, and 103 × 104 = 107 — without multiplying out billions of individual factors. The shortcut comes from expanded form, and it only works when the bases match.

5Worked examples — building the laws from expanded form

Use the "Reveal next step" button on each example. In teacher mode, all steps show at once.

Example 1 — Product law: Simplify 23 × 22
1
Write each power in expanded form
Replace each power with repeated multiplication, then combine.
23 × 22
= (2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2)
2
Count the total number of 2s
Brackets don't change anything — all factors are 2s multiplied together.
(2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2)
= 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2
= 25    (there are 3 + 2 = 5 twos)
3
Notice the pattern — this is the Product Law
The exponents added: 3 + 2 = 5. This works for any base, as long as both powers share the same base.
Product Law am × an = am + n
⚠ The bases must be the same
Answer 23 × 22 = 25
Example 2 — Quotient law: Simplify 35 ÷ 32
1
Write in expanded form
Write the numerator and denominator of the division as repeated multiplication.
35 ÷ 32
= (3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 3) ÷ (3 × 3)
2
Cancel common factors
Each 3 in the divisor cancels one 3 in the dividend — like cancelling in a fraction.
(3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 3) ÷ (3 × 3)
= 3 × 3 × 3
= 33    (5 − 2 = 3 threes remain)
3
Notice the pattern — this is the Quotient Law
The exponents subtracted: 5 − 2 = 3. Again, this only works when the bases match.
Quotient Law am ÷ an = am − n
⚠ The bases must be the same
Answer 35 ÷ 32 = 33
Example 3 — Applying both laws: Simplify 54 × 53 ÷ 52
1
Check: do all bases match?
Before applying any law, confirm every term has the same base. If they don't match, stop — neither law applies.
54 × 53 ÷ 52
Base of 54: 5  ·  Base of 53: 5  ·  Base of 52: 5
✓ All bases match — laws can be applied.
2
Apply the product law first
Multiply the first two powers — add their exponents.
54 × 53 ÷ 52
= 54 + 3 ÷ 52
= 57 ÷ 52
3
Apply the quotient law
Divide — subtract the exponents. The result is a single power of 5.
57 ÷ 52
= 57 − 2
= 55
Answer 54 × 53 ÷ 52 = 55
