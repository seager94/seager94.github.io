1Why this matters

Real mathematics, running right now — in weather apps, submarines, and climate science.

Every weather app on every phone uses signed rational numbers. When the Bureau of Meteorology reports that Canberra's overnight low is −4.5°C and the daytime high is 12.8°C, meteorologists are applying operations to signed numbers to calculate the temperature change (+17.3°C).

Oceanographers record depths as negative values — a reef at −38.5 m sits below a surface at 0. Pilots and surveyors use positive values for altitude above sea level. The Dead Sea lies at −430 m; Mount Everest reaches +8 849 m — both are measured from the same zero point.

Today we build mathematical models for these situations — writing signed expressions that represent what's happening, calculating results, and checking whether the answers make sense.

The modelling cycle — used in every I Do example today:
  1. Formulate — assign signed values; choose an operation
  2. Calculate — evaluate the expression
  3. Interpret & Review — explain the answer in context; check it's reasonable

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. You'll use all five terms today.

Signed number
Tap to flip
A number with a positive (+) or negative (−) sign, showing direction or position relative to zero.−3.5°C means 3.5 degrees below zero. +3.5°C means 3.5 degrees above zero.
Rational number
Tap to flip
Any number that can be written as a fraction of two integers. Includes all integers, fractions, and terminating or recurring decimals.−3.5 = −7/2; 0.75 = 3/4; −12 = −12/1 — all rational.
Net change
Tap to flip
The total change after all increases and decreases have been combined — the final difference from the starting value.Start at −5°C, rise 8°C, fall 3°C → net change = +5°C; final temp = 0°C.
Mathematical model
Tap to flip
A mathematical representation of a real-world situation. Here we use signed expressions to represent physical scenarios.Writing −3.5 + 18.4 to model a temperature that starts at −3.5°C and rises 18.4°C.
Reference point
Tap to flip
The zero point used for measurement. Positive values are above it; negative values are below it.Sea level is the reference point for depth and altitude. 0°C is the reference point for temperature.

5Worked example 1 — temperature change

Alice Springs has a midnight temperature of −3.5°C. By noon, the temperature has risen 18.4°C. What is the noon temperature?

1
Formulate — assign signed values and choose an operation
Identify what we know and what we need to find. Assign signs based on the reference point (0°C).
Starting temperature = −3.5°C   (below zero → negative)
Temperature change = +18.4°C   (a rise → positive)

Model: noon temp = starting temp + change
              = −3.5 + 18.4
2
Calculate — evaluate the expression
Work out the sum of the two signed values.
−3.5 + 18.4
= 18.4 − 3.5     (18.4 is larger and positive, so the result is positive)
= 14.9
3
Interpret & Review — explain in context; check reasonableness
Translate the number back into the real-world situation. Ask: does this answer make sense?
Interpretation: The noon temperature in Alice Springs is 14.9°C.

Review: We started below zero (−3.5°C) and rose by more than 18 degrees. A positive answer in the teens is reasonable — it should definitely be above zero. ✓
Answer Noon temperature = 14.9°C

6Worked example 2 — submarine depth

A submarine is at 45.5 m below sea level. It rises 28.75 m. What is its new depth?

1
Formulate — assign signed values and choose an operation
Sea level = 0. Below sea level = negative; above = positive. A rise means moving in the positive direction.
Current depth = −45.5 m   (below sea level → negative)
Change = +28.75 m   (rising → positive)

Model: new depth = current depth + change
              = −45.5 + 28.75
2
Calculate — evaluate the expression
The negative value is larger in size, so the result will be negative.
−45.5 + 28.75
= −(45.5 − 28.75)     (45.5 is larger; result stays negative)
= −16.75
3
Interpret & Review — explain in context; check reasonableness
What does −16.75 mean in this real-world situation? Is it plausible?
Interpretation: The submarine is now 16.75 m below sea level.

Review: The submarine started at −45.5 m and rose 28.75 m — not enough to reach the surface. It should still be below zero, and 16.75 m is a shallower depth than 45.5 m. ✓
Answer New depth = −16.75 m (16.75 m below sea level)

7Worked example 3 — comparing average temperatures

Adelaide's average winter temperature is 11.2°C. A Canadian city's average winter temperature is −14.8°C. How much warmer is Adelaide's winter?

1
Formulate — assign signed values and choose an operation
To find how much warmer Adelaide is, subtract the Canadian temperature from Adelaide's.
Adelaide winter = +11.2°C
Canadian winter = −14.8°C

Model: difference = Adelaide − Canadian
                 = 11.2 − (−14.8)
2
Calculate — evaluate the expression
Subtracting a negative is the same as adding its positive — so we add the magnitudes.
11.2 − (−14.8)
= 11.2 + 14.8     (subtracting a negative = adding a positive)
= 26.0
3
Interpret & Review — explain in context; check reasonableness
What does 26°C mean here? The gap between opposite-signed values should exceed both magnitudes.
Interpretation: Adelaide's average winter temperature is 26°C warmer than the Canadian city's.

Review: One city is positive, the other is negative — the distance between them spans both sides of zero, so it should exceed 14.8 (the Canadian magnitude) and exceed 11.2 (Adelaide's). 26 > 14.8 and 26 > 11.2 ✓
Answer Adelaide's winter is 26°C warmer
