1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's maths.

You and two friends split a restaurant bill of $10 equally. Your calculator shows $3.3333333… — the digits go on forever. But if four of you split it, the answer is exactly $2.50.

Why does dividing by 3 produce a never-ending decimal while dividing by 4 gives a tidy answer? The secret lies in the prime factors of the number you divide by.

Today you will learn to predict, just by looking at the denominator of a fraction, whether its decimal expansion will end or repeat forever — without doing any division at all.

The big idea:

A fraction's decimal terminates if the denominator (in lowest terms) has only the prime factors 2 and/or 5.

Any other prime factor — 3, 7, 11, 13, … — causes the decimal to recur (repeat a block of digits forever).

Recurring decimals are written with dot notation: a dot above the first and last digit of the repeating block. For example, 13 = 0.3

Terminating decimal

A decimal that ends after a finite number of digits. EXAMPLE — 34 = 0.75 — stops after 2 decimal places.
Recurring decimal
Tap to flip
A decimal with a digit or block that repeats forever. EXAMPLE — 13 = 0.333… = 0.3
Prime factorisation
Tap to flip
Writing a number as a product of its prime factors. EXAMPLE — 24 = 2³ × 3. The prime factors are 2 and 3.
Dot notation
Tap to flip
A dot above a digit to show it repeats. Dots on the first and last digit of a multi-digit block. EXAMPLE — 0.3 means 0.3333… and 0.454 means... wait — 0.45 means 0.4545…
Lowest terms
Tap to flip
A fraction where the numerator and denominator share no common factors other than 1. EXAMPLE — 4/6 → 2/3 in lowest terms. Apply the terminating rule to the simplified denominator.

38 29 725 56 1116 415

✓ Terminating — denominator has only factors 2 and/or 5

↻ Recurring — denominator has another prime factor

💡 Why does the denominator decide? A decimal terminates when the fraction can be written with a denominator that is a power of 10. Since 10 = 2 × 5, the only primes that can build a power of 10 are 2 and 5. Any other prime factor in the denominator means no power of 10 is reachable — so the decimal must recur.

5Worked examples — predict and convert

Use "Reveal next step" on each example. In teacher mode all steps show at once.

The Terminating Decimal Rule A fraction in lowest terms gives a terminating decimal if and only if the denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5.
Any other prime factor (3, 7, 11, 13, …) in the denominator → the decimal recurs.
Example 1 — Terminating: Predict and convert 725
1
Predict — prime factorise the denominator
Find all prime factors of 25, then apply the rule.
25 = 5 × 5 = 5²
Prime factors of 25: only 5
Rule check: only 2s and/or 5s? Yes ✓
Prediction: TERMINATING
2
Convert — make the denominator a power of 10
Multiply top and bottom by whatever turns 25 into 100 (or 1000, etc.).
25 × 4 = 100
725 = 7 × 425 × 4 = 28100
3
State the answer — confirm it terminates
A denominator of 100 gives exactly 2 decimal places.
28100 = 0.28 — this is a finite decimal. ✓
Answer 725 = 0.28  (terminating)
Example 2 — Recurring and dot notation: Predict and convert 13
1
Predict — prime factorise the denominator
Find all prime factors of 3.
3 = 3¹  (3 is itself prime)
Prime factors of 3: only 3
Rule check: only 2s and/or 5s? No ✗ — 3 is a different prime
Prediction: RECURRING
2
Divide — watch the remainder cycle
Perform 1 ÷ 3. Notice that the remainder 1 keeps reappearing.
1 ÷ 3:
  10 ÷ 3 = 3 remainder 1
  10 ÷ 3 = 3 remainder 1
  10 ÷ 3 = 3 remainder 1  ← remainder repeats forever

1 ÷ 3 = 0.333333… (the digit 3 recurs)
3
Write using dot notation
Place a dot above the repeating digit. Since only the digit 3 repeats, one dot is used.
0.333… = 0.3
The dot above 3 means "this digit repeats forever."
Answer 13 = 0.3  (recurring — the digit 3 repeats)
Example 3 — Recurring with mixed factors: Predict and convert 724
1
Predict — prime factorise the denominator
24 has more than one distinct prime factor — find them all.
24 = 8 × 3 = 2³ × 3
Prime factors of 24: 2 and 3
Rule check: only 2s and/or 5s? No ✗ — factor 3 is present
Prediction: RECURRING
The factor of 2³ cannot fix the factor of 3 — any 3 in the denominator causes a recurrence.
2
Divide — find the decimal and locate where it starts repeating
Perform 7 ÷ 24. The non-repeating part comes first, then the repeating digit appears.
7 ÷ 24 = 0.291666666…
  Non-repeating part: 0.291
  Repeating digit: 6 (remainder 16 recurs at this point)
Common slip: Students sometimes write 0.291̇6̇ thinking both digits in the last block repeat. Only the digit 6 repeats here — the digits 2, 9 and 1 appear once only.
3
Write using dot notation — dot above the repeating digit only
Since only the 6 repeats, place a single dot above it.
7 ÷ 24 = 0.29166
Read as: "zero point two nine one six recurring."
Answer 724 = 0.29166  (recurring — the digit 6 repeats)
