1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's maths.

A telescope image is stored in a data file. The file is compressed in three stages. After the first compression, the file size is 24 kilobytes. In the second stage, the file size is squared — making it (24)2. In the third stage it is cubed — making it ((24)2)3.

To find the final file size, do you really need to multiply out billions of 2s? No — today we find the shortcut. We also discover a strange result: what does any non-zero number to the power zero equal?

Two new laws today:

1. Power of a Power: (24)3 = 24 × 3 = 212 — multiply the exponents instead of expanding.

2. Zero Exponent: 34 ÷ 34 = 1 and = 34−4 = 30, so 30 = 1 — a surprising result proved directly from the quotient law.

Example 1 — Deriving the Power of a Power Law: Simplify (23)2
1
Expand the outer exponent — write as repeated multiplication
The outer exponent 2 means "multiply 23 by itself 2 times."
(23)2
= 23 × 23   (two copies of 23)
2
Expand each inner power and count all the 2s
Write each 23 in full, then count all the factors of 2.
(2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2 × 2)
= 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2
= 26   (3 + 3 = 6 twos altogether)
3
Spot the pattern — this is the Power of a Power Law
The exponents multiplied: 3 × 2 = 6. We could have gone straight to 26 without expanding. This works for any base and any positive integer exponents.
Power of a Power Law (am)n = am × n
Multiply the exponents — don't add them
Answer (23)2 = 26
Example 2 — Applying the law: Simplify (54)3
1
Identify the structure
This is a power raised to a power. Identify the base, the inner exponent, and the outer exponent.
(54)3
Base: 5  ·  Inner exponent: 4  ·  Outer exponent: 3
✓ This is a power of a power — the law applies.
2
Apply the Power of a Power Law — multiply the exponents
Multiply the inner and outer exponents. Keep the base unchanged.
(54)3
= 54 × 3
= 512
3
Verify using the product law (optional but confirms)
Expanding the outer exponent gives three copies of 54. By the product law: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. Same answer — confirms the shortcut works.
(54)3 = 54 × 54 × 54 = 54+4+4 = 512 ✓
Answer (54)3 = 512
Example 3 — Justifying the Zero Exponent: Why does 30 = 1?
1
Write a quotient where both powers are equal
Any non-zero number divided by itself equals 1. Choose 34 ÷ 34.
34 ÷ 34 = 1   (any non-zero number ÷ itself = 1)
2
Apply the Quotient Law to the same expression
The quotient law says: same base, subtract the exponents. 4 − 4 = 0.
34 ÷ 34 = 34 − 4 = 30
3
Both results are equal — so 30 = 1
We found two different ways to simplify the same expression. Since both are correct, they must be equal. This works for any non-zero base.
34 ÷ 34 = 1
34 ÷ 34 = 30
Therefore: 30 = 1
Zero Exponent Rule a0 = 1  (for any a ≠ 0)
00 is undefined — the base must be non-zero
Result 30 = 1  (justified using the Quotient Law)

All four exponent laws — covered so far

Product Law am × an = am+n
Quotient Law am ÷ an = am−n
Power of a Power ★ New (am)n = am × n
Zero Exponent ★ New a0 = 1  (a ≠ 0)
