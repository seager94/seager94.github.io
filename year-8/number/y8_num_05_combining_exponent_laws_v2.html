1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's maths.

A sculptor enlarges a 2 cm cube to a 6 cm cube. How many times larger is the volume of the big cube compared to the small cube?

The volume of a cube with side length s is s3, so the scale factor of volume is:

63 ÷ 23

At first glance you might try to use the quotient law — but can you? The bases are 6 and 2. They are different. The quotient law only applies when the bases match. So instead we evaluate numerically: 216 ÷ 8 = 27. That's 33.

The big idea today:

The four exponent laws — product, quotient, power of a power, and zero exponent — can be applied in combination, one after another, to simplify a single expression.

The golden rule: always check that the bases match before applying the product or quotient law.

If they don't match, the law does not apply — evaluate numerically instead.

5Worked examples — combining the laws

Use the "Reveal next step" button on each example. In teacher mode all steps show at once.

Example 1 — Product law then Quotient law: Simplify 35 × 33 ÷ 34
1
Check the bases — do they match?
Before applying any law, identify all bases in the expression.
35 × 33 ÷ 34
All bases are 3 — the laws apply. ✓
2
Apply the Product Law to the first two powers
Same base — add the exponents. Work left to right.
35 × 33 ÷ 34
= 35 + 3 ÷ 34
= 38 ÷ 34
3
Apply the Quotient Law
Same base — subtract the exponents. The answer is a single power.
38 ÷ 34
= 38 − 4
= 34
Answer 35 × 33 ÷ 34 = 34
Example 2 — Power of a Power law then Product law: Simplify (23)2 × 25
1
Check the bases — do they all match?
Identify the base inside the brackets and the base of the separate power.
(23)2 × 25
Both powers have base 2 — the laws apply. ✓
2
Apply the Power of a Power Law first
A power inside brackets raised to another power — multiply the exponents.
(23)2 × 25
= 23 × 2 × 25
= 26 × 25
3
Apply the Product Law
Now both terms have base 2 — add the exponents.
26 × 25
= 26 + 5
= 211
Answer (23)2 × 25 = 211
Example 3 — Application: Volume scale factor 63 ÷ 23
1
Check the bases — do they match?
Enlarging a 2 cm cube to a 6 cm cube. Volume scale factor = 63 ÷ 23. Check bases before applying any law.
63 ÷ 23
Base 6 ≠ Base 2 — the quotient law does not apply.
Stop! A common error is to write 63 ÷ 23 = (6 − 2)3 = 43. This is wrong — the quotient law subtracts exponents (not bases), and only when the bases already match.
2
Evaluate each power separately
Since the bases differ, compute each power as a whole number first.
63 = 6 × 6 × 6 = 216
23 = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8
3
Divide the results — interpret the answer
The volume scale factor tells us how many times larger the big cube is.
216 ÷ 8 = 27
Note: 27 = 33, so the volume grows by a factor of 33.
Answer 63 ÷ 23 = 27  (the 6 cm cube has 27 times the volume of the 2 cm cube)

All four exponent laws — use these as your reference

Product Law am × an = am+n
Quotient Law am ÷ an = am−n
Power of a Power (am)n = am × n
Zero Exponent a0 = 1  (a ≠ 0)

⚠ Always check: do the bases match before applying the product or quotient law?
