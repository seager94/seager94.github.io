1Why this matters

Income tax affects every working person in Australia — and it is pure percentage mathematics.

When you earn income in Australia, the government takes a portion as income tax. The rate you pay depends on how much you earn — higher earners pay a higher percentage on the income in each extra bracket, but not on their whole income.

This is a marginal tax system. The first $18,200 is tax-free. Income above that is taxed in steps — each step has its own rate. The table below is based on Australia's individual income tax rates.

Taxable incomeTax on this incomeRate
$0 – $18,200Nil0%
$18,201 – $45,00019c for each $1 over $18,20019%
$45,001 – $120,000$5,092 plus 32.5c for each $1 over $45,00032.5%
$120,001 – $180,000$29,467 plus 37c for each $1 over $120,00037%
$180,001 and over$51,667 plus 45c for each $1 over $180,00045%
Two key rates — and why they differ:
  • Marginal rate — the rate applied to each extra dollar in a given bracket (e.g. 32.5%)
  • Effective rate — total tax ÷ total income × 100; always lower than the marginal rate because lower brackets pay lower rates first
Modelling cycle used in every I Do example:
Formulate → identify bracket, write the tax expression
Calculate → evaluate; find effective rate
Interpret & Review → explain in context; check reasonableness

5Worked example 1 — low income: $28,000

A part-time worker earns $28,000 per year. Calculate their income tax and effective tax rate.

1
Formulate — locate the bracket and write the tax expression
Find $28,000 in the tax table. It sits between $18,201 and $45,000 — the 19% bracket.
Income = $28,000
Bracket: $18,201 – $45,000   →   marginal rate = 19%

Tax on first $18,200 = $0   (tax-free zone)
Amount above $18,200 = $28,000 − $18,200 = $9,800

Tax model: tax = 19% × $9,800
2
Calculate — evaluate the tax and the effective rate
Multiply out, then divide total tax by total income to get the effective rate.
Tax = 19% × $9,800
      = 0.19 × $9,800
      = $1,862

Effective rate = $1,862$28,000 × 100  ≈ 6.7%
3
Interpret & Review — explain in context; check reasonableness
What does the answer mean? Why is the effective rate (6.7%) so much lower than the marginal rate (19%)?
Interpretation: On a $28,000 income, income tax = $1,862.
The worker keeps $28,000 − $1,862 = $26,138 (take-home pay).

Review: The effective rate is 6.7%, well below the 19% marginal rate. This makes sense: only $9,800 (out of $28,000) is taxed at 19% — the first $18,200 is taxed at 0%. Averaging these lower and higher rates gives an effective rate well below 19%. ✓
Answer Tax = $1,862  |  Marginal rate: 19%  |  Effective rate: 6.7%

6Worked example 2 — middle income: $75,000

A full-time professional earns $75,000 per year. Calculate their income tax and effective tax rate.

1
Formulate — locate the bracket and write the tax expression
$75,000 sits between $45,001 and $120,000 — the 32.5% bracket. The table already gives us the tax on the first $45,000.
Income = $75,000
Bracket: $45,001 – $120,000   →   marginal rate = 32.5%

Tax on first $45,000 = $5,092   (read directly from the table)
Amount above $45,000 = $75,000 − $45,000 = $30,000

Tax model: tax = $5,092 + 32.5% × $30,000
2
Calculate — evaluate the tax and the effective rate
Calculate the 32.5% portion, add it to $5,092, then find the effective rate.
32.5% × $30,000 = 0.325 × $30,000 = $9,750
Tax = $5,092 + $9,750 = $14,842

Effective rate = $14,842$75,000 × 100  ≈ 19.8%
3
Interpret & Review — explain in context; check reasonableness
Compare effective rate (19.8%) to marginal rate (32.5%). Why is the gap smaller here than in Example 1?
Interpretation: On a $75,000 income, income tax = $14,842.
Take-home pay = $75,000 − $14,842 = $60,158.

Review: The effective rate (19.8%) is well below the marginal rate (32.5%) — but the gap is narrower than in Example 1. As income grows, more of it sits in higher brackets, so the effective rate rises towards (but never reaches) the marginal rate. ✓
Answer Tax = $14,842  |  Marginal rate: 32.5%  |  Effective rate: 19.8%

7Worked example 3 — high income: $150,000

A senior manager earns $150,000 per year. Calculate their income tax, effective rate, and compare all three examples.

1
Formulate — locate the bracket and write the tax expression
$150,000 sits between $120,001 and $180,000 — the 37% bracket. The table gives the tax on the first $120,000.
Income = $150,000
Bracket: $120,001 – $180,000   →   marginal rate = 37%

Tax on first $120,000 = $29,467   (read from table)
Amount above $120,000 = $150,000 − $120,000 = $30,000

Tax model: tax = $29,467 + 37% × $30,000
2
Calculate — evaluate the tax and the effective rate
Calculate the 37% portion, add it to $29,467, then find the effective rate.
37% × $30,000 = 0.37 × $30,000 = $11,100
Tax = $29,467 + $11,100 = $40,567

Effective rate = $40,567$150,000 × 100  ≈ 27.0%
3
Interpret & Review — compare all three examples and explain the pattern
Now compare across all three incomes. Notice how the effective rate rises gradually while the marginal rate jumps in steps.
Income Tax paid Marginal rate Effective rate
$28,000 $1,862 19% 6.7%
$75,000 $14,842 32.5% 19.8%
$150,000 $40,567 37% 27.0%
Key insight: The marginal rate jumps in steps (19% → 32.5% → 37%) every time income crosses a bracket boundary. The effective rate rises gradually (6.7% → 19.8% → 27.0%) and is always lower — because the lower-taxed income earned first brings the average down.
Answer Tax = $40,567  |  Marginal rate: 37%  |  Effective rate: 27.0%
