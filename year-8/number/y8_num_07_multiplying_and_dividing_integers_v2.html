1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's maths.

A coastal city records a temperature drop of 3°C every hour during a cold snap. At noon the temperature is 12°C.

  • After 1 hour: 12 + (−3) = 9°C
  • After 2 hours: 12 + 2 × (−3) = 6°C
  • After 4 hours: 12 + 4 × (−3) = 0°C
  • After 5 hours: 12 + 5 × (−3) = ?°C

To answer that last question, we need a reliable way to multiply a positive number by a negative number. Today we find the rule — by looking at a pattern.

What we discover today:

1. Sign rules for × and ÷ — discovered by extending number patterns past zero, not stated as facts to memorise.

2. Effect of sign in repeated multiplication — why (−2)4 is positive but (−2)5 is negative: an even number of negatives always cancels to positive.

These ideas appear in finance, science, and programming wherever quantities can be positive or negative.

5Worked examples — establishing the sign rules from patterns

Use "Reveal next step" on each example. In teacher mode all steps show at once.

Example 1 — Positive × Negative: use a pattern to find 3 × (−5)
1
Build the pattern from known facts — decreasing the multiplier by 1 each row
Start where multiplication is straightforward and observe what happens to the product each time the multiplier drops by 1.
3 × 3=9} each row the product drops by 3 3 × 2=6 3 × 1=3 3 × 0=0 3 × (−1)=?← pattern continues below zero 3 × (−2)=? 3 × (−5)=?
2
Continue the pattern — the product keeps decreasing by 3
Since the product decreases by 3 with each step, the pattern must continue the same way past zero.
3 × 0=0 3 × (−1)=−30 − 3 = −3 3 × (−2)=−6−3 − 3 = −6 3 × (−3)=−9 3 × (−4)=−12 3 × (−5)=−15← our target
3
State the sign rule — positive × negative = negative
The pattern forces the result to be negative. The magnitude is simply 3 × 5 = 15; the sign rule determines the rest.
Sign Rule — Different signs Positive × Negative = Negative
3 × 5 = 15, so 3 × (−5) = −15
Answer 3 × (−5) = −15
Example 2 — Negative × Negative: extend the pattern to find (−3) × (−5)
1
Build the pattern using the result from Example 1
Now the first factor is −3. Use the sign rule from Example 1 to fill in the positive-multiplier rows, then observe what happens to the product each time.
(−3) × 3=−9} each row the product increases by 3 (−3) × 2=−6 (−3) × 1=−3 (−3) × 0=0 (−3) × (−1)=?← continues past zero (−3) × (−5)=?
2
Continue the pattern past zero — the product keeps increasing by 3
The product rises by 3 with every step. Crossing zero, the products must keep rising — now into positive territory.
(−3) × 0=0 (−3) × (−1)=30 + 3 = 3 (−3) × (−2)=63 + 3 = 6 (−3) × (−3)=9 (−3) × (−4)=12 (−3) × (−5)=15← our target
3
State the sign rule — negative × negative = positive
The pattern demands a positive result. The magnitude is 3 × 5 = 15; the sign rule handles the rest. Division follows the same sign rules as multiplication.
Sign Rule — Same signs Negative × Negative = Positive
3 × 5 = 15, so (−3) × (−5) = 15. Division: same rule.
Answer (−3) × (−5) = 15
Example 3 — Repeated multiplication: find (−2)5 and state the power rule
1
List the first few powers of −2 to find the pattern of signs
Build up the powers one factor at a time, applying the sign rule at each step.
(−2)1=−21 factor → negative (−2)2=4(−2)×(−2): same signs → positive (−2)3=−84×(−2): diff signs → negative (−2)4=16(−8)×(−2): same signs → positive (−2)5=?16×(−2): diff signs → ?
2
Compute (−2)5 — each extra factor of (−2) flips the sign
5 is odd, so after five sign flips there is one unmatched negative remaining. The sign rule settles it.
(−2)4 = +16
16 × (−2) = −32  (different signs → negative)
Therefore (−2)5 = −32
3
State the general rule for powers of negative numbers
An even exponent means every negative factor pairs with another to give positive. An odd exponent leaves one negative unpaired.
Powers of negative numbers negativeeven = positive  ·  negativeodd = negative
Special case: (−1)even = 1  ·  (−1)odd = −1
Answer (−2)5 = −32
