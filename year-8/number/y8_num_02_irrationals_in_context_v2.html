1Why this matters

Irrational numbers are everywhere in the real world — in paper, architecture, and art.

Every time you use A4 paper, you're holding an irrational number in your hands.

The A-series paper sizes (A0, A1, A2, A3, A4, A5 …) are designed so that folding any sheet in half gives you the next size down. This only works if the ratio of the longer side to the shorter side is always the same — and that ratio is exactly √2 ≈ 1.414…, which is irrational.

Today we explore the contexts in which irrational numbers arise naturally — from right-angled triangles to the golden ratio to ancient estimates of π.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. You'll use these five terms throughout today.

Perfect square
Tap to flip
A whole number that equals another whole number squared. Its square root is rational.
1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36… are perfect squares. √25 = 5 exactly.
Surd
Tap to flip
An irrational root — a square (or other) root that cannot be simplified to a whole number or fraction.
√2, √3, √5, √7 are surds. √4 = 2 is not a surd.
Exact value
Tap to flip
The true mathematical value, written symbolically rather than as a rounded decimal.
Side = √7 is exact. Side ≈ 2.646 is an approximation.
Approximation
Tap to flip
A rational number used to estimate an irrational value. Always involves some rounding error.
22/7 ≈ 3.143 approximates π ≈ 3.14159…
Golden ratio
Tap to flip
The number φ = (1 + √5) ÷ 2 ≈ 1.618… It is irrational and appears in art, architecture, and nature.
A rectangle with sides in ratio 1 : φ is called a golden rectangle.

5Worked examples

Three examples showing how irrational numbers arise in measurement contexts.

Example 1 A square tile has area 7 m². What is the side length? Is it rational or irrational?
1
Write the relationship between area and side length
For a square: area = side², so side = √(area).
side = √7
2
Check whether 7 is a perfect square
List perfect squares near 7: 2² = 4, 3² = 9. There is no whole number whose square equals 7.
2² = 4  <  7  <  9 = 3²
∴ 7 is not a perfect square
3
Conclude and express the exact value
Because 7 is not a perfect square, √7 cannot be written as a fraction — it is irrational. We leave the answer as √7 (exact) or use a decimal approximation if needed.
√7 ≈ 2.6457… (non-terminating, non-repeating)
Answer Side length = √7 m  (irrational — a surd)
Example 2 A right-angled triangle has legs of 1 m and 2 m. Find the hypotenuse.
1
Apply the Pythagorean theorem
Label the hypotenuse c. Pythagoras gives c² = a² + b².
c² = 1² + 2² = 1 + 4 = 5
2
Take the square root
The hypotenuse is the positive square root of 5.
c = √5
3
Check and classify
Is 5 a perfect square? 2² = 4 and 3² = 9 — so no. The hypotenuse √5 is a surd (irrational).
√5 ≈ 2.2361… (irrational)
Answer Hypotenuse = √5 m ≈ 2.24 m  (irrational)
Example 3 The golden ratio φ is defined as φ = (1 + √5) ÷ 2. Show that φ is irrational, and find its approximate decimal value.
1
Identify the irrational component
The expression contains √5. Since 5 is not a perfect square, √5 is irrational (a surd).
φ = 1 + √52   and   √5 is irrational
2
Apply the rule: irrational ± rational stays irrational
Adding 1 (rational) to √5 (irrational) gives an irrational sum. Dividing by 2 (rational) does not change irrationality. So φ is irrational.
1 + √5 → irrational
÷ 2 → still irrational
3
Calculate the approximate value
Using √5 ≈ 2.2361: φ = (1 + 2.2361) ÷ 2 = 3.2361 ÷ 2 ≈ 1.618. The decimal never terminates or repeats, confirming irrationality.
φ ≈ 1.6180339887… (non-terminating, non-repeating)
Answer φ = (1 + √5) ÷ 2 ≈ 1.618  (irrational)

6Together — diagonal of a 1 m × 1 m square

A square has side length 1 m. We want the exact length of its diagonal. Work through three steps with the class.

Setup: The diagonal cuts the square into two right-angled triangles. Each triangle has legs of 1 m and 1 m, and the diagonal is the hypotenuse.
💡 Why this works: whenever Pythagoras gives you c² = n and n is not a perfect square, the hypotenuse is a surd. The diagonal of any unit square is exactly √2 — an irrational number humans discovered 2500 years ago. The ancient Greeks found this deeply unsettling!
