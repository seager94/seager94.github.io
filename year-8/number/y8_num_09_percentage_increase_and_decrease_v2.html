1Why this matters

A real-world problem that is secretly today's maths.

You spot a pair of headphones with an original price of $240. The store has put them on a 15% off sale. You'd also like to know the price including GST (Australia's 10% Goods and Services Tax).

  • What do you actually pay after the discount?
  • What if GST is added to that discounted price?
  • What if the price rises 8% next year — how much then?

Each of these is a percentage change — and today you'll learn the fastest way to calculate any one of them in a single step.

The key idea: instead of calculating the change and adding (or subtracting) it separately, you multiply by a single decimal — the multiplier.

A 15% decrease → multiply by 0.85 (you keep 85% of the original).
A 10% increase → multiply by 1.10 (you have 110% of the original).

Two operations become one. That's the multiplier method.

5aWorked example — discount

A jacket has an original price of $180. It is on sale with a 35% discount. Find the sale price using the multiplier method.

1
Identify the direction and write the multiplier
A discount is a decrease. We keep (100% − 35%) = 65% of the original.
Decrease of 35% → keep 65% → multiplier = 0.65
2
Multiply the original price by the multiplier
One calculation. No intermediate steps needed.
$180 × 0.65 = $117
3
Interpret and verify
$117 is less than $180 — consistent with a decrease. Check: 35% of $180 = $63; $180 − $63 = $117. ✓
The sale price is $117. The saving is $63 (35% of the original).
Sale price $180 × 0.65 = $117

5bWorked example — mark-up and GST

A retailer buys a bicycle for $320 (cost price). They add a 25% mark-up, then add GST (10%) to the selling price. Find the final customer price.

1
Apply the mark-up multiplier
A 25% increase → keep 125% → multiplier = 1.25.
Cost price: $320
$320 × 1.25 = $400 (selling price before GST)
2
Apply the GST multiplier
GST is a 10% increase → multiplier = 1.10.
$400 × 1.10 = $440 (price including GST)
3
Interpret and model the chain of multipliers
Both changes can be combined: $320 × 1.25 × 1.10. This is a key idea for multi-period change.
$320 × 1.25 × 1.10 = $320 × 1.375 = $440 ✓
Final customer price $320 → mark-up → $400 → GST → $440

5cWorked example — multi-period percentage change

A town's population was 12 000 at the start of Year 1. It grew by 5% in Year 1, then by 8% in Year 2, then decreased by 3% in Year 3. Find the population after three years.

1
Write a multiplier for each period
Each percentage change gets its own multiplier. A decrease gives a multiplier less than 1.
Year 1: +5% → × 1.05
Year 2: +8% → × 1.08
Year 3: −3% → × 0.97
2
Multiply all three multipliers together, then apply to the original
The combined multiplier is the product of the three individual ones.
Combined multiplier: 1.05 × 1.08 × 0.97 = 1.10124
Population = 12 000 × 1.10124 ≈ 13 215
3
Interpret: what was the overall percentage change?
The combined multiplier tells us the overall change. 1.10124 means an overall increase of about 10.1%.
Overall change: (1.10124 − 1) × 100 ≈ +10.1%
Note: this is not simply 5 + 8 − 3 = 10%, because percentages compound.
Population after 3 years 12 000 × 1.05 × 1.08 × 0.97 ≈ 13 215
