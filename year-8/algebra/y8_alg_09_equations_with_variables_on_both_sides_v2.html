1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's algebra.

Two streaming services offer different pricing:

  • StreamBlue costs a $6 monthly fee plus $4 per movie rented.
  • StreamRed costs a $18 monthly fee plus $1 per movie rented.

Your friend asks: "For how many movies are the two services the same price?"

To answer this, you need to find where the two costs meet — an equation with the variable on both sides.

Setting the two costs equal:

6 + 4m = 18 + m

Notice the variable m appears on both sides of the equation. Today you will learn to collect all the variable terms onto one side and all the constants onto the other — then solve as usual.

Solution: 3m = 12, so m = 4 movies. At 4 movies, both services cost $22.

5Worked examples — the collect-and-solve method

Every equation with variables on both sides follows the same routine: collect variable terms on one side, collect constants on the other, then solve. Verify by substitution and connect to a graphical picture.

Example A — solve 5x + 3 = 2x + 15
1
Collect variable terms on the left — subtract 2x from both sides
We remove 2x from the right side so all variable terms are on the left.
5x + 3 = 2x + 15
− 2x on both sides:   3x + 3 = 15
2
Collect constants on the right — subtract 3 from both sides
We remove the constant from the left so all numbers are on the right.
3x + 3 = 15
− 3 on both sides:   3x = 12
÷ 3 on both sides:   x = 4
3
Verify by substitution and visualise graphically
Substitute x = 4 into the original equation. Both sides must be equal. Then see what this looks like on the Cartesian plane.
LHS:   5(4) + 3 = 20 + 3 = 23
RHS:   2(4) + 15 = 8 + 15 = 23 ✓
The graph below shows y = 5x + 3 (coral) and y = 2x + 15 (navy). They cross at the point (4, 23) — that is the solution.
Solutionx = 4  (intersection of the two lines at the point (4, 23))
Example B — solve 3x + 7 = 6x − 8  (variable terms collected to the right)
1
Collect variable terms on the right — subtract 3x from both sides
Here it is tidier to move x-terms to the right. We subtract 3x from both sides so the variable term is on the right side only.
3x + 7 = 6x − 8
− 3x on both sides:   7 = 3x − 8
2
Collect constants on the left — add 8 to both sides
Add 8 to both sides to move the constant to the left, leaving the variable term alone on the right.
7 = 3x − 8
+ 8 on both sides:   15 = 3x
÷ 3 on both sides:   5 = x,   i.e. x = 5
3
Verify by substituting x = 5 into the original equation
Both sides of the original equation must give the same value.
LHS:   3(5) + 7 = 15 + 7 = 22
RHS:   6(5) − 8 = 30 − 8 = 22 ✓
Graphically: the lines y = 3x + 7 and y = 6x − 8 intersect at the point (5, 22).
Solutionx = 5
Example C — solve 7x − 4 = 3x + 20  (larger coefficients, graphical focus)
1
Collect variable terms on the left — subtract 3x from both sides
Subtract 3x from both sides to group all x-terms on the left.
7x − 4 = 3x + 20
− 3x on both sides:   4x − 4 = 20
2
Collect constants on the right — add 4 to both sides
Add 4 to both sides to leave the variable term alone on the left.
4x − 4 = 20
+ 4 on both sides:   4x = 24
÷ 4 on both sides:   x = 6
3
Verify by substitution and interpret the graphical picture
Substitute x = 6 into both sides of the original equation.
LHS:   7(6) − 4 = 42 − 4 = 38
RHS:   3(6) + 20 = 18 + 20 = 38 ✓
Graphically: the line y = 7x − 4 is steeper (slope 7) and starts below; the line y = 3x + 20 starts higher (y-intercept 20) but grows slower (slope 3). They cross exactly at x = 6 where both equal 38. The solution to the equation is the x-coordinate of the intersection point.
Solutionx = 6  (intersection of the lines at the point (6, 38))
