1Why this matters

A real situation that is secretly today's algebra.

You've been offered a part-time job at a local café paying $24 per hour. Before you say yes, you want to know:

  • How much do I earn for a 4-hour shift?
  • How many hours until I've earned $192?
  • Is casual work better than a fixed salary?

A linear equation can answer all of these in seconds — once you know the rule.

The maths behind it

At $24/hr, the income rule is I = 24h.

Every extra hour adds the same amount — $24. That constant rate of increase is what makes this a linear relationship. Today we model these pay-rate situations, including overtime, and compare casual pay with salaried pay to find the break-even point.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. These five terms will be used throughout the lesson.

Hourly rate
Tap to flip
The amount of money earned for each hour of work. It is the rate of change in a pay-rate model.
$22/hr means you earn $22 more for every extra hour worked.
Linear function
Tap to flip
A rule where the output increases by equal amounts for each unit increase in the input. Its graph is a straight line.
I = 25h — for every +1 hour, income goes up by $25.
Overtime
Tap to flip
Hours worked beyond a standard threshold (often 8 hrs/day), paid at a higher rate — usually 1.5× the standard rate.
Standard: $20/hr. Overtime: $30/hr (= 1.5 × 20).
Break-even point
Tap to flip
The value of the input where two different pay rules give the same income. Found by setting the two rules equal and solving.
28h = 560 → h = 20 hrs is the break-even.
Rate of change
Tap to flip
How much the output changes per unit increase in the input. In pay models, it equals the hourly rate. It is the gradient (slope) of the graph.
I = 18h → rate of change = $18/hr.

💡 A pay-rate relationship is linear when every extra hour earns exactly the same amount. If the first differences are all equal, the relationship is linear — and its graph is a straight line.

Maya earns $25 per hour as a checkout operator. Write the income rule, complete a table of values for h = 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, and plot the relationship on a graph. Interpret what the gradient represents.

Plot (0, 0), (2, 50), (4, 100), (6, 150), (8, 200). All five points lie on one straight line passing through the origin. The gradient equals $25/hr — the hourly rate.
Conclusion Rule: I = 25h — gradient = $25/hr (hourly rate) — all 5 points lie on the straight line. ✓
Example 2 of 3 — Overtime pay
Jordan earns $22/hr for his first 8 standard hours each day. Any additional hours are paid at overtime rate: $33/hr (1.5 × standard). On Saturday he works 10 hours. Calculate his total income.
1
Calculate standard pay (first 8 hours)
Standard hours = 8. Apply the standard rate of $22/hr.
Standard pay = 8 × $22 = $176
2
Calculate overtime pay (hours beyond 8)
Overtime hours = 10 − 8 = 2. Overtime rate = 1.5 × $22 = $33/hr.
Overtime hours = 10 − 8 = 2 hours
Overtime pay = 2 × $33 = $66
3
Add to find total income and write the rule
Total = standard pay + overtime pay. For any number of hours h > 8, the rule is I = 176 + 33(h − 8).
Total = $176 + $66 = $242

General rule for h > 8:
I = 8 × 22 + (h − 8) × 33 = 176 + 33h − 264 = 33h − 88

Check: h = 10 → I = 33(10) − 88 = 330 − 88 = 242 ✓
Answer Jordan earns $242 for 10 hours (8 standard + 2 overtime).
Example 3 of 3 — Comparing casual vs salary
A café offers two pay structures: Casual at $28/hr, or a fixed weekly salary of $560. For how many hours per week does the salary become more favourable? Represent both options on a graph and interpret the intersection.
1
Write both rules and build a comparison table
Casual income: IC = 28h. Salary income: IS = 560 (constant, regardless of hours).
h (hours/wk)0510152025
IC = 28h0140280420560700
IS = 560560560560560560560
2
Find the break-even point algebraically
Set the two rules equal and solve for h.
Set equal: 28h = 560
Divide both sides by 28: h = 20

At h = 20: both options pay $560/week.
If h < 20 → Salary pays more.   If h > 20 → Casual pays more.
3
Graph both rules and identify the intersection
The casual line I = 28h starts at the origin and rises steeply. The salary line I = 560 is horizontal. They intersect at (20, 560) — the break-even point.
Conclusion Break-even at h = 20 hrs. Fewer than 20 hrs → salary pays more. More than 20 hrs → casual pays more.
