1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's algebra.

A mobile phone plan charges $p per month for data and $8 per month for calls. A student signs up for 5 months.

The total cost is:

5(p + 8) dollars

But the phone company needs to bill the data component and the calls component separately on the invoice.

Expanding brackets solves this. By applying the distributive property:

5(p + 8) = 5p + 40
Now the two components are visible: $5p for data and $40 for calls. Today you'll learn to do this for any bracket expression.

Bracket
Tap to flip
A symbol ( ) that groups terms to be treated together.
In 3(x + 4): the bracket groups x + 4 so that 3 multiplies the whole sum.
Expand
Tap to flip
To remove brackets by multiplying out — writing an expression without brackets.
Expanding 3(x + 4) gives 3x + 12.
Distributive Property
Tap to flip
Multiplying a factor by a sum equals the sum of the factor times each term.
a(b + c) = ab + ac
The factor distributes across the bracket.
Factor
Tap to flip
The multiplier placed outside the bracket.
In −2(x + 5): the factor is −2. Notice it can be negative.
Equivalent Expressions
Tap to flip
Two expressions that give the same value for every value of the variable.
3(a + 4) and 3a + 12 are equivalent — substitute any value to check.

5Worked examples — the distributive property

Three examples showing the method. Work through each one before moving on.

Example A  Expand 3(a + 4)

1
Draw the area model
Split the bracket into its two terms and set up a rectangle. The factor 3 is the height; the two parts of the bracket form the width columns.
a + 4
3 3 × a 3 × 4
Each cell shows one multiplication.
2
Calculate each product
Evaluate the contents of each cell in the area model.
3 × a = 3a
3 × 4  = 12
3
Write the expanded expression and name the property
Join the two products with the correct operation sign.
3(a + 4) = 3a + 12
Distributive Property a(b + c) = ab + ac

2
Multiply the factor by each term — the sign stays
Collect the tiles: three a-tiles give 3a; three −b-tiles give −3b. The subtraction sign belongs to the b-term, not the bracket.
3 × a = 3a
3 × (−b) = −3b
3
Write the result and verify
Let a = 4, b = 1. LHS: 3(4 − 1) = 3 × 3 = 9. RHS: 3(4) − 3(1) = 12 − 3 = 9. ✓
3(a − b) = 3a − 3b
Distributive Property — subtraction form a(b − c) = ab − ac

Example C  Expand −2(x + 5)  — negative factor

1
Identify the key challenge: the factor is negative
When the factor is negative, every product will be negative. This is the sign rule: negative × positive = negative.
Factor: −2  (negative)
Terms inside brackets: +x and +5  (both positive)

Sign rule:  − × + = −
2
Apply the distributive property to each term
Multiply −2 by x, then multiply −2 by 5. Keep careful track of the signs.
(−2) × x = −2x   (negative × positive = negative ✓)
(−2) × 5  = −10     (negative × positive = negative ✓)
3
Write the expanded form and verify
Let x = 3. LHS: −2(3 + 5) = −2 × 8 = −16. RHS: −2(3) − 10 = −6 − 10 = −16. ✓
−2(x + 5) = −2x − 10
Key rule — negative factor When the factor is negative, all result terms are negative
