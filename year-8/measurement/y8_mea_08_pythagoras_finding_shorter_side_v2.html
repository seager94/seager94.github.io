1Why this matters

A situation where we know the longest side — and need to find a shorter one.

A 6 m ladder leans against a vertical wall. The base sits 2 m from the wall along the ground. How high up the wall does the ladder reach?

The ladder is the hypotenuse — the longest side, and we already know it. The unknown is the shorter vertical side (how high up the wall).

We cannot just add sides together. We need to rearrange Pythagoras' theorem — from a² + b² = c² to b² = c² − a². The key step is subtraction, not addition. By the end of this lesson, you'll solve this in three steps.

The ladder is the hypotenuse. The height up the wall is the unknown shorter side.

2Key vocabulary

Rearrangement
Tap to flip
Changing the subject of a formula by applying inverse operations to both sides.
From a² + b² = c², subtract a² from both sides to get b² = c² − a².
Subject of a formula
Tap to flip
The variable expressed on its own on one side of the equation.
In b² = c² − a², the subject is b². We then take the square root to find b.
Pythagorean triple
Tap to flip
Three whole numbers that exactly satisfy a² + b² = c².
(3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13), (7, 24, 25), (8, 15, 17) are famous triples. Multiples also work: (6, 8, 10).
Acute triangle
Tap to flip
A triangle where all angles are less than 90°.
If a² + b² > c², the triangle is acute — the sum exceeds the hypotenuse square.
Obtuse triangle
Tap to flip
A triangle with one angle greater than 90°.
If a² + b² < c², the triangle is obtuse. Compare to classify any triangle.

5How to find a shorter side — rearrange and subtract

Three reveal steps cover the rearrangement, two worked examples with labelled triangles, a practical application, and Pythagorean triples.

1
Recap and rearrangement
From last lesson: a² + b² = c². If c and a are known, subtract a² from both sides to isolate b².
Start:   a² + b² = c²
Subtract a²:   b² = c² − a²
Take √:        b = √(c² − a²)
⚠ Central misconception — add-instead-of-subtract error

When finding a shorter side, students mistakenly add the squares instead of subtracting — writing b² = a² + c². This is wrong. When the hypotenuse is already known, you must subtract the known leg's square from the hypotenuse's square. Notice also that adding gives a result bigger than the hypotenuse — impossible for a shorter side.

2
Example A — hypotenuse 13, one leg 5, find the other leg
Identify: c = 13, a = 5, find b
Write:   a² + b² = c²
Sub:     5² + b² = 13²
Square:  25 + b² = 169
Subtract:  b² = 169 − 25 = 144
Root:     b = √144 = 12
Answer — shorter sideb = 12  (This is the 5-12-13 Pythagorean triple.)
⚠ Common error — do NOT do this

A student writes: b² = 5² + 13² = 25 + 169 = 194, so b = √194 ≈ 13.9. This is the add-instead-of-subtract error. Notice too that 13.9 > 13 (bigger than the hypotenuse) — a shorter side can never exceed the hypotenuse.

3
Example B — hypotenuse 10, leg 6  ·  Practical application  ·  Pythagorean triples

Example B — hypotenuse 10, one leg 6

a² + b² = c²
6² + b² = 10²
36 + b² = 100
b² = 100 − 36 = 64
b = √64 = 8

Practical application — horizontal distance of a leaning ladder

A 6.5 m ladder leans against a wall and reaches 6 m up the wall. How far is the base of the ladder from the wall?

c = 6.5, b = 6, find a
a² + b² = c²
a² + 36 = 42.25
a² = 42.25 − 36 = 6.25
a = √6.25 = 2.5 m

Pythagorean triples — recognise and verify

These sets of whole numbers exactly satisfy a² + b² = c². Recognising them saves calculation time.

Triple (a, b, c) Check: a² + b² = c²?
(3, 4, 5)9 + 16 = 25✓ = 5²
(5, 12, 13)25 + 144 = 169✓ = 13²
(7, 24, 25)49 + 576 = 625✓ = 25²
(8, 15, 17)64 + 225 = 289✓ = 17²

Any whole-number multiple of a triple is also a triple — e.g. (6, 8, 10) is 2 × (3, 4, 5). When you see numbers from a triple, you can read off the missing side without a calculator.

6Spot the mistake — error analysis

Read the student's working carefully. The add-instead-of-subtract error appears in one of lines 1–4. Identify it, then check the correction.

⚠ Named misconception: add-instead-of-subtract error

When finding a shorter side, a student mistakenly adds the squares instead of subtracting. The rearrangement b² = c² − a² requires subtraction — the student's sign error cascades through every subsequent line.

Find the unknown leg when hypotenuse c = 17 and one leg a = 8.
1 a² + b² = c² ✓
2 b² = 8² + 17²  [student wrote + instead of −] ✗
3 b² = 64 + 289 = 353
4 b = √353 ≈ 18.8
Show the correction ↓

Line 2 is the faulty line. The add-instead-of-subtract error: the hypotenuse (17) is already known, so we must subtract not add:

b² = 17² − 8² = 289 − 64 = 225
b = √225 = 15

Sense-check: 18.8 > 17 (bigger than the hypotenuse) — this is impossible for a shorter side and should trigger immediate suspicion.

Now work through this one correctly, step by step. Find the unknown shorter side when c = 15 and a = 9.
