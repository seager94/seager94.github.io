1Why this matters

A real situation — and where the maths shows up.

You're booking a flight from Adelaide to Perth. The airline website shows departure at 22:45 and arrival at 01:10. Your friend says, "Wait — that lands before it takes off?"

They've forgotten that 01:10 is the next day. The flight crosses midnight. Without understanding 24-hour time and how to handle a duration that crosses midnight, you'd mis-read your own boarding pass.

Bus timetables, shift rosters, cinema session times, sporting schedules — every one uses time notation and requires calculating how long something lasts. That's today's lesson.

The two big ideas today:
  • 24-hour time removes the ambiguity of am/pm — there's only one 13:30, never a morning and evening version of the same digits.
  • Duration is the length of time between start and finish. The trap is treating time like a decimal number — but 60 minutes make an hour, not 100.

5Worked examples — four example types

Use the Reveal button on each example to step through the solution.

⚠ Two common errors to name and avoid:
  1. Treating an hour as 100 minutes. If you write 11:45 − 09:20 as if it were 11.45 − 09.20 = 2.25, you get "2 hours 25 minutes" — but that's only correct by coincidence here. Try 11:15 − 09:45: as decimals you'd write 1.30, suggesting 1 hour 30 minutes, but the real answer is 1 hour 30 minutes. Try 12:10 − 10:50 the decimal way: 12.10 − 10.50 = 1.60 — and "1 hour 60 minutes" is wrong (it's 1 hour 20 minutes). Always work with hours and minutes separately.
  2. Mishandling a duration that crosses midnight. Subtracting 23:00 from 01:30 gives a negative number. Instead, use the bridge strategy: count forward from the start time to midnight (00:00), then count from midnight to the finish time, and add the two spans.

Example A — Convert 12-hour time to 24-hour time

Convert 7:35 pm to 24-hour time.

1
Identify: pm → add 12
7:35 pm is afternoon/evening. The pm rule: add 12 to the hours.
pm → add 12 to the hour number
2
Add 12 to the hour; minutes stay the same
7 + 12 = 19. Minutes stay at 35.
7 + 12 = 19  →  19:35
3
Write the final answer
24-hour times use four digits with a colon, or sometimes written without (1935).
Answer7:35 pm = 19:35

Example B — Convert 24-hour time to 12-hour time

Convert 08:45 to 12-hour time.

1
Is the hour less than 12?
Hour 08 is less than 12 → this is an am time. Remove the leading zero.
Hour < 12 → am.  08 → remove leading zero → 8
2
Write with am; minutes unchanged
Minutes stay at 45.
Answer08:45 = 8:45 am

Example C — Duration within the same day

A bus departs at 09:20 and arrives at 11:45. How long is the journey?

1
Bridge to the next whole hour
From 09:20 to 10:00: how many minutes?
60 − 20 = 40 minutes to reach 10:00
2
Count from 10:00 to 11:45
From the whole hour to the finish: 1 hour and 45 minutes.
10:00 → 11:45 = 1 h 45 min
3
Add the two spans
40 min + 1 h 45 min. Combine the minutes: 40 + 45 = 85 min = 1 h 25 min. Carry the 1 hour: 1 + 1 = 2 hours total.
40 min + 1 h 45 min = 2 h 25 min
AnswerJourney time = 2 hours 25 minutes

Example D — Duration crossing midnight

A nurse starts a night shift at 22:30 and finishes at 06:15 the next morning. How long is the shift?

1
Count from 22:30 to midnight (00:00)
22:30 to 23:00 = 30 min. 23:00 to 00:00 = 1 h. Total to midnight = 1 h 30 min.
22:30 → 00:00 = 1 h 30 min
2
Count from midnight to 06:15
00:00 to 06:15 = 6 hours and 15 minutes.
00:00 → 06:15 = 6 h 15 min
3
Add the two spans
1 h 30 min + 6 h 15 min: hours 1 + 6 = 7; minutes 30 + 15 = 45. No carry needed.
1 h 30 min + 6 h 15 min = 7 h 45 min
AnswerShift length = 7 hours 45 minutes
