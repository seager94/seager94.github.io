1Why this matters

Real situations where the volume of a prism drives decisions.

A builder is pouring a concrete footing for a retaining wall. The footing has an L-shaped cross-section and runs for 12 metres. To order the right amount of concrete, the builder must calculate the exact volume — too little and the job stalls; too much wastes money.

A juice company is switching from square cartons to triangular-prism-shaped bottles. They need to know which holds more liquid at the same packaging cost.

Both problems reduce to the same question: how much 3D space does this shape occupy?

Every right prism has a constant cross-section — the same shape all the way through.

Volume = area of that cross-section × length of the prism.

Today you will apply this single formula to four different cross-section shapes, including composite ones.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip it and reveal the definition. You will use all five terms today.

Right prism
Tap to flip
A 3D solid with two identical parallel faces (the cross-sections) joined by rectangular lateral faces perpendicular to them.
A wooden plank, a Toblerone box, a concrete beam — all right prisms.
Cross-section
Tap to flip
The face you see when you slice a prism perpendicular to its length. The same shape appears all the way through.
Cutting a triangular prism through its middle reveals a triangle — that is the cross-section.
Volume
Tap to flip
The amount of 3D space a solid occupies, measured in cubic units (cm³, m³, mm³).
A box 10 cm × 5 cm × 3 cm has volume 150 cm³ — it fits exactly 150 unit cubes.
Length
Tap to flip
The depth of a prism — how far the cross-section is extruded into 3D. Sometimes called depth or height depending on orientation.
A triangular prism lying on its side: the cross-section is the triangle; the length is how far the prism extends.
Composite shape
Tap to flip
A shape built from two or more simpler shapes joined together. Its area = sum of the parts (or larger minus the removed part).
An L-shape = two rectangles joined. Calculate each rectangle’s area, then add.

5Worked examples

Four prism types, each with the cross-section shaded in the diagram. Click Reveal next step to see the working unfold.

Key formula — works for every right prism
V = area of cross-section × length
Find the area of the shaded end-face first, then multiply by the length of the prism.

Example A — Rectangular prism

A rectangular prism is 8 cm long, 5 cm wide and 3 cm tall. Find its volume.

1
Identify the cross-section and find its area
The shaded face is a rectangle, 5 cm wide and 3 cm tall.
A = 5 × 3 = 15 cm²
2
Multiply by the length
The prism extends 8 cm. Apply the formula.
V = A × l
= 15 × 8
= 120 cm³
3
Note: the same as length × width × height
For rectangular prisms the cross-section is always a rectangle, so V = l × w × h. The prism formula and the box formula are identical.
Volume of rectangular prismV = 120 cm³

Example B — Triangular prism

A triangular prism has a right-angled triangular cross-section with base 6 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm. The prism is 10 cm long. Find its volume.

1
Find the area of the triangular cross-section
Use A = ½ × base × height for a triangle.
A = 12 × 6 × 4 = 12 cm²
2
Multiply by the length
The prism is 10 cm long.
V = 12 × 10 = 120 cm³
3
Key reminder
Always use the perpendicular height of the triangle — the height that forms a right angle with the base. Never use a slanted side.
Volume of triangular prismV = 120 cm³

Example C — Trapezoidal prism

The cross-section is a trapezium with parallel sides 5 cm and 9 cm, and perpendicular height 4 cm. The prism is 12 cm long.

1
Find the area of the trapezoidal cross-section
Use A = ½ × (a + b) × h, where a and b are the two parallel sides.
A = 12 × (5 + 9) × 4 = 12 × 14 × 4 = 28 cm²
2
Multiply by the length
The prism is 12 cm long.
V = 28 × 12 = 336 cm³
3
Check your trapezium formula
Add the two parallel sides (not the slant sides) and use the perpendicular height between them. Label the diagram before substituting.
Volume of trapezoidal prismV = 336 cm³

Example D — Prism with an L-shaped cross-section

The cross-section is an L-shape. Outer dimensions: 8 cm wide, 6 cm tall. A rectangular notch 5 cm wide × 4 cm tall is removed from the top-right. The prism is 15 cm long.

⚠ Strategy — split or subtract
The L-shape is not a standard formula shape. Either split it into two rectangles and add, or calculate the full outer rectangle minus the removed notch. Both methods give the same answer.
1
Find the area of the L-shaped cross-section
Method: large outer rectangle minus the missing notch.
Outer rectangle: 8 × 6 = 48 cm²
Notch removed: 5 × 4 = 20 cm²
A = 48 − 20 = 28 cm²
2
Multiply by the length
The prism is 15 cm long.
V = 28 × 15 = 420 cm³
3
Check via alternative split
Left strip: 3 × 6 = 18 cm². Right bottom strip: 5 × 2 = 10 cm². Total = 28 cm². ✓ Both methods agree.
Volume of L-shaped prismV = 420 cm³
