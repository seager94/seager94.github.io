1Why this matters

A real situation that secretly uses today's maths.

Mia works a casual job at the school canteen. She earns $19.50 for every hour she works. Her friend Zac works at a petrol station earning $117 for a 6-hour shift.

Who earns more? You can't just compare $19.50 and $117 — the amounts are for different lengths of time.

To compare fairly, you need to find how much each person earns per hour — a rate. Today you'll learn exactly how to do that, and how the same rate can be seen four different ways.

The solution uses a rate:

Mia: $19.50 per hour (already a rate).

Zac: $117 ÷ 6 = $19.50 per hour.

Equal pay rates — same deal! A rate always compares two quantities with different units (here: dollars and hours).

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. These five terms are the language of rates.

Rate
Tap to flip
A comparison of two quantities measured in different units, written as one per the other. Speed: 60 km/h compares kilometres and hours.
Ratio
Tap to flip
A comparison of two quantities in the same unit, written as a : b. 2 red : 3 blue marbles. Both quantities are marbles — no units needed.
Unit rate
Tap to flip
A rate where the second quantity equals 1 — the amount per one unit. $4.20 per kg means $4.20 for exactly 1 kg.
Per
Tap to flip
Means "for each" or "for every one." The slash / in a rate is read as "per." 80 km/h reads as 80 kilometres per hour.
Best buy
Tap to flip
The option with the lowest cost per unit — found by dividing price by quantity for each option, then comparing. $2.40 for 600 g vs $3.50 for 1 kg: compare both as $/kg.

5Worked example — which is better value?

Problem: Brand A sells 400 g of muesli for $3.20. Brand B sells 750 g for $5.25. Which is the better buy?

1
Identify the rate you need
To compare fairly, both prices must be per the same quantity. We'll use cost per gram ($/g).
Rate needed: cost per gram ($/g)
Brand A: 400 g for $3.20  →  $/g = $3.20 ÷ 400
Brand B: 750 g for $5.25  →  $/g = $5.25 ÷ 750
2
Calculate each unit rate
Divide price by quantity for each brand.
Brand A: $3.20 ÷ 400 g = $0.0080 per gram
Brand B: $5.25 ÷ 750 g = $0.0070 per gram
3
Compare and conclude
Lower cost per gram = better value.
Brand B ($0.0070/g) < Brand A ($0.0080/g)
∴ Brand B is the better buy — it is $0.001 cheaper per gram.
Answer Brand B is better value: $0.007/g vs Brand A's $0.008/g.
