1Why this matters

A real situation where knowing two sides of a right angle lets you find the third — and it has been used for over 2 500 years.

A builder needs to check that the corner of a room is a perfect right angle. She measures 3 m along the floor from the corner and marks that point. She measures 4 m up the wall and marks that point. Then she stretches a tape measure diagonally between the two marks.

If the diagonal is exactly 5 m, the corner is a perfect right angle and the room is square. If it is not 5 m, the walls are out of square — a problem that will affect every wall, floor tile, and window frame.

How did she know to expect 5 m? She used Pythagoras' theorem — the relationship between the three sides of any right-angled triangle. Today you will discover why it is true and how to use it to find the longest side, the hypotenuse.

The diagonal must be exactly 5 m for the corner to be a perfect right angle.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip it and reveal the definition. These five terms are the language of today's lesson.

Right-angled triangle
Tap to flip
A triangle with one angle equal to 90°, marked with a small square in the corner. The other two angles are both acute.
A set square is a right-angled triangle.
Hypotenuse
Tap to flip
The longest side of a right-angled triangle — always opposite the right angle. Always labelled c in Pythagoras' theorem.
In a 3-4-5 triangle, the hypotenuse is 5.
Legs (shorter sides)
Tap to flip
The two shorter sides that meet at the right angle — labelled a and b in Pythagoras' theorem.
In a 3-4-5 triangle the legs are 3 and 4.
Square of a number
Tap to flip
A number multiplied by itself, written with the exponent ². Geometrically it gives the area of a square with that side length.
3² = 3 × 3 = 9. A square of side 3 has area 9.
Square root (√)
Tap to flip
The inverse of squaring: √(c²) = c. We take the square root as the final step when finding a side length from its square.
√25 = 5 because 5² = 25.

5Why does a² + b² = c²? — The geometric proof

Six steps: the square-dissection proof on a 3-4-5 triangle, then the general theorem. Click to reveal each step.

A 3-4-5 right-angled triangle with squares on each side. Each square's area equals the side length squared: 9 + 16 = 25.
1
A right-angled triangle
Start with a 3-4-5 right-angled triangle. The right angle is at the bottom-left corner, marked with a small square. The two shorter sides — the legs — are a = 4 and b = 3. The side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse, c = 5.
Legs: a = 4 and b = 3  ·  Hypotenuse: c = 5
2
Draw a square on each leg
Draw a square on the vertical leg (a = 4) — it has area 4² = 16 cm². Draw a square on the horizontal leg (b = 3) — it has area 3² = 9 cm². The area of each square equals the side length squared.
Square on a: area = 4² = 16 cm²    Square on b: area = 3² = 9 cm²
3
Draw a square on the hypotenuse
Draw a square on the hypotenuse c = 5. This tilted square has area 5² = 25 cm². It is shown in green in the diagram.
Square on c: area = 5² = 25 cm²
4
The dissection result — 9 + 16 = 25
The two smaller squares together exactly equal the large square on the hypotenuse. The area of the terracotta square (16) plus the area of the blue square (9) equals the area of the green square (25). Not approximately — exactly.
16 + 9 = 25   ⟹   4² + 3² = 5²
This is not a coincidence — it is true for every right-angled triangle.
5
Generalise — Pythagoras' Theorem
Replace 3, 4, 5 with any two legs a and b and any hypotenuse c. The relationship holds for every right-angled triangle:
Pythagoras' Theorem a² + b² = c²
The square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares on the two shorter legs.
In words: square each leg, add those squares, then take the square root — that gives you the hypotenuse.
6
The five-step procedure for finding the hypotenuse
Use this routine every time. Here it is applied to a triangle with legs 3 and 4.
Step 1 — Identify: c is opposite the right angle
Step 2 — Write: c² = a² + b²
Step 3 — Substitute: c² = 3² + 4²
Step 4 — Square: c² = 9 + 16
Step 5 — Sum then √: c² = 25 ⟹ c = √25 = 5
AnswerHypotenuse c = 5
