1Why this matters

A real situation that secretly uses today's algebra.

A doctor prescribes medication with a half-life of 1 hour — each hour, exactly half the dose remains in the body.

Starting from 8 mg at the beginning:

  • After 1 hr: 4 mg
  • After 2 hrs: 2 mg
  • After 3 hrs: 1 mg
  • After 4 hrs: 0.5 mg
  • After 5 hrs: 0.25 mg

Each step divides by 2. How do we express this pattern using index notation?

The maths behind it:

8 = 23
4 = 22
2 = 21
1 = 2? ← index must be zero!
0.5 = 2? ← index must be negative!

Today we discover what 20, 2−1, and 2−2 must equal — and why.

5The zero and negative index laws

Vocabulary, the descending-powers pattern, three worked examples, and two misconceptions to watch for.

Key vocabulary — tap each card to flip
Index / Exponent
Tap to flip
The small raised number that tells you how many times the base is multiplied by itself.
In 23: the index is 3. Also called the exponent or power.
Base
Tap to flip
The number or variable being raised to a power — written at full size, before the index.
In 53: the base is 5. In x4: the base is x.
Zero index
Tap to flip
Any non-zero base to the power 0 equals 1.
50 = 1   1000 = 1   x0 = 1 (provided x ≠ 0)
Negative index
Tap to flip
A negative index means take the reciprocal — flip the base into the denominator.
a−n = 1an   Result is never negative!
Reciprocal
Tap to flip
The reciprocal of a number is 1 divided by that number. Multiplying a number by its reciprocal gives 1.
Reciprocal of 5 is 15. Reciprocal of 23 is 32.
The descending-powers pattern — base 2

Each row, the index decreases by 1. Notice what happens to the value — click to reveal one row at a time.

ExpressionMeaningValuePattern
23 2 × 2 × 2 8 Starting point
22 2 × 2 4 8 ÷ 2 = 4
21 2 2 4 ÷ 2 = 2
20 pattern forces this… 1 2 ÷ 2 = 1 ← must be 1!
2−1 12 12 1 ÷ 2 = 12
2−2 122 = 14 14 12 ÷ 2 = 14
⭐ Key insight: The halving pattern forces 20 = 1. If 21 = 2, the next step must be 2 ÷ 2 = 1. There is no other consistent answer.
The two laws this pattern gives us:
Zero index: a0 = 1 (for any a ≠ 0)
Negative index: a−n = 1an (for any a ≠ 0)

Example C — Simplify x0
1
Apply the zero index law
The base is the variable x. The zero index law applies to any non-zero base — including variables.
x0 = 1  (provided x ≠ 0)
2
Why does this work?
Using index division laws: x³ ÷ x³ = x^(3−3) = x⁰. But x³ ÷ x³ also equals 1 (any number divided by itself). So x⁰ = 1.
Answerx0 = 1   (for x ≠ 0)
Two misconceptions to watch for
⚠ Misconception 1 — zero index
✗ Wrong x0 = 0   "The index is zero, so the answer must be zero."
✓ Correct x0 = 1   The descending-powers pattern forces this. 2⁰ must equal 1 because it sits between 2¹ = 2 and 2⁻¹ = ½, each step dividing by 2: 2 ÷ 2 = 1, not 0.
⚠ Misconception 2 — negative index
✗ Wrong 5−3 = −125 or 2−3 = −8   "A negative index makes the answer negative."
✓ Correct A negative index means reciprocal, not negative. 5−3 = 1125 — a small positive number. The minus sign moves the base to the denominator; it does not make the result negative.
