1Notice and Wonder — a pattern in every circle

Three circles of very different sizes have been carefully measured. Look at the numbers and discuss with a partner: what do you notice? What do you wonder?

Circle A — small
Diameter: 6 cm
Circumference: 18.85 cm
C ÷ d ≈ 3.14
Circle B — medium
Diameter: 10 cm
Circumference: 31.42 cm
C ÷ d ≈ 3.14
Circle C — large
Diameter: 25 cm
Circumference: 78.54 cm
C ÷ d ≈ 3.14
The big idea: For every circle — no matter the size — the ratio C ÷ d equals the same constant: approximately 3.14159… It never ends and never repeats, so mathematicians gave it a name: pi, written with the Greek letter π. This single fact gives us the formula C = πd.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. These five words are essential for circles.

Circumference
Tap to flip
The distance around the outside of a circle — its perimeter.
A circle with d = 10 cm has circumference ≈ 31.4 cm.
Diameter
Tap to flip
A straight line through the centre from one side to the other. Symbol: d. Always twice the radius.
r = 7 cm → d = 14 cm.
Radius
Tap to flip
The distance from the centre to the edge. Symbol: r. Always half the diameter.
d = 14 cm → r = 7 cm.
Pi (π)
Tap to flip
The constant ratio C ÷ d for every circle. π ≈ 3.14159… — it never terminates or repeats.
Use the π button on your calculator for full accuracy.
Arc
Tap to flip
A portion of the circumference — a curved part of the circle’s edge. A semicircle is a half-arc.
Semicircle arc = πr.

Example D — Practical context: distance a wheel travels per rotation
1

Each full rotation of the wheel traces out exactly one circumference on the ground.
Distance per rotation = C = 2πr
