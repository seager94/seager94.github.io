1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's statistics.

A real-estate agent advertises that the average (mean) home in a beachside suburb costs $1.2 million. But when you scroll the listings, almost every house is priced between $600k and $750k.

How can both be true? A handful of clifftop mansions sell for $7–8 million each. Those few extreme values drag the mean upward — far away from what a typical family actually pays.

That is why property reports quote the median price, not the mean. The median sits in the middle and barely moves when a few mansions are added. Today you'll learn to read the shape of a data set — symmetric, skewed or bi-modal — and judge when the mean tells the honest story and when the median does.

The pattern showing how often each value occurs across a data set.

Symmetric
Tap to flip
A distribution whose left and right sides are roughly mirror images. The mean and median sit close together.
Heights of students often form a symmetric "hump".
Skewed
Tap to flip
When one tail is longer than the other. Positive skew = tail to the right; negative skew = tail to the left.
House prices are positively skewed by a few mansions.
Bi-modal
Tap to flip
A distribution with two clear peaks — often two separate clusters with a gap between them.
Shoe sizes for a mixed adult class can be bi-modal.
Outlier
Tap to flip
A value far away from the rest of the data. It pulls the mean strongly but barely affects the median.
In 4, 5, 6, 6, 40 — the 40 is an outlier.

Example 1 — Describe the shape and locate the centre

A team's goals scored in their last 11 matches: 0, 0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 5, 8. Describe the shape of this distribution.

1
Read the display — clusters, gaps and tails
Look at where the dots bunch up and where they thin out.
The dots cluster low (at 0 and 1), then thin into a long tail stretching right toward 8. There is a gap between 5 and 8.
2
Find the median and the mean
11 values, so the median is the 6th value. The mean is the total divided by 11.
Median = 6th value = 1
Mean = (0+0+1+1+1+1+2+2+3+5+8) ÷ 11 = 24 ÷ 11 ≈ 2.2
3
Connect shape, mean and median
The mean (2.2) is larger than the median (1). The long right tail has pulled the mean up.
Mean > median, and the tail points right → the distribution is positively skewed.
Answer Positive skew — mean (2.2) pulled right of median (1)

Example 2 — Compare two data sets

Two classes sat the same quiz (out of 10).
Class A: 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 8, 8, 9
Class B: 3, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9, 9, 10, 10
Which class did better, and which was more consistent?

1
Compare the means
Each class has 9 students. Add the scores and divide by 9.
Class A mean = 63 ÷ 9 = 7
Class B mean = 63 ÷ 9 = 7   → the means are equal.
2
Compare the medians and the spread
The median is the 5th value of each ordered list. The range is largest − smallest.
Class A: median = 7, range = 9 − 5 = 4
Class B: median = 8, range = 10 − 3 = 7
3
Interpret the comparison
Same mean — but the median and spread tell us more.
Class B has the higher median (8), so its typical score is higher. Class A has the smaller range (4), so its scores are more consistent.
Answer Equal means; Class B scored higher (median 8), Class A more consistent (range 4)

Example 3 — The effect of an outlier

Weekly pay of 7 people at a small business ($): 220, 240, 250, 260, 270, 280, 1900 (the last is the owner). Find the mean and median, then see what happens when the owner's pay is removed.

1
Mean and median with the outlier
7 values — the median is the 4th. The mean is the total ÷ 7.
Median = 4th value = $260
Mean = (220+240+250+260+270+280+1900) ÷ 7 = 3420 ÷ 7 ≈ $489
2
Remove the outlier and recalculate
Drop $1900. Now 6 values remain — the median is the average of the 3rd and 4th.
Median = (250 + 260) ÷ 2 = $255
Mean = (220+240+250+260+270+280) ÷ 6 = 1520 ÷ 6 ≈ $253
3
Compare the effect
See how far each measure moved when the outlier was removed.
Mean dropped from $489 to $253 — a huge $236 fall. Median moved only $5. The mean is sensitive to outliers; the median is resistant.
Answer With an outlier, the median ($260) describes typical pay far better than the mean ($489)
