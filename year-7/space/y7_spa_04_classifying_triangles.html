1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's geometry.

Look at a bridge, a roof truss, or a bike frame and you'll see triangles everywhere. Builders choose triangles because a triangle is rigid — push on it and it keeps its shape, while a square folds flat into a diamond.

But there's a catch. Not every set of three beams will actually close up into a triangle. If one beam is too long, the other two simply can't reach across to meet it.

Today you'll learn the triangle inequality — a fast test for whether three lengths can form a triangle at all — and how to classify any triangle by its sides (scalene, isosceles, equilateral) and by its angles (acute, right-angled, obtuse).

Example 1 — Can these lengths form a triangle?

Three timber rods measure 4 cm, 5 cm and 11 cm. Can they be joined to make a triangle?

1
Identify the longest side
Pick out the longest rod — it's the one the other two must reach across.
Longest side = 11 cm  ·  the two shorter sides are 4 cm and 5 cm
2
Add the two shorter sides and compare
The two shorter sides must reach further than the longest side.
4 + 5 = 9
Compare: 9 < 11
3
Conclude
The two shorter sides add to only 9 cm — that's not more than the 11 cm side, so the rods can't meet to close the triangle.
9 is not greater than 11 — the triangle inequality fails.
Answer No — these three lengths cannot form a triangle.
Example 2 — Classify by side lengths

Classify the triangle with sides 7 cm, 7 cm and 10 cm.

1
Count how many sides are equal
Compare the three side lengths.
7 cm and 7 cm are equal  ·  10 cm is different — so exactly two sides match.
2
Match the count to a name
Each side-classification depends only on how many sides are equal.
All three equal → equilateral
Exactly two equal → isosceles
All three different → scalene
3
Conclude
Two equal sides places this triangle in the isosceles family. Its two base angles will be equal too.
Exactly two equal sides → isosceles.
Answer Isosceles triangle
Example 3 — Classify by angle sizes

Classify the triangle with angles 35°, 45° and 100°.

1
Check the angle sum
The three angles of any triangle add to 180° — a quick check the triangle is valid.
35° + 45° + 100° = 180° ✓
2
Find the largest angle and compare to 90°
The angle-classification depends only on the biggest angle.
All angles below 90° → acute
Exactly one angle = 90° → right-angled
One angle above 90° → obtuse
Largest angle here = 100°, and 100° > 90°
3
Conclude
Because one angle is greater than 90°, the triangle is obtuse. (A triangle can only ever have one such angle — the other two must share what's left of 180°.)
Largest angle 100° is greater than 90° → obtuse.
Answer Obtuse triangle
