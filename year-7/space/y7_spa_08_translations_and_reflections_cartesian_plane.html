1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's geometry.

Picture moving a sprite around a video game level. Every time the character takes a step — one square right, two squares up — you're applying a translation: a slide.

Now picture designing a tiled mosaic for a kitchen splashback. You draw one motif, then mirror it across a centre line so the pattern is symmetrical. That mirror is a reflection: a flip.

A transformation is a rule that moves every point of a shape in the same way. A translation slides; a reflection flips. In both, the shape keeps its size and form — only its position or orientation changes. Same shape, new address. Today we'll write these rules using coordinates.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. You'll use these five words constantly today.

Transformation
Tap to flip
A rule that moves every point of a shape in the same way.
A translation, a reflection, and a rotation are all transformations.
Translation
Tap to flip
A slide — every point moves the same distance in the same direction. No flipping or turning.
(x, y) → (x+3, y−1) slides 3 right and 1 down.
Reflection
Tap to flip
A flip across a mirror line. Each point lands the same distance the other side.
Reflecting (3, 4) in the x-axis gives (3, −4).
Image
Tap to flip
The new figure after the transformation. We mark image points with a prime: P → P′.
If P = (2, 3) and we translate by (x+1, y), the image is P′ = (3, 3).
Coordinate rule
Tap to flip
A formula showing what happens to a general point (x, y) under the transformation.
(x, y) → (−x, y) is the rule for reflection in the y-axis.

5Worked example — the 3-step method

Translate triangle A(1, 2), B(3, 2), C(2, 4) by the rule (x, y) → (x+4, y−1).

1
Read the coordinate rule
The rule tells us exactly what to do to every point.
(x, y) → (x+4, y−1)
Add 4 to every x. Subtract 1 from every y.
2
Apply the rule to each vertex
Substitute each vertex's coordinates into the rule.
A(1, 2) → A′(1+4, 2−1) = (5, 1)
B(3, 2) → B′(3+4, 2−1) = (7, 1)
C(2, 4) → C′(2+4, 4−1) = (6, 3)
3
Sanity-check the image
Every vertex should have moved the same way — 4 right and 1 down. And the image triangle should be the same shape and size as the original.
A → A′: 1 → 5 (right 4 ✓), 2 → 1 (down 1 ✓)
The image triangle A′B′C′ is the same size and shape — only its address changed.
Answer A′(5, 1), B′(7, 1), C′(6, 3)

6Together — reflect triangle PQR in the y-axis

Triangle PQR has vertices P(−2, 1), Q(−2, 4), R(−5, 4). Apply the rule for reflection in the y-axis: (x, y) → (−x, y).
Type each image as (x, y) — for example (2, -3).

💡 Why this rule: reflecting across the y-axis is like folding the page along the y-axis. A point 5 units left of the axis lands 5 units right of it — same height, opposite side. Only the x-coordinate's sign changes.

7Spot the mistake

A student tried to reflect a point in the x-axis but made the most common mistake in this whole topic. Read each line carefully — click on the line where the mistake first appears.

Reflect the point P(4, −3) in the x-axis.
1 Mirror line: the x-axis (a horizontal line).
2 Reflection rule: (x, y) → (−x, y)
3 Apply: P(4, −3) → P′ = (−4, −3)

✓ Right — that's where the mistake first appears.

Why: the x-axis is the horizontal mirror, so a point's height (its y-coordinate) flips while its left-right position (its x-coordinate) stays the same. The correct rule is (x, y) → (x, −y) — change the sign of y, not x. The rule the student wrote, (x, y) → (−x, y), is the rule for reflection in the y-axis. Easy to mix up — always pause to ask "which axis is the mirror?"
