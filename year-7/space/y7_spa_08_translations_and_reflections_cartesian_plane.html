1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's geometry.

You're coding a 2D game. Your character — a little sprite at point (3, 2) on the grid — needs to take 4 steps right and 1 step down. Inside the game's code, every frame just runs the rule:

(x, y) → (x+4, y−1)

The sprite is now at (7, 1). Same shape, same size — just moved.

Or maybe you're designing a tiling pattern: one motif on the left, its mirror image on the right. To reflect the motif across the centre line, the code applies (x, y) → (−x, y) to every vertex.

A transformation is a rule that moves every point of a shape in the same way. A translation slides a shape; a reflection flips it. In both, the shape keeps its size and form — only its position or orientation changes. Today you'll learn to describe these moves on the Cartesian plane using coordinate rules.

5Worked example — translate a triangle

Translate triangle A(1, 2), B(3, 2), C(2, 4) by the rule (x, y) → (x+4, y−1) and list the image coordinates.

1
Read the rule
The rule (x, y) → (x+4, y−1) says: for every point, add 4 to the x-coordinate and subtract 1 from the y-coordinate. Same rule applies to every vertex.
For each vertex (x, y), the image vertex is (x+4, y−1).
2
Apply the rule to each vertex
Work through A, B and C one at a time. Substitute the coordinates into the rule.
A(1, 2) → (1+4, 2−1) = A′(5, 1) B(3, 2) → (3+4, 2−1) = B′(7, 1) C(2, 4) → (2+4, 4−1) = C′(6, 3)
3
Plot and check
Plot both triangles on the Cartesian plane. The image should be congruent — same size, same shape, same orientation — just slid 4 right and 1 down. The pre-image is shown in teal; the image in terracotta.

6Together — reflect a triangle in the y-axis

First, look at a single point. The point P(3, 2) reflected in the x-axis is (3, −2) — flip the sign of y. Reflected in the y-axis it's (−3, 2) — flip the sign of x. Now we'll apply this to a whole triangle.

P(3, 2) — pre-image Images after reflection
P(3, 2) reflected in the x-axis lands at (3, −2). Reflected in the y-axis it lands at (−3, 2).

Now your turn — together: Reflect triangle A(2, 1), B(4, 1), C(3, 3) in the y-axis. Use the rule (x, y) → (−x, y).

Pre-image (ABC) Image (A′B′C′)
Reflection in the y-axis flips left-to-right. Notice the image is a mirror image.
💡 Why this works: reflecting in the y-axis flips left and right. The y-coordinate (how far up or down) doesn't change — only the x-coordinate flips sign. The rule is (x, y) → (−x, y).

7Error analysis — Sam's tricky mistake ⚠ Spot the slip

A very common mistake: muddling up which coordinate to flip. Let's diagnose Sam's working before you do your own practice.

The task Sam was given

Reflect the point Q(4, −3) in the x-axis.

Sam wrote:

"Reflecting in the x-axis flips the x-coordinate, so Q′ = (−4, −3)."
Correct image Sam's wrong image
Sam's point landed across the y-axis, not across the x-axis.

The fix:

  • Reflecting in the x-axis means the image is the mirror across the horizontal axis. The shape moves up or down, so the y-coordinate flips sign. Rule: (x, y) → (x, −y).
  • Reflecting in the y-axis means the mirror is the vertical axis. The shape moves left or right, so the x-coordinate flips sign. Rule: (x, y) → (−x, y).
  • Sam used the y-axis rule by mistake. The correct image of Q(4, −3) in the x-axis is Q′(4, 3) — the y flips, not the x.

🎯 Memory trick: the axis you reflect in is the axis you cross. Reflecting in the x-axis means you cross the x-axis, so y changes sign. Reflecting in the y-axis means you cross the y-axis, so x changes sign.
