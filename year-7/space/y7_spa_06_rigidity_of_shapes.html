1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's geometry.

A timber garden gate is built as a simple square frame of four boards. After one winter it no longer closes — it has slowly sagged sideways into a leaning, diamond shape. The four boards are exactly the same length they always were, yet the gate's shape has changed.

The carpenter fixes it in five minutes by screwing a single board diagonally from one corner to the opposite corner. The gate never sags again.

That diagonal board turns the square into two triangles — and a triangle simply cannot be pushed out of shape. Today you'll discover why: triangles are rigid, but quadrilaterals are flexible. It's the reason triangles appear in roof trusses, cranes, bridges and bicycle frames everywhere you look.

A flat (2D) shape made of straight sides that fully enclose a region.
A triangle, a square and a hexagon are all polygons.
Diagonal
Tap to flip
A straight line joining two corners that are not next to each other.
A square has 2 diagonals; a pentagon has 5.
Rigid
Tap to flip
A framework is rigid if it cannot be pushed out of shape — it holds its form. The opposite is flexible.
A triangle made of paper strips is rigid.
Brace
Tap to flip
An extra strip, usually a diagonal, added to a frame to stop it flexing.
A diagonal brace splits a square into two triangles.
Triangulation
Tap to flip
Dividing a shape into triangles so the whole framework becomes rigid.
Two braces triangulate a pentagon into three triangles.

5Worked examples — three to watch

Each example reveals one step at a time. Watch how the same idea — split it into triangles — solves every one.

Example 1 — Bracing a square gate

A garden gate is a square frame of four timber strips. Pushed from the side, it sags into a leaning shape. How many diagonal braces make it rigid?

1
Identify the shape
Count the sides of the frame.
4 strips → a quadrilateral. A four-sided frame is flexible: the corner angles can change while every side stays the same length.
2
Add a diagonal brace
Join one corner to the opposite corner with an extra strip.
One diagonal splits the square into 2 triangles.
3
Check the result
Each triangle has three fixed side lengths, so each one is rigid. Two rigid triangles sharing the diagonal lock the whole frame together.
Every region is now a triangle → the gate cannot deform.
Answer 1 diagonal brace
Example 2 — A wobbly pentagon window

A pentagon-shaped window frame is built from five strips and wobbles badly. How many diagonal braces does it need to become rigid?

1
Count the sides
Name the polygon and record how many sides it has.
A pentagon has n = 5 sides.
2
Apply the bracing rule
To triangulate a polygon, draw every diagonal from one corner. The number of braces is always the number of sides minus 3.
braces = n − 3 = 5 − 3 = 2
3
Check the result
The 2 diagonals drawn from one corner cut the pentagon into 3 triangles. Every region is a triangle, so the frame is rigid.
2 braces → 3 triangles → fully triangulated frame.
Answer 2 diagonal braces
Example 3 — Why is a triangle rigid?

This one is a reasoning example. Explain why a triangle holds its shape but a quadrilateral does not.

1
Fix the three sides of a triangle
Imagine three strips of set lengths joined at their ends.
Given three side lengths, only one triangle can ever be built. The three angles are forced — there is no freedom left to push it.
2
Fix the four sides of a quadrilateral
Now do the same with four strips of set lengths.
The four side lengths are fixed, but the angles are not forced. A square can be pushed sideways into a leaning parallelogram — same sides, different shape.
3
Draw the conclusion
Compare what fixing the sides actually achieved in each case.
Fixing the sides of a triangle fixes its shape completely; fixing the sides of a quadrilateral does not. That is exactly why a triangle is rigid and a quadrilateral is flexible.
Answer A triangle's three fixed sides determine one unique shape
