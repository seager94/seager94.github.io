1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's geometry.

Imagine a zoologist finds an unknown creature in a rainforest. How does she identify it? She uses a dichotomous key — a list of yes/no questions:

  • Does it have feathers? No → not a bird
  • Does it have fur? Yes → mammal
  • Does it lay eggs? Yes → monotreme…

Each answer eliminates half the possibilities. After a few questions, only one creature is left.

Geometry uses the same trick. A shape can't tell you its name — but its attributes can. How many sides? Any parallel sides? Any right angles? Today you'll design a classification scheme — a sequence of yes/no questions — that identifies any polygon you draw.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. These five words give you the language for today's scheme.

Polygon
Tap to flip
A closed 2D shape made of straight line segments only.
A triangle is a polygon. A circle is not (curved). A shape with a gap is not (not closed).
Attribute
Tap to flip
A property of a shape you can measure or check — like number of sides, side length, angle size, or parallel pairs.
Attributes of a square: 4 sides, all equal, 4 right angles, 2 pairs of parallel sides.
Convex
Tap to flip
Every interior angle is less than 180°. No "dents."
A regular hexagon is convex. A 5-point star ★ is not.
Regular polygon
Tap to flip
All sides equal AND all angles equal. Both at once.
A square is regular. A rectangle is not (sides not all equal). A rhombus is not (angles not all equal).
Classification scheme
Tap to flip
A sequence of yes/no questions about attributes that sorts shapes into named categories.
Like a flowchart. Each question splits the remaining shapes; after a few steps only one name fits.

5Worked example — name this shape

A 4-sided polygon with all four sides equal (5 cm each) but only opposite angles equal (no right angles). Let's walk it through our scheme.

1
List the attributes
Before we ask any classification questions, gather the facts.
Number of sides: 4
All sides equal? Yes (all 5 cm)
All angles 90°? No
Opposite sides parallel? Yes (both pairs)
2
Walk down the classification scheme
Use a flowchart of yes/no questions. Each answer narrows the possibilities.
Q1: Is it a polygon? Yes ✓
Q2: How many sides? 4 → quadrilateral
Q3: All sides equal? Yes ✓
Q4: All angles 90°? No ✗
→ Rhombus
3
Sanity-check against the definition
A rhombus is a quadrilateral with four equal sides; its opposite angles are equal but the angles need not be 90°. Our shape matches every condition. If all angles had been 90°, we'd have stopped at "square" instead.
Rhombus: 4 sides ✓   all sides equal ✓   angles not 90° ✓
Answer Rhombus

6Together — name this triangle

A triangle has sides 5 cm, 5 cm, and 7 cm, and one of its angles is 90°. We'll narrow it down with three questions, just like the scheme. After each prompt, discuss with the person next to you, then we'll fill in the box together.

💡 Why we do it this way: we always start with the most general attribute (number of sides) and work toward more specific ones (equal sides, equal angles). This is exactly the design pattern in any classification scheme — broad first, then narrow.
