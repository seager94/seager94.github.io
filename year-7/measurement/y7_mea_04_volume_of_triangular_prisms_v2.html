1Why this matters — one formula for every prism

A big idea that connects last lesson to today.

Last lesson you found the volume of a rectangular prism by multiplying its three side lengths. But that hides a deeper pattern:

  • A rectangular prism is a stack of identical rectangles.
  • A triangular prism is a stack of identical triangles.
  • A cylinder is a stack of identical circles.

The volume of any right prism is the area of one slice multiplied by how long the stack is.

The big idea V = (cross-section area) × length

A Toblerone bar is just identical triangles stacked end-to-end. Find the area of one triangle, multiply by how long the bar is, and you have the volume. Today we apply this to wheelchair ramps, animal-feed troughs, and Toblerone bars.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip it. Five words you'll use the whole lesson.

Prism
Tap to flip
A 3-D solid with two identical parallel ends joined by rectangles.
A Toblerone bar, a brick, and a hexagonal pencil are all prisms.
Cross-section
Tap to flip
The shape you see when you slice straight through a prism at right angles to its length.
Slice a Toblerone bar and you see a triangle.
Right prism
Tap to flip
A prism whose rectangular sides meet the cross-section at 90° — i.e. not slanted or leaning.
A normal Toblerone is a right prism; a leaning one is oblique.
Perpendicular height
Tap to flip
The height of a triangle measured at a right angle to its base — not the slanted side.
Base 4 cm, perpendicular height 3 cm → area = 6 cm².
Capacity
Tap to flip
The volume a container can hold, usually in litres or millilitres.
1 litre = 1000 cm³. So 2500 cm³ = 2.5 L.

5Worked example — a Toblerone bar

A Toblerone-style prism has a triangular cross-section with base 4 cm and perpendicular height 3 cm. The bar is 20 cm long. Find the volume.

1
Identify the cross-section
A Toblerone bar is a stack of identical triangles. The cross-section is a triangle with base 4 cm and perpendicular height 3 cm.
Cross-section: a triangle, base 4 cm, perpendicular height 3 cm.
Length of prism: 20 cm.
2
Find the cross-section area
Area of a triangle = ½ × base × perpendicular height.
Area = ½ × 4 × 3 = 6 cm²
3
Multiply by the length
Volume = cross-section area × length. Don't forget to use cubic units (cm³) because we are measuring volume.
Volume = 6 × 20 = 120 cm³
Answer Volume of the Toblerone bar = 120 cm³

💡 Why we do it this way: the routine is always the same — (1) find the cross-section area, (2) multiply by length. The units change (cm³, m³, mm³) but the method doesn't. The ramp is the same problem as the Toblerone, just with different units.
