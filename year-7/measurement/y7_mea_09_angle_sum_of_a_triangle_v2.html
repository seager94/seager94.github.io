1The torn-corner demonstration

A hands-on way to discover today's rule. (Try it with a paper triangle later — the result is always the same.)

Take any triangle — any shape at all. Tear off the three corners. Now arrange the three torn corners so the points meet and the edges line up.

Every time you do this, the three corners fit together along a straight line. A straight line is 180°, so the three angles of any triangle must add up to 180°.

💡 This works for every triangle — small, large, pointy, flat. The shape doesn't matter; the angle sum is always 180°.
Three corners → one straight line

Interior angle

An angle inside the triangle, between two sides.
A triangle has three interior angles. They always sum to 180°.
Exterior angle
Tap to flip
The angle outside the triangle, formed by extending one side past a vertex.
An interior angle and its exterior angle sit on a straight line, so they add to 180°.
Angle sum
Tap to flip
The total of all interior angles in a shape.
For a triangle, the angle sum is 180° — always.
Isosceles
Tap to flip
A triangle with two equal sides — and therefore two equal base angles.
If the apex is 40°, the two base angles are equal: each is (180 − 40) ÷ 2 = 70°.
Alternate angles
Tap to flip
When a line crosses two parallel lines, the angles on opposite sides of the crossing line are equal. (Lesson 07 — Z-shape.)
We'll use this in today's proof to shift two triangle angles up onto a parallel line.

4Spot the mistake

A student has tried to find x in a triangle with angles x°, 2x°, and 3x°. Read each line carefully — click on the line where the mistake first appears.

Find x:  a triangle has interior angles x°, 2x°, and 3x°.
1 x + 2x + 3x = 6x
2 So x = 6°
3 The angles are 6°, 12°, and 18°.

✓ Right — that's where the mistake first appears.

Why: the student combined the three angle expressions — x + 2x + 3x = 6x — but forgot to use the rule. The three angles of any triangle add to 180°, so the equation should have been x + 2x + 3x = 180°. That gives 6x = 180°, so x = 30°. The angles are 30°, 60°, 90° — a right-angled triangle! Tip 6° + 12° + 18° = 36°, not 180°, so the student's "answer" couldn't have been right.

5Why the angle sum is 180° — a parallel-line proof

Three steps. We'll build the proof one stage at a time.

1
Take any triangle and label the three interior angles
Call the angles α (alpha) at A, β (beta) at B, and γ (gamma) at C. We want to show α + β + γ = 180°.
2
Draw a line through A parallel to BC
Now use alternate angles (Lesson 07, Z-shape): β re-appears at A on the left of the parallel line, and γ re-appears at A on the right.
3
The three angles at A sit on a straight line
β, α and γ are now arranged side-by-side along the line through A. Angles on a straight line sum to 180°, so:
β + α + γ = 180°
∴  α + β + γ = 180°
Result The interior angles of any triangle sum to 180°.
