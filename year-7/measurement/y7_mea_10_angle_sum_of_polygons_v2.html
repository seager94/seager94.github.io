1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's geometry.

Look closely at a beehive 🐝. The cells are regular hexagons — six equal sides, six equal angles. Bees aren't taught geometry, but they've evolved the shape that uses the least wax to store the most honey, and which tiles together without gaps.

For that tiling to work, three hexagons have to meet at a point with no overlap and no gap. That only works because each interior angle of a regular hexagon is exactly 120° — and 3 × 120° = 360°.

How did we get 120°? A hexagon's six interior angles add to 720°. In a regular hexagon they're equal, so each one is 720° ÷ 6 = 120°. Today you'll learn the rule that gives you the angle sum of any polygon.

💡 The big idea: any polygon with n sides can be split into n − 2 triangles by drawing diagonals from a single vertex. Each triangle's angles add to 180°, so the polygon's interior angle sum is (n − 2) × 180°.

5Worked example — angle sum of a hexagon

Find the interior angle sum of a hexagon.

1
Identify n
Count the sides. A hexagon has 6 sides, so n = 6.
n = 6
2
Apply the formula (n − 2) × 180°
Substitute n = 6. We are saying the hexagon is made of 6 − 2 = 4 triangles, each contributing 180°.
Sum = (6 − 2) × 180°
= 4 × 180°
3
Compute
4 × 180° = 720°.
= 720°
Answer Interior angle sum of a hexagon = 720°
🔁 Try the same routine yourself: for an octagon, n = 8, so the sum is (8 − 2) × 180° = 6 × 180° = 1080°. That's the next problem in the We Do.
