1Why this matters

Ratios let us scale recipes, mixtures and designs while keeping the proportions correct.

Imagine you're baking biscuits for a class party. Your recipe makes 12 biscuits and uses 2 cups of flour and 1 cup of sugar. But you need biscuits for 36 people.

You can't just guess more flour and sugar — if you change the ratio, the biscuits won't taste right. To triple the recipe, you triple every ingredient: 6 cups of flour, 3 cups of sugar. The ratio stays 2:1.

The same idea works for concrete (cement:sand:gravel must stay 1:2:3 or the wall cracks), paint (red:white = 2:3 makes a coral shade — change it and the colour shifts), and design (architects pick rectangle shapes by ratio because some shapes look more pleasing than others). Today we use ratios to model these real situations.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. You'll use these five words constantly today.

Ratio
Tap to flip
A way of comparing two or more quantities of the same kind.
Oil to vinegar in dressing is 3:1 — three parts oil for every one part vinegar.
Parts
Tap to flip
Each number in a ratio represents one part of the total.
In concrete 1:2:3, there are 1+2+3 = 6 parts in total.
Equivalent ratios
Tap to flip
Two ratios that mean the same thing because both sides have been multiplied or divided by the same number.
2:3 and 4:6 are equivalent (multiply both sides by 2).
Scale factor
Tap to flip
The number you multiply each part by to scale a ratio up (or divide to scale it down).
A 1:2 recipe with scale factor 5 becomes 5:10.
Proportion
Tap to flip
A statement that two ratios are equal. Keeping the same proportion means keeping the same ratio.
2:3 = 8:12 is a proportion — both ratios simplify to 2:3.

✨ The golden ratio

There's a special ratio called the golden ratio, written with the Greek letter φ (phi) ≈ 1.618. A rectangle whose long side is about 1.618 times its short side appears in the Parthenon in Athens, the Mona Lisa, credit cards, and Apple's logo grid. Many people find it the most visually pleasing shape — but does your eye agree?

5Worked example — concrete mixing

A builder needs 24 buckets of concrete in the ratio cement : sand : gravel = 1 : 2 : 3. How many buckets of each ingredient?

1
Add the parts to find the total parts
Each number in the ratio is a "part". Add them all up.
Total parts = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 parts
2
Find the size of one part
Divide the total amount by the number of parts.
1 part = 24 buckets ÷ 6 parts = 4 buckets per part
3
Multiply each part of the ratio by the size of one part
Then check: the three amounts should add to 24.
Cement = 1 × 4 = 4 buckets
Sand   = 2 × 4 = 8 buckets
Gravel = 3 × 4 = 12 buckets
Check: 4 + 8 + 12 = 24 ✓
Answer 4 buckets cement · 8 buckets sand · 12 buckets gravel

6Together — salad dressing for a bigger party
