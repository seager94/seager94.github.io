2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. The word perpendicular is the one that catches students out — pay close attention to that one.

Base
Tap to flip
Any side of the triangle you choose as the bottom. Once chosen, the perpendicular height is measured from this side.
A triangle has three sides — any of them can be the base.
Perpendicular height
Tap to flip
The distance from the top vertex straight down to the base — at a right angle (90°).
Often drawn as a dashed line with a small square showing the 90° angle.
Right angle
Tap to flip
A 90° angle — a perfect corner like the corner of a page.
Shown as a small square in the corner, never as an arc.
Slanted side
Tap to flip
A side of the triangle that is not perpendicular to the base. This is NOT the height.
Using a slanted side instead of the perpendicular height is the most common mistake.
Area
Tap to flip
The amount of space inside a 2D shape, measured in square units (cm², m², etc.).
A square 1 cm on each side has an area of 1 cm².

5The big idea — and three cases

Why the formula is what it is, then three worked examples covering every kind of triangle.

Any triangle fits inside a rectangle with the same base and height. The triangle is exactly half of that rectangle.

The spatial argument. A rectangle with base b and height h has area b × h. If we draw any triangle inside that rectangle using the same base and height, the two "leftover" corners of the rectangle together have the same area as the triangle itself.

So the triangle takes up exactly half the rectangle:

Formula A = 12 × base × perpendicular height

The trick is the word perpendicular. You must measure the height at right angles to the base — not along any slanted side.

Right-angled triangle
Case A Right-angled triangle

Identify. The two sides that meet at the right angle are the base and the perpendicular height. Here, base = 8 cm, height = 5 cm.

Apply the formula.

A = 12 × base × height
A = 12 × 8 × 5
A = 12 × 40 = 20 cm²

Check: a 5 × 8 rectangle has area 40 cm². The triangle is half of that — 20 cm². ✓

Acute triangle — dashed height inside
Case B Acute triangle

Identify. The base is the 10 cm side at the bottom. The perpendicular height is the dashed line drawn from the top vertex down to the base at 90° — that's 6 cm. The slanted sides are not the height.

Apply the formula.

A = 12 × base × height
A = 12 × 10 × 6
A = 12 × 60 = 30 cm²

Check: enclose it in a 10 × 6 rectangle. Rectangle area = 60 cm². Triangle = half = 30 cm². ✓

Obtuse triangle — the height lands outside the triangle
Case C — key Obtuse triangle (height outside)

Identify. The triangle is obtuse — one of its angles is bigger than 90°. To draw a perpendicular from the far vertex to the base, we have to extend the base outwards (the small dashed line on the left). The dashed perpendicular is still the height — it just lands outside the triangle.

Apply the formula. Same rule, no exceptions:

A = 12 × base × height
A = 12 × 7 × 4
A = 12 × 28 = 14 cm²

Big idea: the perpendicular height is a measurement, not a side. It doesn't have to be inside the triangle.

7Spot the mistake ⚠ Common error

A student has tried to find the area of this triangle. Read each line of their working — click the line where the mistake first appears.

The problem. Find the area of this triangle.
The base is 8 cm. The slanted side on the right is 10 cm. The dashed perpendicular height inside the triangle is 6 cm.
1 A = 12 × base × height
2 A = 12 × 8 × 10
3 A = 12 × 80
4 A = 40 cm²

✓ Right — line 2 is where the mistake first appears.

Why: the student grabbed the slanted side (10 cm) instead of the perpendicular height (6 cm — the dashed line at 90° to the base). Every line after that is wrong because it's built on the wrong height. This is by far the most common mistake with triangle areas — always look for the dashed line, or the side that meets the base at a right angle.
