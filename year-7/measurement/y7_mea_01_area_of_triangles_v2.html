1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's measurement.

The school is having a triangular shade sail installed over the Year 7 courtyard. Before the order can go in, the installer needs to know how much sail fabric to buy.

The shade-cloth costs $45 per square metre. Order too little and the sail won't reach the posts; order too much and the school wastes money. They need the exact area of the triangle.

The triangular sail has a base of 6 m and a perpendicular height of 4 m. Today's formula gives the area in one line: A = ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 m². So the fabric costs 12 × $45 = $540. Learn the formula and you can quote any triangular sail.

6Worked example 1 — a right-angled triangle

Find the area of a right-angled triangle with base 10 cm and perpendicular height 6 cm — and discover where the formula comes from.

1
Build a rectangle around the triangle
A right-angled triangle fits exactly inside a rectangle with the same base and height. This rectangle is 10 cm wide and 6 cm tall.
The shaded triangle is half of the rectangle.
Rectangle area = base × height = 10 × 6 = 60 cm²
2
A triangle is half of that rectangle
The diagonal cuts the rectangle into two identical triangles. Each triangle is exactly half of the rectangle — so halve the rectangle's area.
Area of triangle = ½ × (base × height)
So A = ½ × base × perpendicular height
3
Substitute the measurements
Put base = 10 and perpendicular height = 6 into the formula.
A = ½ × 10 × 6 = ½ × 60 = 30
Answer Area = 30 cm²

7Worked example 2 — an acute triangle

Find the area of an acute triangle with base 14 cm and perpendicular height 8 cm.

1
Identify the base and the perpendicular height
The perpendicular height is the dashed line from the base up to the opposite vertex. It meets the base at a right angle — it is not one of the slanted sides.
For an acute triangle the height lands inside the triangle.
base = 14 cm,   perpendicular height = 8 cm
2
Apply the formula
Use A = ½ × base × perpendicular height.
A = ½ × 14 × 8
3
Calculate
Halve the base first, then multiply — it keeps the numbers small.
½ × 14 = 7,   and   7 × 8 = 56
Answer Area = 56 cm²

8Worked example 3 — an obtuse triangle

Find the area of an obtuse triangle with base 9 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm — where the height falls outside the base.

1
Find the perpendicular height — even though it lands outside
This triangle is obtuse, so the perpendicular from the top vertex meets the base line outside the triangle. We extend the base (dashed) to reach it. The perpendicular height is still measured at a right angle — here it is 4 cm.
The base is extended (dashed) so the height has somewhere to land.
base = 9 cm,   perpendicular height = 4 cm
2
The formula does not change
Acute, right-angled or obtuse — every triangle uses the same formula. The height being outside the base makes no difference at all.
A = ½ × base × perpendicular height = ½ × 9 × 4
3
Calculate
Multiply 9 × 4 first, then halve.
9 × 4 = 36,   and   ½ × 36 = 18
Answer Area = 18 cm²
