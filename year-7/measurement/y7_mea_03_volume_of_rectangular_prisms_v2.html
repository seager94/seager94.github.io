1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's measurement.

Mrs Tran is buying a new fish tank 🐠. The shop has two on display:

  • Tank A: 60 cm long, 30 cm wide, 40 cm deep
  • Tank B: 50 cm long, 40 cm wide, 35 cm deep

She needs the bigger one — but bigger how? Tank A is longer; Tank B is wider. To compare fairly she has to ask: how much water fits inside?

That "how much fits inside" is called volume. For a rectangular box-shape, the volume is found by multiplying the three side lengths: length × width × height. Tank A holds 72 000 cm³ of water; Tank B holds 70 000 cm³. Today you'll learn the rule for any rectangular prism.

2Key vocabulary

Volume
Tap to flip
The amount of 3D space a solid takes up — how much "stuff" fits inside.
A small Lego brick has a tiny volume; a fridge has a large one.
Rectangular prism
Tap to flip
A 3D shape with 6 rectangular faces and all corners square.
A cereal box, a brick, and a shipping container are all rectangular prisms.
Cubic units
Tap to flip
The units we use to measure volume — every dimension is multiplied, so the unit is cubed.
cm × cm × cm = cm³. Also: mm³, m³.
Base
Tap to flip
The bottom face of a prism — the rectangle the prism stands on.
For a 6 × 4 × 5 cm box: the base is a 6 × 4 rectangle (area 24 cm²).
Capacity
Tap to flip
How much a container can hold (usually liquid). Closely related to volume but uses litres/mL.
A 1 L water bottle has a capacity of 1000 mL — which equals a volume of 1000 cm³.

5Worked example — building the formula from unit cubes

Build a rectangular prism that is 4 units long, 3 units wide, and 2 units high out of unit cubes (each cube is 1 × 1 × 1). How many cubes do we use? And what rule does that give us?

1
Build the bottom layer
Lay cubes flat on the floor in a rectangle that is 4 wide and 3 deep.

3
Spot the formula
Look at what we just multiplied: 4 × 3 (the base) × 2 (the number of layers). Those three numbers are the prism's length, width, and height.
Volume = length × width × height
        = 4 × 3 × 2
        = 24 cubic units
Two ways to see it. Volume = length × width × height or Volume = (area of base) × height. They give the same answer because length × width is the area of the base. Pick whichever is easier for the question.
Formula V = l × w × h  |  V = 24 cubic units

6Together — find the volume of a 6 mm × 4 mm × 5 mm prism

A small Lego-style block measures 6 mm long, 4 mm wide, and 5 mm tall. We'll find its volume in three steps. Discuss each prompt with the person next to you before we fill in the box.

💡 Why we do it this way: finding the area of the base first lets you "see" one layer of unit cubes. Multiplying by the height then tells you how many of those layers are stacked. It's the same idea as the 4 × 3 × 2 cube prism — just with measurements in mm instead of unit cubes.
