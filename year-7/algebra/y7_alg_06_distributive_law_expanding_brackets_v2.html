1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's algebra.

Imagine someone asks you to calculate 7 × 98 in your head — no calculator, no paper. Tricky?

Most people would split it up like this:

7 × 98 = 7 × (100 − 2)
= 700 − 14
= 686

You just used the distributive law — multiplying through a bracket. Today we'll use the same idea with algebra.

The expression 3(x + 4) means "3 lots of (x + 4)". To expand it, we multiply the 3 by every term inside the bracket: 3 × x + 3 × 4 = 3x + 12. The area model shows why this works — and that's where we start.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. These five words drive today's lesson.

Bracket
Tap to flip
The round symbols ( ) that group terms together as one quantity.
In 3(x + 4): the bracket groups x + 4.
Expand
Tap to flip
To remove the bracket by multiplying through. The expression looks "bigger" but means the same thing.
3(x + 4) expands to 3x + 12.
Distribute
Tap to flip
To share the multiplier across every term inside the bracket — like handing out one to each.
Distribute 5 across (x + 2): 5 goes to x and 5 goes to 2.
Coefficient
Tap to flip
The number that multiplies a variable — sits in front of it.
In 8x: the coefficient is 8. In x: the coefficient is 1.
Distributive law
Tap to flip
The rule that a(b + c) = ab + ac. Multiply the outside by every term inside.
4(2x − 3) = 4·2x + 4·(−3) = 8x − 12.

5Worked example — expand 3(x + 4) using the area model

The area model is our anchor: a rectangle's area can be found by splitting it into pieces. That's why the distributive law works.

1

Height = 3. Width = x + 4. The total area is 3(x + 4) — that's what we're trying to expand.

6Together — expand 4(2x − 3)
