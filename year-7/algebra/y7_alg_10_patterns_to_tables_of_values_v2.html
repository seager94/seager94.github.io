1Why this matters

A growing pattern tells a story. Today we learn to capture that story — and then predict every future chapter.

Imagine a graphic novel where each page shows a tower made of matchsticks. On page 1 the tower has 1 square. On page 2 it has 2 squares. On page 3, three squares — and so on.

Your teacher asks: "How many matchsticks would page 100 need?"

You could draw all 100 pages — but that would take hours. Or you could find the rule that turns "page number" into "matchstick count," and use it to skip straight to the answer.

The pictures are the story. A table captures the story in numbers. A rule predicts every future chapter — even page 100, or 1000. Today you'll move all the way from pictures, to table, to rule.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. These five words are the language of patterns.

Term
Tap to flip
A single value in a sequence or pattern.
In 4, 7, 10, 13, ... the 3rd term is 10.
Position (n)
Tap to flip
The address of a term — 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and so on. We call it n.
When n = 4 you want the 4th term.
Growing pattern
Tap to flip
A sequence where each term changes from the one before in a predictable way.
4, 7, 10, 13, 16, ... grows by adding 3 each step.
Rule
Tap to flip
A formula that gives any term from its position number.
Matchstick rule: m = 3n + 1. Plug in n = 20 → 61 matches.
Variable
Tap to flip
A letter that stands for a number that can change.
In m = 3n + 1: both m and n are variables.

Look for what stays the same — what changes
From one column to the next, the matches go up by 3 each time (not 4 — because each new square shares a side with the last). That "+3 each step" is the coefficient of n in our rule.
4 → 7 → 10   steps of +3
So the rule looks like m = 3n + ?
3
Pin down the "+ something" and check
Try n = 1: 3(1) = 3, but we need 4. So add 1. Rule: m = 3n + 1. Check n = 2: 3(2) + 1 = 7 ✓ and n = 3: 3(3) + 1 = 10 ✓
Predict n = 20: m = 3(20) + 1 = 61 matches. We didn't have to draw 20 pictures!
Rule m = 3n + 1

6Together — triangle-number dots

Each new row of dots is one longer than the row above. The first few terms are 1, 3, 6, 10.

n = 1
1 dot
n = 2
3 dots
n = 3
6 dots
n = 4
10 dots

From term 1 to 2 we added 2. From 2 to 3 we added 3. From 3 to 4 we added 4. The amount added grows each step — this pattern is non-linear.

💡 The big idea here: we can still build a table and describe how this pattern grows, even though there's no neat "add the same number each time" rule. Not every story is a linear one — and noticing the difference is the start of understanding sequences.
