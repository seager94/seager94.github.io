1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's mathematics.

It's a cold July morning in South Australia. Overnight, the Bureau of Meteorology recorded these temperatures:

  • Adelaide CBD: −2°C ❄️
  • Mount Lofty: −5°C ❄️❄️
  • Port Augusta: +3°C

The weather report says Mount Lofty was "the coldest". But how do we decide that −5 is smaller than −2, when 5 looks bigger than 2?

On a number line, numbers further left are smaller. So −5 < −2 — Mount Lofty was colder. With negative numbers, the bigger the magnitude (size), the smaller the number. Today we'll learn to compare and order any two integers using < and >.

Integer

A whole number — positive, negative, or zero. No fractions, no decimals.
−4, 0, and 7 are integers. ½ is not.
Sign
Tap to flip
Whether a number is positive (+) or negative (−).
In −5: the sign is negative. In +3 (or just 3): the sign is positive.
Magnitude
Tap to flip
The size of a number, ignoring its sign — how far it is from zero.
−7 and +7 both have magnitude 7.
Greater than (>)
Tap to flip
"Is bigger than" — or "is further right on the number line."
5 > −3. The open mouth points to the bigger number.
Less than (<)
Tap to flip
"Is smaller than" — or "is further left on the number line."
−7 < −2. The point of the arrow points at the smaller number.

5Worked example — order from smallest to largest

Order this set from smallest to largest: { −7,   2,   −1,   5,   −4 }

1
Plot each integer on a number line
Mark every value from the set on the line below.
2
Read them off the line, left to right
Left is smaller; right is larger. Just read the markers in order.
−7,   −4,   −1,   2,   5
3
Sanity-check with sign and magnitude
All negatives sit before all positives ✓  |  Among the negatives, the one with the biggest magnitude (−7) is smallest ✓  |  Among the positives, 2 < 5 ✓.
−7 < −4 < −1 < 2 < 5
Answer — smallest to largest −7,   −4,   −1,   2,   5
