1Why this matters — same share, different name

Two ways of describing the same amount of pizza. Both are correct. Knowing they're equal is what equivalent fractions are about.

🍕 Three friends, two pizzas

You and two friends share 2 whole pizzas equally. Each person gets 23 of a pizza — 2 pizzas split into 3 equal shares.

Then the pizzeria cuts every pizza into 6 slices before you take your share — 12 slices in total. Each person now gets 4 slices, which is 46 of a pizza.

📐 Same amount, two names

Nobody got more pizza because of the extra cuts. 23 and 46 name the same quantity.

Cut every slice in half again? Each person ends up with 6 thinner slices out of 18 total = 69. Or 8 out of 12 = 812. Still the same share of pizza, just sliced finer.

💡 The big idea today: multiplying or dividing both the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number gives an equivalent fraction. The amount doesn't change — only how finely we've cut it up.

5Worked example — simplify 2436

The cleanest way to simplify is to find the HCF of the numerator and denominator, then divide both by it in one go. Three steps.

1
Find the HCF of 24 and 36
List the factors of each, then pick the largest one they share.
Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36
→ HCF = 12
2
Divide both numerator and denominator by 12
Same operation, top and bottom. This is what keeps the fraction equivalent.
2436 = 24 ÷ 1236 ÷ 12 = 23
3
Check it's now in simplest form
HCF of 2 and 3 is 1, so we can't simplify any further. Done.
Factors of 2: 1, 2. Factors of 3: 1, 3. Only shared factor is 1 ✓
Answer 2436 = 23
💡 Don't see the HCF straight away? Cancel any common factor you spot, then repeat. 2436 ÷ 22 = 1218 ÷ 22 = 69 ÷ 33 = 23. Same answer, three small steps.

6Together — which is bigger, 23 or 56?

We can't compare them directly — they have different denominators (thirds vs sixths). Three small steps: choose a common denominator, rewrite, then compare.

💡 Why we pick 6 as the common denominator: 6 is a multiple of both 3 and 6, so we can rewrite each fraction in sixths without changing its value. Once both fractions have the same denominator, we just compare the numerators — bigger numerator means bigger share.
