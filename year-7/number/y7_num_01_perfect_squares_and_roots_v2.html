1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's number work.

Imagine you're tiling a small square patio outside the back door. You've bought 49 paving stones, all the same size, and you want to lay them in a perfect square.

How many stones go along each side?

You need a number that, multiplied by itself, gives 49. That number is 7 — because 7 × 7 = 49. So 7 stones across and 7 stones down.

Today, "7 squared" (72) means 7 × 7 = 49 — the area of a 7-by-7 square. The reverse move — "what side length squared gives 49?" — is called the square root, written √49 = 7. Squaring and square-rooting are inverse operations: one undoes the other.

Two operations that undo each other. Squaring and square-rooting are inverses.
Start with 4. Square it: 42 = 16. Now root it: √16 = 4. Back where you started.

5Three worked examples

One example for each move today: build a perfect square from dots, find a square root by reverse-thinking, and estimate a non-perfect root between two whole numbers.

Example 1 — Build 62 using a dot array

1
Visualise as a square array
"6 squared" means 6 rows of 6 dots — arranged in a perfect square.
6 rows of 6 dots
2
Count by multiplying
Don't count dots one by one — multiply rows × columns.
6 rows × 6 dots per row = 36
3
Write it the short way
"6 squared" is written 62. The small 2 is the index — it tells you to multiply 6 by itself.
62 = 6 × 6 = 36
Answer 62 = 36

Example 2 — Find √81 using an area square

1
Re-state the question as a side length
A square of area 81 has what side length? That's what √81 asks.

2
Ask: what whole number squared makes 81?
Run through nearby squares.
82 = 8 × 8 = 64   — too small
92 = 9 × 9 = 81   ✓
102 = 10 × 10 = 100   — too big
3
Write the answer and check with the inverse
Square root and squaring undo each other — that's the check.
√81 = 9   and check: 92 = 81 ✓
Answer √81 = 9

Example 3 — Estimate √70 between two consecutive whole numbers

1
List the perfect squares around 70
Find the largest perfect square below 70 and the smallest above.
648 × 8 < 70 < 819 × 9
2
Take the square root of both sides
If 64 < 70 < 81, then √64 < √70 < √81 — that puts √70 between 8 and 9.
√70 lies between 8 and 9 — and is closer to 8, because 70 is closer to 64 than to 81.
3
State the estimate clearly
For a non-perfect square, we don't get a whole-number root. We pin it between the two nearest whole numbers.
8 < √70 < 9
Answer √70 is between 8 and 9 (closer to 8)
