1Why this matters — place value is powers of 10

A real number, two ways of saying it. Both are correct. The second is what mathematicians, scientists and engineers actually write.

🏫 The Year-3 way

A school has 3,750 students. A teacher writes it on the board as:

3 thousand + 7 hundred + 5 tens

This is correct, and it's how we read the number aloud. But the words get clumsy fast — try saying a population of 1,400,000.

🔬 The Year-7 way

The same number, using powers of 10:

3 × 103  +  7 × 102  +  5 × 101

Same meaning, but now we can scale to millions or billions without inventing more words. This is expanded notation.

💡 The big idea today: "thousand" is just another word for 103; "hundred" is 102; "ten" is 101; and "one" is 100. Place value is powers of 10 — wearing different clothes.

3Power of 10, or not?

Drag each number into the correct bucket. A number is a power of 10 only if it's exactly 1, 10, 100, 1000, … — a 1 followed by some zeros. Numbers like 200 or 50 are not powers of 10, even though they contain a 1 in earlier columns. Wrong drops will shake.

Each digit means "how many of that column's power of 10." The 3 in the thousands column means three 103's — three thousands.

3
Read the expanded form from the chart
For each column, write digit × power, then add them. Drop any term where the digit is 0 — it adds nothing.
3,750  =  3 × 103  +  7 × 102  +  5 × 101  +  0 × 100
          = 3 × 103  +  7 × 102  +  5 × 101
Answer 3,750  =  3 × 103 + 7 × 102 + 5 × 101

Sanity check: 3 × 1000 + 7 × 100 + 5 × 10 = 3000 + 700 + 50 = 3,750 ✓

💡 Shortcut: the exponent equals the column number, counting from the right starting at 0. The rightmost digit is the 100 column, the next is 101, and so on. Practise reading the exponent off without writing the chart.
