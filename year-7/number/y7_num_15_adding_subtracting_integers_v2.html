1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's number sense.

It's 7 am at Mt Buller. The overnight temperature dropped to −6 °C. By 2 pm the sun has been out for hours and the temperature has climbed 11 °C.

What does the thermometer read at 2 pm? You can't just say "11 °C" — you have to start at −6 and move up by 11. That means you're crossing zero on the way.

The same kind of question shows up everywhere a quantity can be negative: a basement floor, a submarine's depth, an overdrawn bank balance, a losing score in a card game.

The number line is your model. The operation tells you which way to walk; the size of the second number tells you how many steps. Today you'll learn the four cases — add a positive, add a negative, subtract a positive, subtract a negative — and apply them to lifts, sea level, bank balances, and temperature.

💡 The four rules — write these in your book:
  • Add a positive → move right → (e.g. 5 + 3: move right 3 from 5, land on 8)
  • Add a negative → move ← left (e.g. 5 + (−8): move left 8 from 5, land on −3)
  • Subtract a positive → move ← left (e.g. 5 − 8: move left 8 from 5, land on −3)
  • Subtract a negative → move right → (the trickiest — subtraction flips direction, and negative flips it again, so the two flips cancel)

5Worked example — subtracting a negative

Calculate: 3 − (−4). This is the trickiest of the four cases — let's break it down with the number line.

1
Identify the operation
Read the expression carefully. We're starting at 3. The operation is subtract. The number being subtracted is −4 (negative four).
Start: 3   Operation: subtract   Number: (−4)
2
Apply the rule
Subtraction normally moves us left. But the number we're subtracting is negative — that flips the direction again. Two flips cancel out, so we end up moving right.
subtract + negative = move RIGHT →
So 3 − (−4) is the same as 3 + 4.
3
Walk it on the number line
Start at 3. Walk 4 steps right: 4, 5, 6, 7. We land on 7. Sense-check: subtracting a negative made the answer bigger, which fits the rule.
