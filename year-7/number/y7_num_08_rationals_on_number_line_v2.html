1Why this matters

Some real-world situations need numbers below zero — and those numbers aren't always whole.

You already use a number line every day — you just don't call it that. A thermometer is a number line standing on its end. A bank balance is a number line. The depth gauge on a submarine is a number line.

Today, the values that sit on that line aren't only whole numbers. They include halves, quarters, thirds — and they can be on either side of zero.

🌡️Temperature. Mount Lofty this morning: −212°C.
💳Bank balance. Owing $7.25 is a balance of −$7.25.
⛰️Altitude. The Dead Sea shore sits at about −43012 m.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. You'll use these five words constantly today.

Number line
Tap to flip
A straight line where each point stands for a number. Values increase to the right and decrease to the left.
… −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 …
Zero (origin)
Tap to flip
The fixed reference point. Everything to its right is positive; everything to its left is negative.
Zero is neither positive nor negative.
Negative number
Tap to flip
A number less than zero. Written with a minus sign in front.
−34,  −2.5,  −7
Mixed numeral
Tap to flip
A whole number combined with a proper fraction.
114 = 1 + 14 = 54
Rational number
Tap to flip
Any number that can be written as a fraction ab where a, b are integers and b ≠ 0.
Includes all integers, fractions, and terminating/recurring decimals.

5Worked example — plot 114 and −212

Plot both values on a number line that runs from −3 to 3.

1
Decide which side of zero
A positive value sits to the right of zero; a negative value sits to the left.
−212  ⟶ left of 0   |   114  ⟶ right of 0
2
Find the whole-number "neighbours"
For each value, identify the two consecutive integers it sits between. That tells us which gap to subdivide.
114 lies between 1 and 2
−212 lies between −3 and −2
3
Subdivide the gap and mark the point
Split each gap into the right number of fractional parts. 114 is one quarter past 1. −212 is halfway between −3 and −2.
Answer Both points placed on a single number line from −3 to 3.
