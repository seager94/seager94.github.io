1Why this matters — two questions, same tool

Two real situations. They look completely different — but the same trick solves both.

🚌 Shared multiples

Two buses leave the depot at 9:00 a.m. The red bus runs every 12 minutes, the blue bus every 18 minutes.

When do they next leave together?

You need a time that is both a multiple of 12 and a multiple of 18 — the smallest one. This is the LCM.

🟩 Shared factors

A rectangular floor is 24 cm by 36 cm. You want to cover it with the biggest possible identical square tiles — no cutting allowed.

What size are the tiles?

You need a side length that divides both 24 and 36 — the biggest one. This is the HCF.

💡 Notice: one problem needs the smallest shared multiple, the other needs the biggest shared factor. Today we learn one method — using prime factorisation — that answers both in seconds.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. The last two — HCF and LCM — are the goal of today's lesson.

Factor
Tap to flip
A whole number that divides another number exactly — no remainder.
The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12.
Multiple
Tap to flip
The result of multiplying a number by 1, 2, 3, … — its "times table".
The first multiples of 12 are 12, 24, 36, 48, 60…
Common factor
Tap to flip
A number that is a factor of two (or more) numbers at once.
The common factors of 12 and 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6.
HCF
Tap to flip
Highest Common Factor — the biggest number that divides both.
HCF(12, 18) = 6, because 6 divides 12 and 18, and nothing bigger does.
LCM
Tap to flip
Lowest Common Multiple — the smallest number that both divide into.
LCM(12, 18) = 36, because 36 is a multiple of both, and nothing smaller is.

5Worked example — HCF and LCM of 12 and 18

The big idea: write each number as primes, then draw a Venn. Overlap = HCF. Everything = LCM.

1
Write each number in prime-factor form
Use a factor tree (yesterday's tool) if you need to.
12 = 2 × 2 × 3 = 22 × 3
18 = 2 × 3 × 3 = 2 × 32
2
Place the primes in a Venn diagram
Primes that appear in both lists (paired off, one-for-one) go in the overlap. Anything left over goes in its own circle.
Prime factors of 12 and 18
Only in 12: one extra 2 Shared: 2 and 3 Only in 18: one extra 3
3
Read off both answers
HCF is the product of the primes in the overlap. LCM is the product of every prime in the diagram.
HCF — overlap only
2 × 3
= 6
LCM — everything
2 × 2 × 3 × 3
= 36
Answer HCF(12, 18) = 6  ·  LCM(12, 18) = 36

Sanity check: 6 divides 12 and 18 ✓. 36 = 12 × 3 = 18 × 2 ✓. Also 6 × 36 = 216 = 12 × 18 ✓.

💡 Exponent shortcut: if you don't want to draw the Venn, just look at the prime-factor forms. HCF = take the lower power of each shared prime. LCM = take the higher power of every prime that appears. From 12 = 22×3 and 18 = 2×32: HCF = 21×31 = 6, LCM = 22×32 = 36.
