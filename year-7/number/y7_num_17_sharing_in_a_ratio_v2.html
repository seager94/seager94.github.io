1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's maths.

Mia and Jay buy a $20 raffle ticket together. Mia chips in $8, Jay chips in $12 — they share the cost in the ratio 2 : 3.

The ticket wins $60. How should they split the prize fairly?

They didn't pay equal amounts, so splitting the prize in half wouldn't be fair. We need a method that matches the ratio.

Count the parts: 2 + 3 = 5 equal shares. Divide the $60 into 5 parts of $12 each. Mia (2 parts) gets $24; Jay (3 parts) gets $36. Check: $24 + $36 = $60. ✓ Today: the same three-step method works for any quantity and any ratio.

Ratio

A way of comparing two or more quantities, written with a colon.
A ratio of 2 : 3 means 2 parts of one to 3 parts of the other.
Parts
Tap to flip
The numbers in the ratio. Each part is the same size.
In 2 : 3 the parts are 2 and 3.
Total parts
Tap to flip
Add all the parts together. This tells you how many equal pieces the quantity is split into.
For 2 : 3, total parts = 2 + 3 = 5.
One part
Tap to flip
The value of a single part — found by dividing the total quantity by the total parts.
$60 ÷ 5 parts → $12 per part.
Share
Tap to flip
A person's portion — their number of parts × value of one part.
Jay's share (3 parts × $12) = $36.

5Worked example — the 3-step method

Share $60 between Mia and Jay in the ratio 2 : 3.

1
Count the total parts
Add the numbers in the ratio.
Total parts = 2 + 3 = 5
2
Find the value of one part
Divide the total quantity by the total parts.
One part = $60 ÷ 5 = $12
3
Multiply for each share — and check
Each person's share = their number of parts × the value of one part. Always check the shares add back to the original total.
Mia: 2 × $12 = $24
Jay: 3 × $12 = $36
Check: $24 + $36 = $60 ✓
Answer Mia gets $24, Jay gets $36
