1Why this matters

A real fundraiser, and the three formulae that tell you whether it "worked".

Last term, Year 7 ran a sausage sizzle at the school fair. They spent $180 on sausages, buns, sauces, and gas. By the end of the day they'd taken $300 in cash.

So — was it "worth it"? They made $120, which sounds good. But how would you compare that to another stall that spent only $20 and made $40? Both made a profit, but which one used its money better?

That's what today's lesson does: it gives you the language of profit, loss, and % profit so you can model and compare real financial situations.

The three formulae

Profit = revenue − costs
Loss = costs − revenue
(used when costs are bigger than revenue)
% Profit = profit ÷ costs × 100
"Revenue" = money in from sales. "Costs" = money you spent to run the activity.

5Worked example — the school cake stall

Buying ingredients cost $80. The stall sold $130 worth of cakes. What's the profit and the % profit?

1
Identify the revenue and the costs
First, label each dollar amount — money in or money out.
Revenue (money in from cake sales) = $130
Costs (money out for ingredients) = $80
2
Calculate the profit
Revenue is bigger than costs, so it's a profit. Use profit = revenue − costs.
Profit = $130 − $80 = $50
3
Calculate the % profit
% profit = profit ÷ costs × 100. It tells you how efficiently your costs turned into profit.
% profit = $50 ÷ $80 × 100
= 0.625 × 100
= 62.5%
Answer Profit = $50  ·  % profit = 62.5%

💡 Why we work it this way: revenue isn't always written as a single dollar amount — sometimes you need to multiply quantity × price first. Once revenue is a single number, the profit formula does the rest.

One more — the loss case

Year 7 spent $150 on stock for the school disco, but only sold $120 worth of tickets. What's the loss?

Costs ($150) are bigger than revenue ($120), so it's a loss.
Loss = costs − revenue = $150 − $120 = $30

Note: when costs > revenue, we don't say "negative profit" — we use the word "loss" and write it as a positive dollar amount.
