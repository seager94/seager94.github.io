1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's fraction maths.

Anna's afternoon timetable looks like this:

  • 12 hour — tutoring
  • 14 hour — piano practice

How much time in total? You'd say three-quarters of an hour — but how did you get there? You couldn't just add "1 + 1 = 2 over 2 + 4 = 6" — that would give 26, which is only 20 minutes. Wrong.

The trick: rewrite both fractions with the same denominator. 12 is the same quantity as 24. Now both are "fourths of an hour", and you can add: 24 + 14 = 34 hour = 45 min. Today we learn the rules behind that move.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. You'll use these five words constantly today.

Common denominator
Tap to flip
When two fractions share the same bottom number. You can only add or subtract once the denominators match.
For 12 and 13, a common denominator is 6.
Numerator
Tap to flip
The top number of a fraction — counts how many parts you have.
In 38: the numerator is 3. When denominators match, you add the numerators.
Mixed numeral
Tap to flip
A whole number plus a fraction written together.
214 means 2 + 14. As an improper fraction, that's 94.
Regrouping
Tap to flip
When subtracting, swap one whole for fraction parts so you can take away.
314 − 134: rewrite 314 as 254, then subtract.
Place value (decimals)
Tap to flip
For decimals, line up the decimal points so tenths sit under tenths and hundredths under hundredths.
0.45 + 0.70 — line up the points; add column by column = 1.15.

5Worked example — 12 + 13

The denominators are different. We'll build a 2 rows × 3 columns rectangle — the 2 and the 3 multiplied give us 6 small cells, our common denominator.

1
Build the array: 2 rows × 3 columns = 6 cells
The 2 in the first denominator becomes the number of rows. The 3 in the second denominator becomes the number of columns. Every cell now represents 16.
6 cells total → denominator 6
2
Shade each fraction inside the same array
12 is one of the two rows — that's 3 cells = 36. 13 is one of the three columns' worth — 2 cells = 26. We place them so they don't overlap.
■ 12 = 36  ·  ■ 13 = 26
3
Now the denominators match — add the numerators
Same denominator, so 36 + 26 just adds the tops: 3 + 2 = 5. Five shaded cells out of six.
36 + 26 = 56
Answer 12 + 13 = 56

4 rows × 3 cols = 12
Coral cells = 34 = 912. Crossed-out cells = the 212 we're taking away. Remaining coral = the answer.
💡 The routine: (1) find a common denominator — the array shows 4×3 = 12 cells; (2) rewrite each fraction over that denominator; (3) add or subtract the numerators only — denominators stay the same.
