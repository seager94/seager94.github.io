1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's maths.

You spot a pair of jeans at Cotton On marked:

"25% off $48"

The sign tells you the percentage and the original price — but not the saving. To know how much you actually save (and what you'll pay at the till), you need to find 25% of $48.

By the end of this lesson you'll have two methods for finding any percentage of any quantity — and you'll meet this skill everywhere: GST on shopping, tips at restaurants, sale prices, interest on savings.

The word "of" in maths means multiply. So 25% of $48 means 25100 × 48  or  0.25 × 48. Both give the same answer: $12 saving. Today we learn when each method is fastest.

5Worked example — find 15% of $80

15% doesn't have a quick fraction in your head — so this is a great problem to compare both methods. Then we'll check using a clever shortcut.

1
Write the percentage as either a fraction or a decimal
"15%" means 15 out of 100. We can write it two equally valid ways.
Fraction approach
15% = 15100
Decimal approach
15% = 0.15
2
Multiply by the quantity — "of" means ×
15% of $80 = (fraction or decimal) × 80. Both routes give the same answer.
Fraction approach
15100 × 80
= 15 × 80100
= 1200100
= $12
Decimal approach
0.15 × 80
= 12
= $12
3
Check using the 10% trick — split 15% as 10% + half of 10%
10% is just "divide by 10" — and 5% is half of 10%. This is the fastest mental method when the percentage is friendly.
10% of $80 = $80 ÷ 10 = $8
5% of $80 = half of $8 = $4
15% of $80 = $8 + $4 = $12 ✓
Answer 15% of $80 = $12
