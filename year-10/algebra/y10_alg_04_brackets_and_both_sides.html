1Warm-up — when are two plans the same price?

You're shopping for a phone plan. Two networks are offering you different deals…

TeleA
$20 monthly fee
+ $4 for every GB of data used
Cost = 20 + 4g
TeleB
$5 monthly fee
+ $7 for every GB of data used
Cost = 5 + 7g

If TeleA is cheaper for low data users but TeleB charges less to join, there must be a break-even point — a number of GB at which both plans cost exactly the same. To find it, set the costs equal:

20 + 4g = 5 + 7g
💡 Look at that equation — the variable g appears on both sides. That's something new. And later, if TeleA bundles a 6-month deal sold as 6(20 + 4g), you'd need to deal with brackets. Both of those moves are what today's lesson is about — and they're the doorway into the expanding-and-factorising work that follows next week.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. The distributive property card is the one to remember — it's the key to brackets, both this lesson and the expanding lessons next week.

Equation
Tap to flip
A statement that two expressions are equal, joined by an = sign.
2x + 8 = 18 is an equation. 2x + 8 on its own is just an expression.
Solution
Tap to flip
The value of the variable that makes both sides equal.
For 2x + 8 = 18, the solution is x = 5 — because 2(5) + 8 = 18.
Distributive property
Tap to flip
Multiplying a bracket: a(b + c) = ab + ac. Each term inside is multiplied by the number outside.
3(x + 4) = 3x + 12. This is how you "expand" brackets.
Inverse operation
Tap to flip
The operation that undoes another. + ↔ −, × ↔ ÷.
To undo "+ 8" you subtract 8. To undo "× 2" you divide by 2.
Like terms
Tap to flip
Terms with the same variable. Constants are like terms with each other. You can combine them by adding or subtracting.
5x and 2x are like terms — 5x − 2x = 3x.

5Worked example — solve 2(x + 4) = 18

Three steps: expand the brackets, solve the simpler equation, then check.

1
Expand the brackets (distributive property)
Multiply each term inside the brackets by the 2 outside: 2 × x and 2 × 4.
2(x + 4) = 18
2x + 8 = 18 ← brackets gone
2
Solve the two-step equation
Subtract 8 from both sides, then divide both sides by 2 — the same routine you used in Lesson 03.
2x + 8 = 18
2x = 10 ← subtract 8 from both sides
x = 5 ← divide both sides by 2
3
Check by substituting back into the original
Always check using the original equation, not the expanded one — that way you catch mistakes from Step 1 too.
2(5 + 4) = 2(9) = 18 ✓
Solution x = 5

6Together — solve 5x − 3 = 2x + 9

Three small steps. After each prompt, discuss with the person next to you — then we'll fill in the box together.

💡 The big idea: the equals sign is a balance. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other. To get all the x's on the left, subtract 2x from both sides — then you're back to a normal two-step equation. The pattern is: collect variables → collect constants → divide.
