1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's algebra.

A gardener installs a sprinkler at the point (3, −2) on the grid plan of their vegetable plot. The sprinkler reaches everything within 4 metres.

Which seedlings get watered? Rather than measuring each distance by hand, the gardener wants one tidy equation they can test any seedling's coordinates against.

That equation is exactly today's topic.

The sprinkler's spray boundary is the circle (x − 3)2 + (y + 2)2 = 16. Any point exactly on the boundary makes the left side equal 16; inside the circle makes it less; outside makes it more. Once we can read centre and radius straight off this form, we can write — and use — the equation of any circle on the plane.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. You'll use these five words constantly today.

Centre
Tap to flip
The point a circle is built around — every point on the circle is the same distance from it.
In (x−3)2+(y+2)2=16, the centre is (3, −2).
Radius
Tap to flip
The distance from the centre to any point on the circle.
If r2 = 25, then r = 5. The radius is always positive.
Unit circle
Tap to flip
The circle with centre (0, 0) and radius 1.
Equation: x2+y2=1. Every other circle is a transformation of this one.
Standard form
Tap to flip
The "centre & radius" version of a circle equation.
(x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2 — centre (h, k), radius r.
Translation
Tap to flip
A slide of the whole graph — no rotation, no resizing.
(x−3)2+y2=1 is 3 right of the unit circle. (y+2) means 2 down.

5Three worked examples — read centre & radius from standard form

Standard form is (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2. Whatever appears in the h and k slots gives the centre (h, k); the right side gives r2.

Example A
Find the centre and radius of x2+y2=1 and sketch it.
1
Match it to standard form
Rewrite each bare x as (x − 0) and each bare y as (y − 0).
x2 + y2 = 1
= (x − 0)2 + (y − 0)2 = 12
2
Read off (h, k) and r
The numbers inside the brackets give the centre; the square root of the right side gives the radius.
h = 0, k = 0 → centre = (0, 0)
r2 = 1 → r = 1
3
Sketch & describe the transformation
This is the unit circle. It's the base case — no transformation. Every other circle today is a copy of this one that has been resized and/or slid around.
Centre = (0, 0), radius = 1.
The circle passes through (1, 0), (0, 1), (−1, 0) and (0, −1).
Answer Centre (0, 0), radius 1 — this is the unit circle.

Example B — same centre, bigger radius

x2+y2=25
Both brackets are bare → h = 0, k = 0, so centre = (0, 0).
r2 = 25 → r = 5.

Transformation from the unit circle: the circle has been dilated (enlarged) by a scale factor of 5 about the origin. The centre stays where it is; every point on the boundary is now 5 times further out than on the unit circle.

Example C — centre moved off the origin

(x−3)2+(y+2)2=16
Compare to (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2.
(x − 3): the "− 3" matches "− h", so h = 3.
(y + 2) = (y − (−2)): the "+ 2" matches "− k", so k = −2.
Centre = (3, −2). r2 = 16 → r = 4.

Transformation from the unit circle: enlarge by scale factor 4, then translate 3 units right and 2 units down. The brackets slide the centre; the right-hand side stretches the radius.

⚠ The bracket signs flip when you read out the centre: (x − 3) means +3; (y + 2) means −2. Watch for this — it's the trap in Section 6.
