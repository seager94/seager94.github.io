1Warm-up — yesterday vs today

Last lesson we expanded brackets with positive multipliers. Today we deal with the trickier case: negatives.

Yesterday — comfortable
3(x + 4)
3 × x = 3x
3 × 4 = 12
= 3x + 12
Today — watch the signs!
−3(x + 4)
−3 × x = −3x
−3 × 4 = −12
= −3x − 12
💡 The method is the same: multiply every term inside by what's outside. The only new thing is keeping track of the sign — a negative multiplier flips the sign of every term inside the bracket.

Hidden −1
Tap to flip
A minus sign in front of a bracket means −1 is multiplying the bracket.
−(x + 7) means −1(x + 7) = −x − 7
Sign rules
Tap to flip
When multiplying:
(+)(+) = +
(+)(−) = −
(−)(+) = −
(−)(−) = +

5Worked example — the 3-step method

Expand: −2(3x − 5)

1
Identify what's outside and what's inside
The multiplier (outside) is −2. The terms inside are 3x and −5. Note the sign in front of the 5 — it belongs to that term.
−2 ( 3x − 5 )
2
Multiply each inside term by the outside
Use the sign rules. A negative times a negative gives a positive.
−2 × 3x = −6x
−2 × (−5) = +10
3
Write the expanded expression and check
Substitute x = 1 into both forms — they must agree. Original: −2(3·1 − 5) = −2(−2) = 4. Expanded: −6·1 + 10 = 4. ✓
−2(3x − 5) = −6x + 10
Answer −6x + 10

6Together — expand and simplify 4(2x + 3) − 2(x − 1)

Discuss each prompt with the person next to you, then we'll fill in the answer box together. The trick is to treat − 2(x − 1) as + (−2)(x − 1) — the minus belongs with the 2.

💡 Common slip: writing −2(x − 1) = −2x − 2. The second term inside is −1, so −2 × (−1) = +2. Always carry the sign across with the number.
