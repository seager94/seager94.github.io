1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's algebra.

You're paving a rectangular courtyard. One side is (2x + 3) metres and the other is (x + 5) metres — the actual dimensions depend on where you site it.

To order tiles you need the total area as a single expression in x. You can see it on paper: split the rectangle into four pieces, find each piece's area, and add them up. That's the area model — and it's exactly the distributive property, drawn.

Today you'll learn the area model, then meet three patterns — perfect squares and the difference of squares — that show up so often they're worth memorising.

Big idea Expansion = the distributive property, made visual. Three patterns recur often enough — (a+b)², (a−b)², and (a+b)(a−b) — that recognising them on sight is faster than expanding from scratch.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip it. Five words you'll use constantly today.

Binomial
Tap to flip
A polynomial with exactly two terms.
2x + 3 is a binomial. So is x − 5. x2 + 3x − 4 is not — it has three terms.
Expand
Tap to flip
To multiply out brackets so the expression is written as a sum of terms.
Expanding (x+2)(x+3) gives x2 + 5x + 6.
Distributive property
Tap to flip
A multiplier outside a bracket multiplies every term inside.
a(b+c) = ab + ac. Today: each term of one binomial multiplies every term of the other.
Perfect square
Tap to flip
A binomial multiplied by itself: (a+b)2 or (a−b)2. Expands to three terms with a middle term ±2ab.
(x+4)2 = x2 + 8x + 16. Don't forget the middle term!
Difference of squares
Tap to flip
A product of the form (a+b)(a−b). The middle terms cancel, leaving just a2 − b2.
(x+7)(x−7) = x2 − 49. Two terms only.

5Worked examples — the area model and three patterns

Expansion is the distributive property made visual. Every product (ax+b)(cx+d) is a rectangle split into four cells. We'll do the first one slowly, then read the three special patterns off their pictures.

Example 1Expand (2x+3)(x+5) using the area model

1
Draw the rectangle and label its sides
One side is (2x+3); the other is (x+5). Split each side at its + sign.
What we know so far
Width = 2x + 3
Height = x + 5
Total area = (2x+3)(x+5)
2
Find each cell's area — multiply the two sides that meet it
Each cell's area is its width × its height. Four cells, four sub-products.
The four sub-products
Top-left: 2x · x = 2x2
Top-right: 3 · x = 3x
Bottom-left: 2x · 5 = 10x
Bottom-right: 3 · 5 = 15
3
Add the four areas and collect like terms
3x and 10x are like terms — combine them to get the middle term.
2x2 + 3x + 10x + 15
= 2x2 + 13x + 15
Answer (2x+3)(x+5) = 2x2 + 13x + 15

Three patterns worth memorising

The area model still works — but for these three shapes, the answer can be read off without doing the work each time.

Example 2(3x + 2)2

= 9x2 + 6x + 6x + 4
= 9x2 + 12x + 4
Pattern (a + b)2 (a+b)2 = a2 + 2ab + b2

Example 3(2x − 5)2

= 4x2 − 10x − 10x + 25
= 4x2 − 20x + 25
Pattern (a − b)2 (a−b)2 = a2 − 2ab + b2

Example 4(4x + 7)(4x − 7)

= 16x2 + 28x − 28x − 49
= 16x2 − 49 (middle terms cancel)
Pattern (a + b)(a − b) (a+b)(a−b) = a2 − b2

The three patterns — worth memorising

Perfect square (+) (a+b)2 = a2 + 2ab + b2
Perfect square (−) (a−b)2 = a2 − 2ab + b2
Difference of squares (a+b)(a−b) = a2 − b2

Example 5Expand (3x + 4)(2x − 7) — a non-monic mixed product

Neither pattern fits — leading coefficients differ (3 and 2). Fall back to the area model.

Four sub-products → collect
Top-left: 3x · 2x = 6x2
Top-right: 4 · 2x = 8x
Bottom-left: 3x · (−7) = −21x
Bottom-right: 4 · (−7) = −28

= 6x2 + 8x − 21x − 28
= 6x2 − 13x − 28
(3x+4)(2x−7) = 6x2 − 13x − 28

6Error analysis — where does this expansion go wrong?

A student tried to expand (2x − 5)2. Read each line carefully. Click the line where the mistake first appears — then type the correct expansion.

Expand:   (2x − 5)2
1 (2x − 5)2 = (2x − 5)(2x − 5)
2 = (2x)2 + (−5)2
3 = 4x2 + 25

✓ Right — that's where the mistake first appears.

Why: the student squared each term and stopped. But (2x − 5)2 means (2x − 5)(2x − 5) — and that produces a middle term of −2·(2x)·(5) = −20x. Without it, the answer is wrong. The general pattern: (a − b)2 = a2 − 2ab + b2 — three terms, never two.

Now write the correct expansion of (2x − 5)2. Use x^2 for x2, no spaces.

💡 Why we do it this way: the area-model picture reminds you there are four cells, not two. Whenever you write (anything)2 and want to expand, expect three terms.
