3Expand the long way — (x + 3)2

A square has all four sides equal. So (x + 3)2 is a square with side length (x + 3). Click each cell to find the product. Then we'll combine.

(x + 3)2  =  (x + 3)(x + 3)
× x + 3
x ? ?
+ 3 ? ?
All four products x2 + 3x + 3x + 9
Collect like terms x2 + 6x + 9

So why isn't it just x2 + 9?

Because the middle gives you two products of 3x — one from x·3 and one from 3·x. Those combine to 6x. Squaring a binomial is never just squaring each term — the middle terms add up and they always survive.

5Worked example — expand (x − 4)2

Watch how the negative sign moves through. The middle term keeps its sign, and the last term is still positive.

1
Write the square as a product
(x − 4)2 means (x − 4) multiplied by itself.
(x − 4)2 = (x − 4)(x − 4)
2
Multiply every term by every term — get all 4 products
x·x, then x·(−4), then (−4)·x, then (−4)·(−4).
= x2 − 4x − 4x + 16
Notice: (−4)·(−4) is positive 16 — a negative times a negative.
3
Collect the two middle terms
−4x + (−4x) = −8x.
= x2 − 8x + 16
Answer x2 − 8x + 16
The pattern — perfect square
(a + b)2 = a2 + 2ab + b2
First term squared · twice the product · last term squared

7Spot the mistake — perfect squares 🔍 Enrichment

A Year 10 student has tried to expand (x + 3)2. Read each line carefully — click the line where the mistake first appears.

Expand:   (x + 3)2
1 (x + 3)2 = (x + 3)(x + 3)
2 = x · x + 3 · 3
3 = x2 + 9

✓ Right — that's where the mistake first appears.

What went wrong: the student multiplied the first term by the first term and the last by the last — but forgot the two cross-products. When you square a binomial, every term in one bracket has to multiply every term in the other. That's four products, not two.

The missing products: x · 3 = 3x  and  3 · x = 3x. These combine to give the middle term 6x — which is exactly what was missing from the answer x2 + 9.

Now rewrite line 2 correctly — show all four products. Use x^2 for x2, no spaces (e.g. x^2+3x+3x+9):

Remember this forever
(x + 3)2 ≠ x2 + 9
A square of a binomial always has a middle term — don't lose it.

8A surprising pattern — Difference of Two Squares

Now look at what happens when the two brackets are identical except for the sign. Watch the middle terms.

1
Expand (x + 5)(x − 5) the long way
Get all four products.
(x + 5)(x − 5) = x2 − 5x + 5x − 25
2
The middle terms cancel
−5x and +5x sum to zero. Only the first squared and last squared survive.
= x2 + 0 − 25 = x2 − 25
Answer x2 − 25
The pattern — difference of two squares (DOTS)
(a + b)(a − b) = a2 − b2
No middle term — square the first, square the last, subtract.

How to spot a DOTS

Two brackets, multiplied. The two terms inside are identical but the signs in the middle are opposite — one + and one −. Whenever you see that, you can jump straight to the answer: square the first, square the second, subtract.

  (2x − 3)(2x + 3)  →  4x2 − 9  (no middle term needed)
