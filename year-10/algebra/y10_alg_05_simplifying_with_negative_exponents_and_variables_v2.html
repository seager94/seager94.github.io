1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's algebra.

A new car worth $24 000 loses 15% of its value each year. After n years it's worth:

Pn = 24 000 × (0.85)n

After 5 years that's about $10 650. But what was the car worth 2 years before we bought it? That's a negative time index:

P−2 = 24 000 × (0.85)−2

A negative exponent runs the model the other way: (0.85)−2 = 1(0.85)2 ≈ 1.384. So 2 years earlier the car was worth about $33 200.

Negative exponents aren't a glitch — they're a tool. Any time you have an exponential model running forwards, a negative exponent runs it backwards. Today we'll see that the exponent laws from Year 9 — multiply, divide, power of a power — still work when the exponents go negative. The only new piece: a−n = 1an. It means reciprocal — never a negative number.

5Worked example — the 3-step method

Simplify and write with positive exponents only:  (3x2y−1)2 ÷ (6x−1y2)

1
Apply the outer power to each factor inside the bracket
Use (ab)n = anbn and the power-of-a-power law (am)n = amn. Multiply each inside exponent by 2.
(3x2y−1)2 = 32x2×2y−1×2 = 9x4y−2
So the whole expression becomes   9x4y−2 ÷ 6x−1y2
2
Divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents
Use am ÷ an = am−n for each variable. Be careful with the signs — subtracting a negative becomes adding.
96 = 32  ·  x4 − (−1) = x5  ·  y−2 − 2 = y−4
= 32x5y−4
3
Rewrite with positive exponents only
Any factor with a negative exponent moves to the denominator. Here only y−4 needs to flip — the coefficient and x5 stay where they are.
32x5y−4 = 3x52y4   (restriction: x ≠ 0 and y ≠ 0)
Answer 3x52y4

6Together — solve 2n = 32 using the exponent laws

When both sides of an equation are powers of the same base, the exponents must be equal. We'll find n in three small steps.

💡 Why this works: 2n is a one-to-one function — different exponents give different values. So if 2n = 25, the only possibility is n = 5. This is the key idea for solving any equation of the form bx = c when c is a power of b.
