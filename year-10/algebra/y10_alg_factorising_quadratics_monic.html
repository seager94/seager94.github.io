1Warm-up — going backwards

You already know how to expand. Today we run the process in reverse.

Last week you expanded brackets like this:

(x + 2)(x + 5)  =  x2 + 7x + 10

Today we go the other way. Given x2 + 7x + 10, can we recover the brackets it came from?

This is called factorising — writing a quadratic as a product of two brackets. Same machinery, run backwards.

The big idea: when (x + p)(x + q) is expanded, the constant term is p × q and the middle coefficient is p + q. To factorise, find the two numbers that multiply to give c and add to give b. That's the whole lesson.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. These five words are the language of today's lesson.

Quadratic
Tap to flip
An expression whose highest power of the variable is 2.
x2 + 5x − 6 is a quadratic. 3x + 1 is not.
Factorise
Tap to flip
To rewrite an expression as a product of factors — the reverse of expanding.
x2 + 7x + 10 factorises to (x + 2)(x + 5).
Factor
Tap to flip
An expression that divides another exactly. Brackets in a factorised form are the factors.
(x + 2) and (x + 5) are the factors of x2 + 7x + 10.
Coefficient
Tap to flip
The number multiplying a variable term. In x2 + bx + c, b is the coefficient of x.
In x2 − 8x + 15: the coefficient of x is −8.
Constant term
Tap to flip
The term with no variable. In x2 + bx + c, that's c.
In x2 − 8x + 15: the constant term is 15.

5Worked example — the 3-step method

Factorise: x2 + 9x + 20

1
Identify b and c
Read off the coefficient of x and the constant term.
x2 + 9x + 20
b = 9  ·  c = 20
We need two integers that multiply to 20 and add to 9.
2
List factor pairs of c and check sums
Test each pair against the target sum of 9.
(1, 20)  → sum 21 ✗
(2, 10)  → sum 12 ✗
(4, 5)  → sum 9 ✓
3
Write the factorisation and check
Drop the two numbers straight into the brackets, then expand to verify.
x2 + 9x + 20  =  (x + 4)(x + 5)
Check: (x + 4)(x + 5) = x2 + 5x + 4x + 20 = x2 + 9x + 20 ✓
Answer (x + 4)(x + 5)

6Together — factorise x2 + 8x + 15

Three small steps. After each prompt, discuss with the person next to you, then we'll fill in the box together.

💡 Why this works: when (x + 3)(x + 5) is expanded you get x2 + 5x + 3x + 15 = x2 + 8x + 15. The 3 and 5 multiply to give the constant (15) and add to give the coefficient of x (8). Find that pair and you've factorised.

8Practice — both signs positive ●○○Building

Every problem here has positive b and positive c — so both numbers in your brackets are positive. Type answers like (x+2)(x+5); the order of the brackets doesn't matter.

9Practice — negative middle term ●●○Stretch

c is still positive, but now b is negative. If the product is positive but the sum is negative, both numbers must be negative. Type answers like (x-2)(x-5).

10Challenge — negative constant ●●●Mastery

Now c is negative — which means one bracket is positive, the other negative. The larger absolute value takes the same sign as b. Type answers like (x+5)(x-3).
