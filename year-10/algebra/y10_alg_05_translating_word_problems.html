1Warm-up — from English to algebra

You already solve linear equations. The harder skill is building one from a worded problem. Today we learn the routine.

The situation

You go to a café and buy a pizza (price unknown) plus a $3 drink. Your total bill is $17.

The equation

Solve it: p = 14. The pizza costs $14.

The Modelling Mindset
  1. Define — pick a letter for the unknown. Write what it represents and the units.
  2. Translate — turn the words into an expression or equation, piece by piece.
  3. Solve — apply the algebra you already know.
  4. Check — does the answer make sense in the real situation?

Expression
Tap to flip
Numbers and variables joined by operations — no equals sign. It represents a quantity.
2k + 3 is an expression — the cost of a k-km taxi trip.
Equation
Tap to flip
Two expressions joined by =. It says one quantity equals another.
2k + 3 = 19 says the cost equals $19.

5Worked example — taxi fare

A taxi charges a $3 flag-fall plus $2 per kilometre. A trip costs $19 in total. How far was the trip?

1
Define the variable
Identify what's unknown. Give it a letter. Always state what it represents and the units.
Let k = the number of kilometres travelled.
2
Translate words → equation
Build the equation in pieces. Total cost = flag-fall + (rate per km × number of km).
3 flag-fall
+ 2k $2 for each of k km
= 19 total bill

2k + 3 = 19
3
Solve the equation
Use the routine from earlier lessons — undo addition, then undo multiplication.
2k + 3 = 19 subtract 3
2k = 16 divide by 2
k = 8
4
Check & answer in context
Substitute back into the original situation. Then write a sentence answering the original question.
Check: 8 km at $2/km = $16, plus $3 flag-fall = $19 ✓
In context: The trip was 8 km long.
Answer The trip was 8 km long.
The 4-step routine
  1. Define — let the unknown be a letter.
  2. Translate — words to equation, phrase by phrase.
  3. Solve — undo operations to isolate the variable.
  4. Check — substitute back; answer in a full sentence.
