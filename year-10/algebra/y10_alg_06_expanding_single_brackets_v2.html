1Warm-up — the garden bed

Before we touch any algebra, look at the picture.

You're planting a rectangular garden bed that's 3 m deep. The bed is split into two sections: a herb patch that's x metres wide and a flower patch that's 4 metres wide.

You can work out the total area two ways, and both must give the same answer:

  • Whole bed at once: width is (x + 4), depth is 3, so area = 3(x + 4).
  • Section by section: herbs = 3 × x = 3x; flowers = 3 × 4 = 12; total = 3x + 12.

Two expressions, one area: 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12

That single picture is the area model, and it's the whole lesson. Today we use it to expand any bracket — turning 3(x + 4) into 3x + 12 without needing to draw a garden each time.

2Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip and reveal the definition. You'll use all five of these words today.

Bracket
Tap to flip
A pair of grouping symbols ( ) that keep terms together so the multiplier outside applies to everything inside.
In 3(x + 4): the bracket holds x + 4 as one unit.
Coefficient
Tap to flip
The number multiplying a variable or a bracket — it sits in front.
In 3(x + 4): the coefficient is 3.
Term
Tap to flip
A single number, variable, or product, separated by + or − signs.
Inside x + 4: the terms are x and 4.
Distributive law
Tap to flip
A rule that says a multiplier outside a bracket must be shared out across every term inside.
a(b + c) = ab + ac
Expand
Tap to flip
To remove the brackets by applying the distributive law — multiplying every inside term by the outside coefficient.
Expand 5(x + 2) → 5x + 10.

6Together — expand 3(2x − 7)

Three small steps. After each prompt, talk to the person next to you, then we'll fill in the box together. Watch the sign on the 7 — it's a subtraction, so it stays a subtraction.

💡 Why this works: the 3 outside multiplies every term inside the bracket — the +2x and the −7. Each term keeps its sign. When you tackle the "You do" practice next, follow the same routine: multiply the outside by each inside term, then write the expansion.
