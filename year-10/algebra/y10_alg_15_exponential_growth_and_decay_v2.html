1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's algebra.

Suppose you put $1,000 into a savings account that pays 5% interest each year. After one year you have $1,050. The next year, 5% is taken of $1,050, not the original $1,000 — so you earn $52.50, not $50.

Each year the multiplier ×1.05 is applied again. After 10 years your balance has grown to about $1,629 — without you adding a cent. After 30 years: $4,322.

The same maths describes population growth, bacterial colonies, drug dosages clearing the body, and radioactive decay.

Today's big idea: a constant percentage change compounds into an exponential model. Each step multiplies by the same factor — so growth accelerates and decay never quite reaches zero. That's very different from linear change, where the same amount is added each step.

5Worked examples — growth and decay

Both follow the same routine: write the model, evaluate at the asked time, then read the doubling time or half-life off the graph.

Example A — population growth (+8% per year)

A town's population is 100,000 and grows by 8% each year. Find the population after 5 years, and the doubling time.

Each step multiplies by 1.08. The dashed marker shows where the population first reaches 200 (double the start).

1
Write the model
Identify the initial value and the per-step multiplier.
P0 = 100,000   growth factor = 1.08
P(n) = 100,000 × 1.08n
2
Evaluate at n = 5
Substitute and compute.
P(5) = 100,000 × 1.085
     = 100,000 × 1.4693…
     ≈ 146,933
3
Read off the doubling time
When does P reach 200,000? From the graph, the curve crosses 2 × P0 at about n = 9.
Check: 1.089 ≈ 1.999 ≈ 2 ✓
So the doubling time is roughly 9 years.
Answer P(5) ≈ 146,900  ·  doubling time ≈ 9 yrs

Example B — radioactive decay (halving each half-life)

A 160 g sample of a radioactive isotope has a half-life of 5 years. Find the mass after 12 years, and confirm the half-life from the graph.

Each 5-year step multiplies by ½. The curve approaches but never touches zero.

1
Write the model
Half-life of 5 years means the multiplier ½ applies once every 5 years.
M0 = 160 g   half-life T = 5 yrs
M(n) = 160 × (½)n/5
2
Evaluate at n = 12
Number of half-lives elapsed = 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4.
M(12) = 160 × (½)2.4
       = 160 × 0.1895
       ≈ 30.3 g
3
Confirm the half-life from the graph
The curve drops from 160 g to 80 g between n = 0 and n = 5 — exactly one half-life.
From 80 g → 40 g takes another 5 years (at n = 10). ✓
The model never quite reaches zero — that's the hallmark of decay.
Answer M(12) ≈ 30.3 g  ·  half-life = 5 yrs
