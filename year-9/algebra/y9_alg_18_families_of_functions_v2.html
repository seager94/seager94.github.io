1Why this matters

Real situations whose graphs are not straight lines and not parabolas.

A cup of coffee left on the bench cools toward room temperature. The graph isn't a line — it falls quickly at first, then more gently.

A bacteria population doubles every hour. The graph isn't a parabola — it climbs slowly at first, then explodes.

The brightness of a torch shining at a wall drops as you walk away. The shape is different again — and so is the rule.

Each of these situations has its own family of curves. Today's lesson is a quick tour of four new families and their signature shapes.

You already know two families: linear (y = mx + c) and quadratic (y = ax2 + ...). Today we add four more: reciprocal, square root, cubic, and exponential. The aim is shape-recognition, not deep maths.

Asymptote
Tap to flip
A line that a graph approaches but never touches.
The graph of y = 1 / x has the x-axis and the y-axis as asymptotes.
Point of inflection
Tap to flip

Exponential growth
Tap to flip

Tap to flip

4The four new families

Each card shows the rule, the graph, and a couple of key features. Read the features alongside the picture — those are the things that let you spot the family in a fraction of a second.

Reciprocal
y = 1x
Signature: two separate branches, one in the top-right and one in the bottom-left.
  • Has two asymptotes: the x-axis (y = 0) and the y-axis (x = 0).
  • x = 0 is undefined — the graph never crosses the y-axis.
  • As x gets bigger, y gets smaller; as x gets closer to 0, y gets enormous.
Square root
y = √x
Signature: a single curve that starts at the origin and climbs to the right, getting gentler as it goes.
  • The graph does not exist for negative x (you can't take the square root of a negative).
  • Passes through (0, 0), (1, 1), (4, 2), (9, 3).
  • Grows slower and slower — the opposite of the parabola.
Cubic
y = x3
Signature: an S-shape through the origin — steep, flat, then steep again.
  • Has a point of inflection at (0, 0) — where the curve changes from bending down to bending up.
  • Symmetric about the origin: a point on one branch matches an opposite point on the other.
  • For large positive x it climbs faster than a parabola.
Exponential
y = 2x
Signature: hugs the x-axis on the left, then shoots upward on the right.
  • Has a horizontal asymptote at y = 0 — the curve never touches the x-axis.
  • Always passes through (0, 1) — because 20 = 1.
  • y is always positive, no matter how negative x gets.
Headline result Four families, four signature shapes. Recognise the shape → name the family.

5Vary the base of an exponential

Three exponential curves on the same axes. Same family — different value of the base parameter. Discuss each prompt with your neighbour before we fill in the answer.

y = 2x
Doubles each step. Growth.
y = 3x
Triples each step. Steeper growth.
y = (12)x
Halves each step. Decay.
💡 The pattern: in y = bx, the parameter b is the base. When b > 1 the curve grows. When 0 < b < 1 the curve decays. Every curve in the family still passes through (0, 1) and still has the x-axis as an asymptote.
