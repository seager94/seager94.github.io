1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's algebra.

Your phone shows two pins on a map: your house and your friend's house. Built into every GPS app are two calculations the phone does in the background:

  • 📍 Halfway point — where should you meet?
  • 📏 Straight-line distance — how far apart are you?

The phone treats the map like a giant Cartesian plane: every location is just an (x, y) pair. Today we learn the two formulas that turn coordinates into a midpoint and a distance.

Two ideas, one plane:
  • Midpoint = average the two x's, average the two y's.
  • Distance = build a right triangle from the two points and use Pythagoras.

Coordinates
Tap to flip
An ordered pair (x, y) that locates a point: the x-coordinate first, the y-coordinate second.
The point (3, −5) means 3 right, 5 down from the origin.
Line interval
Tap to flip
A straight segment of a line with two definite endpoints. Sometimes called a line segment.
The interval from A(1, 2) to B(7, 10) has length 10.
Midpoint
Tap to flip
The point exactly halfway between two endpoints. It's the average of the coordinates.
Midpoint of (1, 2) and (7, 10) is (4, 6).
Distance
Tap to flip
The straight-line length between two points. Found with Pythagoras on the horizontal and vertical gaps.
Distance from (0, 0) to (3, 4) is √(9 + 16) = 5.

5Worked examples

Each example shows the diagram first, then the calculation. The diagram is the diagnostic — if a student can sketch the triangle, they can write the working.

Example 1 Find the midpoint and the distance between A(1, 2) and B(7, 10).
Midpoint — average the coordinates
M = (x1 + x22, y1 + y22)
= (1 + 72, 2 + 102)
= (82, 122) = (4, 6)
Distance — Pythagoras on the differences
Δx = 7 − 1 = 6
Δy = 10 − 2 = 8
d = √(Δx² + Δy²) = √(6² + 8²)
= √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10
Answers M = (4, 6)  ·  AB = 10 units
Example 2 Find the midpoint and the distance between A(−3, 4) and B(5, −2).
Midpoint — average (even with negatives)
M = (−3 + 52, 4 + (−2)2)
= (22, 22) = (1, 1)
Distance — squaring kills the sign
Δx = 5 − (−3) = 8
Δy = −2 − 4 = −6
d = √(8² + (−6)²) = √(64 + 36)
= √100 = 10
Answers M = (1, 1)  ·  AB = 10 units
⚠ Classic error — subtracting instead of averaging A very common mistake is to subtract the coordinates when finding a midpoint. Subtraction gives you a gap, not a halfway point.
✗ Wrong: M = (5 − (−3)2, −2 − 42) = (4, −3)
✓ Right: M = (−3 + 52, 4 + (−2)2) = (1, 1)
Memory hook: midpoint averages, distance differences. If you find yourself subtracting, you're doing distance — not midpoint.
Example 3 Find the midpoint and the distance from the origin O(0, 0) to the point P(3, 4).
Midpoint — origin makes the average easy
M = (0 + 32, 0 + 42)
= (32, 2) = (1.5, 2)
Distance — the classic 3-4-5 triangle
Δx = 3 − 0 = 3
Δy = 4 − 0 = 4
d2 = 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25
d = √25 = 5
Answers M = (1.5, 2)  ·  OP = 5 units
⚠ Classic error — forgetting the square root Pythagoras gives you d2, not d. Skipping the last step leaves the distance squared — a totally different number.
✗ Wrong: d = 3² + 4² = 25  (that's d2, not d)
✓ Right: d = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5
Sanity check: the distance should be less than Δx + Δy (the long way around the triangle, 3 + 4 = 7) and greater than either leg (3 or 4). 5 sits between — good. 25 fails this check by a mile.
