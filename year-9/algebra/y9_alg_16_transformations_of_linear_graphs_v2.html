1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's algebra.

An Adelaide taxi charges a $4 flag-fall the moment you climb in, then $2 for every kilometre. If x is the distance in km and y is the total fare:

y = 2x + 4

Change the per-km rate and the line gets steeper or shallower. Change the flag-fall and the whole line shifts up or down. Today we make those two effects precise — and that gives us the rule for every straight line.

We will do this by systematic experimentation: start with the simplest line y = x, change one parameter at a time, observe what the line does, and write down the pattern. By the end you'll be able to predict any line of the form y = mx + c before drawing it.

2Key vocabulary

Linear function
Tap to flip
A function whose graph is a straight line.
y = 3x + 2 is linear. y = x2 is not.
Gradient (m)
Tap to flip
The steepness of a line — rise over run. Sign tells you which way it slopes.
In y = 3x + 1: the gradient is 3.
y-intercept (c)
Tap to flip
Where the line crosses the y-axis. It's the y-value when x = 0.
In y = 3x + 1: the y-intercept is 1.
Parameter
Tap to flip
A number in an equation that controls a feature of the graph. Change the parameter, change the graph.
In y = mx + c: both m and c are parameters.
Slope-intercept form
Tap to flip
The standard form of a line: y = mx + c. Read off m (gradient) and c (y-intercept) directly.
5x + y = 2 rewrites as y = −5x + 2.

5Four experiments on y = x

In every figure: the dashed grey line is the base y = x. The solid coral line is what happens after one change.

1Make the coefficient of x larger

Replace x with 2x → new equation y = 2x

Observe: The coral line still goes through the origin, but it climbs twice as fast. For every 1 across, it goes up 2 instead of 1.

Generalise: doubling the coefficient of x doubles the gradient. Same y-intercept (still 0), but steeper.

2Make the coefficient of x smaller

Replace x with 12x → new equation y = 12x

Observe: Still through the origin, but the coral line is half as steep. For every 2 across, it goes up only 1.

Generalise: shrinking the coefficient (between 0 and 1) makes the line shallower. The smaller the coefficient, the flatter the line.

3Make the coefficient of x negative

Replace x with −x → new equation y = −x

Observe: Still through the origin, but the line now slopes down from left to right. It's the mirror image of y = x across the x-axis.

Generalise: a negative gradient means the line goes downhill as x increases. The size still tells you the steepness.

4Change both parameters at once

Double the coefficient and subtract 1 → new equation y = 2x − 1

Observe: The coral line is steeper (gradient 2) and it crosses the y-axis at −1 instead of 0.

Generalise: the two parameters act independently. The coefficient of x controls steepness; the constant on the end controls where the line crosses the y-axis.
