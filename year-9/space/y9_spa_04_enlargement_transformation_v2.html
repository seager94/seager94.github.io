1Why this matters

A real situation that is secretly today's mathematics.

A café's logo is printed on its menus at 4 cm × 6 cm. The owner orders a shopfront sign that is the logo enlarged by a scale factor of 25: every length is multiplied by 25, so the sign is 100 cm × 150 cm.

The sign maker charges $18 per metre for the metal edging. The menu logo's perimeter is 20 cm, so the sign's perimeter is 25 × 20 = 500 cm = 5 m, and the edging costs $90.

The corners are still right angles and the logo is not stretched or squashed. Only the size changed.

An enlargement multiplies every length by the same scale factor k, measured out from a fixed point called the centre of enlargement. Today you draw enlargements on a grid and use them to find missing lengths.

5Worked example — the 3-step method

Enlarge triangle ABC with centre O(0, 0) and scale factor k = 3. A(1, 1), B(3, 1), C(1, 2).

1
Measure each vertex from the centre
Count the moves from O to each vertex: right, then up. With the centre at the origin, these moves are just the coordinates.
O → A: right 1, up 1  ·  O → B: right 3, up 1  ·  O → C: right 1, up 2
2
Multiply every move by k = 3
The image is 3 times as far from the centre, in the same direction.
A′: right 3, up 3 → (3, 3)  ·  B′: right 9, up 3 → (9, 3)  ·  C′: right 3, up 6 → (3, 6)
3
Plot the image and check
Each ray from O passes through a vertex and its image. Check the sides: AB = 2 and A′B′ = 6; AC = 1 and A′C′ = 3. Both are 3 times longer. The right angle at A is still a right angle at A′.
Answer A′(3, 3), B′(9, 3), C′(3, 6). Sides × 3, angles unchanged: triangle A′B′C′ is similar to ABC.

★Spot the mistake

Riley enlarged point A with centre C(1, 1) and scale factor 3. Click the line where the mistake first appears.

Centre C(1, 1), scale factor 3, A(3, 2). Find the coordinates of A′.
1 Scale factor 3 means A′ is 3 times as far from the centre as A is.
2 So multiply the coordinates of A by 3: (3 × 3, 2 × 3).
3 A′ is at (9, 6).

✓ Right: that's where the mistake first appears.

Why: multiplying the coordinates measures from the origin, but the centre is C(1, 1). From C, A is right 2 and up 1. Multiply those moves by 3: right 6, up 3. Start at C and make those moves.

Now fix it: what are the coordinates of A′? Type (x, y).

★Hinge check 🎯 Pick one

A triangle is enlarged. Its 6 cm side becomes 15 cm in the image. Another side of the original is 4 cm. How long is that side in the image?

You added 9 cm. 6 → 15 is not '+9', it is '× 2.5'. An enlargement multiplies every length by the same scale factor.
Correct. k = 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5, so the image side is 4 × 2.5 = 10 cm.
You divided by the scale factor. The image is bigger, so multiply: 4 × 2.5, not 4 ÷ 2.5.
You added the scale factor. k = 2.5 is a multiplier: 4 × 2.5, not 4 + 2.5.
