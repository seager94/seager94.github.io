1Why this matters

A real situation that is secretly today's mathematics.

A café owner needs a wheelchair ramp up one step. The builder quotes the handrail by the metre along the sloping ramp. The council charges rent for every metre of footpath the ramp covers. The building code limits how high the ramp may rise for its length.

Three different prices, three different sides of the same right-angled triangle. Mix them up and the quote is wrong.

Measured from the ramp angle θ: the ramp surface is the hypotenuse, the rise is opposite θ, and the footpath run is adjacent to θ. Today you learn to name these three sides in any right-angled triangle.

5Worked example — the 3-step method

Name the hypotenuse, the opposite side and the adjacent side of triangle KLM for the reference angle θ at K.

1
Find the right angle, name the hypotenuse
The square marker is at L. The hypotenuse is the side that does not touch L.
Right angle at L  →  hypotenuse = KM = 13 cm (the longest side ✓)
2
Find θ, name the opposite side
θ is at K. The opposite side is the one that does not touch K.
θ at K  →  opposite = LM = 5 cm
3
The side left over is adjacent
KL touches θ at K and is not the hypotenuse, so it is adjacent. Check: it joins θ to the right angle.
Hypotenuse KM = 13 cm
Opposite LM = 5 cm
Adjacent KL = 12 cm
Answer Hyp = KM  ·  Opp = LM  ·  Adj = KL. If θ moved to M, opposite and adjacent would swap; KM stays the hypotenuse.

★Spot the mistake

Jordan labelled triangle PQR for θ at R. Click the line where the mistake first appears.

Right angle at Q, θ at R. Name the hypotenuse, adjacent and opposite sides.
1 The right angle is at Q, so the hypotenuse is PR = 15 cm.
2 θ is at R. PR touches R, so the adjacent side is PR = 15 cm.
3 The opposite side is the one left over: QR = 12 cm.

✓ Right: that's where the mistake first appears.

Why: PR does touch θ, but it was already named the hypotenuse in line 1. The hypotenuse is never called adjacent. The adjacent side touches θ and the right angle: QR. That leaves PQ as the opposite side.

Now fix line 2: how long is the adjacent side, in cm?

★Hinge check 🎯 Pick one

The triangle is tilted. The right angle is at B and θ is at C. Which side is adjacent to θ?

Not quite: AC is the hypotenuse. It touches θ, but it is across from the right angle at B, so it keeps the name hypotenuse. Adjacent is the other side touching θ.
Correct. BC touches θ at C and also touches the right angle at B. AC is the hypotenuse and AB is opposite θ.
That's the opposite side. AB does not touch C at all, so it is across from θ. Adjacent means it touches θ.
Turning the triangle changes nothing. The names come from where the right angle and θ are, not from which side is at the bottom. BC is adjacent however the triangle is drawn.
