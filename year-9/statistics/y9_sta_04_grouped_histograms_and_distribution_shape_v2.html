1Why this matters

A real health question that is secretly today's statistics.

A hospital is planning its budget for next year: how many stroke-care beds, which rehabilitation services, and for whom. A health manager claims: “Stroke is mainly a problem for older people.”

Two hundred patients are far too many to plot as dots. So the ages are grouped into class intervals (0–9, 10–19, …) and each group becomes one bar of a histogram. The bars touch because age is continuous.

Read the shape: the bars climb to a peak at 70–79 and a thin tail stretches left towards the young patients. That is negative skew.

The shape also tells us something we can't see directly: the few young patients drag the mean age down, so the mean sits below the median. By the end of today you'll be able to use a histogram like this to justify, verify or invalidate a claim, and to say which way the mean and median sit.

💡 The mean uses every value, so a few extreme values in the tail drag it towards the tail. The median is just the middle value, so it hardly moves. That's why the mean sits on the tail side of the median.

A hospital recorded the ages of 24 patients diagnosed with leukaemia (illustrative data). Build a grouped histogram, then describe the shape and test the claim “leukaemia only affects older people”.

Two peaks, at 0–9 and at 60–79: the distribution is bi-modal.

10bHinge check🎯 Pick one

A grouped histogram of house sale prices in a suburb has its tallest bars at the low end and a long tail stretching to the right. The median price is $650 000. Which value is most likely the mean?

Long tail to the right  ⟶  mean = ?
Not quite: you moved the mean towards the peak. The tallest bars are where most houses are, but the mean is dragged towards the tail. A few very expensive houses pull the mean up, above the median.
Only if the distribution were symmetric. Mean and median are about equal when both sides balance. Here a long right tail pulls the mean higher than the median.
Correct. Positive skew: a few very expensive houses in the right tail drag the mean up, so the mean is greater than the median of $650 000.
The shape alone tells you the direction. You can't find the exact mean, but a long right tail always pulls the mean above the median. Only one option is above $650 000.
