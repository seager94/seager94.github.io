1Why this matters

A real situation that is secretly today's maths.

Geologists say the Earth is about 4 600 000 000 years old. Chemists say one sugar molecule has a mass of 0.000 000 000 000 000 000 005 68 grams.

Money gets huge too: the Australian Government spends roughly $700 000 000 000 a year. Count those zeros wrong once and a budget is out by a factor of ten.

Scientific notation writes every number as a × 10n, with 1 ≤ a < 10. The age of the Earth becomes 4.6 × 109 and the sugar molecule becomes 5.68 × 10−21. The power of ten tells you the size at a glance.

Coefficient a
Tap to flip
The front number. It must be at least 1 and less than 10: exactly one non-zero digit before the point.
5.68 is fine; 56.8 and 0.568 are not.
Power of ten
Tap to flip
10n. A positive n means a big number; a negative n means a number between 0 and 1.
103 = 1000; 10−3 = 0.001
Standard (decimal) form
Tap to flip
The ordinary way of writing a number, with all its digits and zeros shown.
52 000 is the decimal form of 5.2 × 104.
SI prefix
Tap to flip
A word in front of a unit that multiplies it by a power of ten, from pico (10−12) to tera (1012).
1 kilometre = 103 m; 1 nanometre = 10−9 m

5Worked example — the 3-step method

The Earth is about 4 600 000 000 years old. Write this in scientific notation.

1
Find the coefficient a
Put the decimal point just after the first non-zero digit, so 1 ≤ a < 10. Drop the trailing zeros.
4 600 000 000 → a = 4.6
2
Count the places the point moves
The point starts at the end of the whole number and hops left to sit after the 4. Count every hop, not just the zeros.
9 hops left. The number is big (10 or more), so the power is positive: n = 9
3
Write a × 10n and check
Multiplying by 109 moves the point back 9 places right, so we return to the original number. Small numbers work the same way with a negative power: 0.000 72 = 7.2 × 10−4 (4 hops right).
4.6 × 109 = 4.6 × 1 000 000 000 = 4 600 000 000 ✓
Answer 4 600 000 000 = 4.6 × 109 years

★Hinge check 🎯 Pick one

Before the exit ticket: one question, four answers. Each wrong answer comes from a different mistake.

Which is 0.000 38 written in scientific notation?
Check the sign of the power. 3.8 × 104 = 38 000, a big number. 0.000 38 is less than 1, so its power of ten must be negative.
Correct. The point hops 4 places right to sit after the 3, giving a = 3.8. The number is less than 1, so the power is −4. Check: 3.8 ÷ 10 000 = 0.000 38.
Right value, wrong form. 38 × 10−5 does equal 0.000 38, but 38 is not between 1 and 10. Move one more place to get a = 3.8, and the power goes up by 1.
You counted the zeros, not the hops. There are 3 zeros after the point, but the point must hop past the 3 as well, which makes 4 hops.
