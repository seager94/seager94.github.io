1Why this matters

A real situation that's secretly today's maths.

A small online shop posts every order in a cardboard box 40 cm long, 30 cm wide and 20 cm high. Their supplier sells flat cardboard at $3.50 per square metre.

How much cardboard goes into one box, and what does it cost? A few cents per box doesn't sound like much. The shop sends about 1000 orders a month, though, so those cents add up.

Before it's folded, a box is a flat piece of cardboard called a net. The amount of cardboard is the area of that net, which is the box's surface area. Today you'll learn to unfold any right prism, add up its faces and find its surface area. We'll cost this exact box in Worked example 3.

6Worked example 2: a triangular prism

Find the surface area of a right triangular prism. Its cross-section is a right-angled triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm, and the prism is 10 cm long.

The prism. Both triangular ends are coral.
Its net: 2 triangles + 3 rectangles
triangular ends (×2) 3 × 10 4 × 10 5 × 10
1
Unfold it and list the faces
The cross-section has 3 sides, so the net has 2 triangles + 3 rectangles. Each rectangle is one side of the triangle × the length.
2 triangles  +  3 × 10,  4 × 10,  5 × 10
2
Find the area of each face
The legs 3 and 4 are at right angles, so they are the base and height of the triangle. Count the triangle twice, once for each end.
Triangles: 2 × 12 × 3 × 4 = 2 × 6 = 12 cm²
Rectangles: 3 × 10 + 4 × 10 + 5 × 10 = 30 + 40 + 50 = 120 cm²
3
Add the faces, then check with a different net
The rectangles add to (3 + 4 + 5) × 10, which is the perimeter of the triangle × the length. Below is a second net with the same five faces in a different arrangement, so its area must also be 132 cm².
SA = 12 + 120 = 132 cm²
Shortcut: 2 × 6 + (3 + 4 + 5) × 10 = 12 + 120 = 132 cm² ✓
A different net of the same prism: same 5 faces, same 132 cm²
Answer · and the general shortcut for any right prism SA = 132 cm²  ·  SA = 2 × (area of cross-section) + (perimeter of cross-section) × length
⚠ Watch out: the three classic mistakes
  • Counting only one triangular end. A prism always has two identical ends.
  • Using the perpendicular height as a rectangle's width. Each rectangle is a side of the cross-section × the length. In a tent, the sloping walls are slant side × length, not height × length.
  • Writing cubic units. Surface area covers the outside, so it is always in cm² or m², never cm³.

7Worked example 3: costing the shipping box

Back to the online shop: a box 40 cm × 30 cm × 20 cm, and cardboard at $3.50 per m². What does the cardboard for one box cost?

The box (all lengths in cm)
Its net: three matching pairs
1
Find one face from each pair
Same colour = same size. There are three different rectangles, and each appears twice.
top/bottom: 40 × 30 = 1200 cm²  ·  front/back: 40 × 20 = 800 cm²  ·  ends: 30 × 20 = 600 cm²
2
Double and add
This is SA = 2(lw + lh + wh).
SA = 2(1200 + 800 + 600) = 2 × 2600 = 5200 cm²
3
Convert to m², then cost it
1 m² is a square 100 cm by 100 cm, so 1 m² = 100 × 100 = 10 000 cm². Divide by 10 000, not 100.
5200 cm² ÷ 10 000 = 0.52 m²
Cost = 0.52 × $3.50 = $1.82 per box  (about $1820 for 1000 orders)
Answer 5200 cm² = 0.52 m² of cardboard, costing $1.82 per box

8Spot the mistake: the tent

A camping tent (floor included) is a triangular prism. The triangle has a base of 6 m, a perpendicular height of 4 m and sloping sides of 5 m. The tent is 8 m long. Jordan worked out the canvas needed, but made a mistake. Click the line where the mistake first appears.

The tent. The dashed coral line is the 4 m perpendicular height.
Its net: the floor between two sloping walls, with a triangle at each end
Jordan's working: surface area of the tent, floor included
1 Faces: 2 triangular ends + floor + 2 sloping walls
2 Ends: 2 × 12 × 6 × 4 = 24 m²
3 Floor: 6 × 8 = 48 m²
4 Sloping walls: 2 × 4 × 8 = 64 m²
5 SA = 24 + 48 + 64 = 136 m²

✓ Right: that's where the mistake first appears.

Why: each sloping wall is a rectangle whose width is the sloping side of the triangle (5 m), not its perpendicular height (4 m). The height runs through the inside of the tent, so it isn't an edge of any face. The height is only used for the triangle's area in line 2, and line 2 is correct.

Fix line 4: type the correct total area of the two sloping walls, in m² (number only).
