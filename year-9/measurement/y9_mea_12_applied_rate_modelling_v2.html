1Why this matters

A real situation that is secretly today's maths. Estimate first: commit a gut answer before anyone calculates.

A smartwatch reports your resting heart rate: about 70 beats per minute. A car ad says 0 to 100 km/h in 8 seconds. A bushfire report gives the fire front's rate of spread in metres per minute.

Every one of these is a rate: one quantity per unit of another. Know the rate and you can predict: how many beats in a day, how fast after 3 seconds, how long until the fire reaches the track.

Today you formulate a rate from data, use it to predict, and evaluate whether the model is sensible. Units do the heavy lifting: a rate in km/h is not a rate in m/s until you convert it.

5Worked example — the 3-step method

The model: acceleration is a rate, the change in velocity per second: a = (v − u) ÷ t, where u is the starting velocity and v the final velocity, both in m/s. A family car goes from rest to 90 km/h in 10 seconds. Find its acceleration, then use it to predict its speed after 6 seconds.

1
Formulate: get the units to match
Time is in seconds, so velocity must be in metres per second. 1 km/h = 1000 m ÷ 3600 s, so divide km/h by 3.6.
u = 0 m/s (from rest)
v = 90 ÷ 3.6 = 25 m/s
2
Solve: apply the rate
Change in velocity divided by time taken.
a = (25 − 0) ÷ 10 = 2.5 m/s²
The car gains 2.5 m/s every second.
After 6 s: v = 0 + 2.5 × 6 = 15 m/s
3
Interpret and evaluate
On the velocity–time graph the acceleration is the gradient: rise 25 over run 10. 15 m/s × 3.6 = 54 km/h after 6 s, which is believable. The model assumes a constant acceleration; a real car accelerates harder in low gears, so this is an average.
Check: the point (6, 15) lies on the line, because 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5 ✓
Model a = 2.5 m/s²  ·  v = 2.5t  ·  after 6 s → 15 m/s (54 km/h)

★Spot the mistake: acceleration

Before the exit ticket: a student has tried this question and made one mistake. Click the line where the mistake first appears.

A car speeds up from 36 km/h to 90 km/h in 6 seconds. Find its acceleration in m/s².
1 a = (v − u) ÷ t
2 a = (90 − 36) ÷ 6
3 a = 54 ÷ 6
4 a = 9 m/s²

✓ Right: that is where the mistake first appears.

Why: the speeds are still in km/h but the time is in seconds, so the units do not match. Convert first: 36 ÷ 3.6 = 10 m/s and 90 ÷ 3.6 = 25 m/s. An acceleration of 9 m/s² would be faster than a Formula One car off the line, which is a sign the model has gone wrong.
