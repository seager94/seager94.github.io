1Why this matters

A real situation that is secretly today's maths. Estimate first, then we calculate.

Australia has about 27 000 000 people, which is 2.7 × 107. Suppose each person spends about $4000 a year on groceries, which is 4 × 103 dollars.

Supermarkets, banks and the Treasury all need the national total. Nobody wants to count zeros on a calculator, so they multiply in scientific notation.

Today's big idea: to multiply (or divide) numbers in scientific notation, deal with the front numbers and the powers of ten separately. The exponent laws do the heavy lifting: multiply means add the indices, divide means subtract them.
💰 Total yearly grocery spending in Australia = (2.7 × 107 people) × (4 × 103 dollars). Before calculating: about how big is it? Pick a rung, then type your estimate of the power of ten, n, in $ ≈ something × 10n.

5Worked example — the 3-step method

A streaming service stores 3 × 104 videos. Each video takes 6 × 105 kilobytes. How many kilobytes is that altogether?

1
Estimate the order of magnitude first
Multiplying adds the powers: 104 × 105 = 109. The front numbers 3 × 6 are about 20, which is another power of ten or so.
Expect an answer around 1010
2
Group the front numbers and the powers
Multiplication can be done in any order, so regroup. Multiply the front numbers; add the indices.
(3 × 104) × (6 × 105) = (3 × 6) × (104 × 105) = 18 × 109
3
Adjust so 1 ≤ a < 10, then check
18 is too big for the front. 18 = 1.8 × 101, so the power goes up by 1. The answer matches the estimate of about 1010. Dividing works the same way: divide the front numbers and subtract the indices.
18 × 109 = 1.8 × 101 × 109 = 1.8 × 1010 ✓
Answer 1.8 × 1010 kilobytes

★Spot the mistake

Jordan divided two numbers in scientific notation and made one mistake. Read each line carefully and click the line where the mistake first appears.

(4 × 10−3) ÷ (8 × 105)
1 = (4 ÷ 8) × (10−3 ÷ 105)
2 = 0.5 × 10−8
3 = 5 × 10−7

✓ Right: that is where the mistake first appears.

Why: lines 1 and 2 are fine: 4 ÷ 8 = 0.5 and −3 − 5 = −8. To fix 0.5 Jordan made the front number 10 times bigger (0.5 → 5), so the power of ten must go down by 1 to keep the value the same. Jordan moved it up instead.

Now fix line 3: it should read 5 × 10n. Type n.
