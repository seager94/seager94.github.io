1Why this matters

A real situation that is secretly today's maths.

You post a photo to social media and the app squeezes it to fit. Sometimes faces look normal; sometimes they look stretched.

The same idea runs your first pay slip: work twice the hours, earn twice the pay. Swap A$500 for US dollars and A$1000 gets you twice as much.

These are all direct proportion: one quantity is a fixed multiple of the other, y = kx. Today you find k, use the model to predict, and spot when a situation breaks proportion, like a distorted photo.

Part B: build and test a model. A science class hangs weights on a spring. A load of 4 N stretches the spring by 6 cm. Hooke's law says the stretch is directly proportional to the load. Find the model, predict, then evaluate it.

1
Find k from one known pair
Direct proportion means e = kF, so k = e ÷ F.
k = 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5 cm per newton
2
Write the model and use it
Multiply to predict the stretch; divide to work backwards to the load.
e = 1.5F
Load 10 N: e = 1.5 × 10 = 15 cm
Stretch 12 cm: F = 12 ÷ 1.5 = 8 N
3
Interpret and evaluate the model
The graph is a straight line through (0, 0): no load, no stretch. But a real spring has an elastic limit. Pull it too far and it bends out of shape, so the model only holds for moderate loads.
Check: (10, 15) lies on the same line as (4, 6), because 15 ÷ 10 = 1.5 ✓
Model e = 1.5F  ·  10 N → 15 cm  ·  valid up to the elastic limit
