1Why this matters

A real situation that is secretly today's algebra.

A builder is tiling a square courtyard. The current design has a side of x metres. She extends it by 5 metres on two sides, making the new side (x + 5) metres.

She needs to order enough tiles to cover the whole new courtyard. That means she needs to know (x + 5)² square metres of tiles.

Without today's pattern, she multiplies out term by term every time. With it, she writes the answer in one line.

(x + 5)² = x² + 10x + 25

The x² is the original square. The 10x is the two rectangular border strips. The 25 is the new corner square.

Today you learn two patterns — perfect squares and the difference of two squares — that appear constantly in algebra, graphing, and physics.

5The fast patterns — and why (x+3)² ≠ x² + 9

See the geometry first, then the algebra. Two patterns do all the work.

Area model for (a+b)²
⚠ The central trap — name it, avoid it
(x+3)² ≠ x² + 9
The two ab rectangles in the area model are always there. You cannot skip to just squaring the two terms. The middle term 2ab is mandatory.
Pattern 1 — Perfect square
(a+b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
(a−b)² = a² − 2ab + b²
Pattern 2 — DOTS
(a+b)(a−b) = a² − b²
The middle terms cancel. No 2ab.
Worked example 1 — step by step

Expand: (x+5)²

1
Identify the pattern
This is (a+b)² form. Match up the parts: a = x, b = 5.
(x + 5)²  →  pattern: a² + 2ab + b²
a = x,   b = 5
2
Substitute into the pattern
Write out each of the three terms separately. Don't skip 2ab.
x² + 2 × x × 5 + 5²
= x² + 10x + 25
3
Write the final answer and check
Three terms: the squared first term, the middle term, and the squared last term. Check: if x = 1, (1+5)² = 36, and 1+10+25 = 36 ✓
Answer x² + 10x + 25
Further examples
Expand (x−4)²
Use (a−b)² with a=x, b=4
= x² − 2(x)(4) + 4²
= x² − 8x + 16
Subtract sign → middle term is negative
Expand (x+6)(x−6)
DOTS pattern: a=x, b=6
= x² − 6²
= x² − 36
Middle terms cancel — only two terms in answer
Expand (2x+3)²
a=2x, b=3
= (2x)² + 2(2x)(3) + 3²
= 4x² + 12x + 9
Square the coefficient too: (2x)² = 4x²
