1Number talk — a mental computation string

Four problems in a row. Mental arithmetic only — no calculator, no paper working. Each one builds on the previous, so take a moment to think before you type. The next problem unlocks after you answer (or skip).

1
25 × 4 = ?
💡 The anchor of the string. 25 × 4 = 100 — one of the most useful facts in mental maths. Keep that 100 in your head — it's coming back.
2
25 × 8 = ?
💡 Doubling. 8 is double 4, so 25 × 8 is double 100 = 200. No multiplication needed — just double the previous answer.
3
25 × 80 = ?
💡 Scaling by 10. 80 is 10 times 8, so 25 × 80 is 10 times 200 = 2000. Build on previous answers.
4
25 × 76 = ?
💡 Subtract to adjust. 76 = 80 − 4. So 25 × 76 = (25 × 80) − (25 × 4) = 2000 − 100 = 1900. You already knew both pieces — no big multiplication needed.

What the string was about

You never had to compute 25 × 76 directly. The whole string was building you toward a strategy: break the hard number into easier nearby numbers. 76 is close to 80, and the difference (4) is easy. This is called compensating, and it works for almost any multiplication.

Try it: 25 × 96 = ?   (Hint: 96 = 100 − 4.)
